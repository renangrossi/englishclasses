Wh- Questions (Who, What, Where, When, Why, How)

Ask for specific information, not just yes or no.

ObjectivesExplanationRulesExamplesCommon MistakesPracticeSummaryRelated

Introduction

Wh- questions ask for real information — a person, a thing, a place, a time, a reason, or a way of doing something — rather than a simple yes or no.

By the end of this lesson you can…

  • Choose the right question word for the information you need
  • Build wh- questions with correct word order
  • Answer wh- questions with the relevant detail

Explanation

Explanation

Each question word targets a different kind of answer: what for things/ideas, where for place, when for time, who for a person, why for a reason, and how for manner or way.

Word order follows the question word + auxiliary + subject + verb pattern: Where do you live? What is her name?

Grammar Rules

The Rules

  • What → things / ideas
  • Where → place
  • When → time
  • Who → person
  • Why → reason
  • How → manner / way

Examples

See It in Action

  • What is your name?
  • Where do you live?
  • When is your birthday?
  • Who is that man?
  • Why are you late?
  • How do you go to work?

Common Mistakes

Watch Out For

AvoidWhere you live?

Use insteadWhere do you live?

Wh- questions with ordinary verbs still need “do/does,” just like yes/no questions.

AvoidWhat means this word?

Use insteadWhat does this word mean?

The auxiliary “does” comes before the subject, and the main verb stays in its base form.

AvoidWho is coming?

Use insteadWho is coming?

(This one is already correct — “who” as the subject doesn't need do/does.)

Tip

If “who” is the subject of the question itself (Who called you?), you don't need do/does — that's a special case worth noticing.
