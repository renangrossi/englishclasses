B2

Wish / If Only

Express regret and unreal wishes about the present and the past.

ObjectivesRulesExamplesCommon MistakesPracticeSummaryTest Yourself

Introduction

"Wish" and "if only" (a stronger, more emotional version of wish) let you express that reality is different from what you want — in the present, or looking back at the past.

By the end of this lesson you can…

  • Use wish/if only + past simple for a present wish
  • Use wish/if only + past perfect for a past regret
  • Use wish/if only + would for a complaint about someone else's behavior

Grammar Rules

The Rules

a) Present wish — wish/if only + past simple

  • I wish I had more free time. (I don't have much now)
  • If only I spoke French. (I don't speak French)

b) Past regret — wish/if only + past perfect

  • I wish I had studied harder. (I didn't, and now I regret it)
  • If only I hadn't said that. (I did say it, and I regret it)

c) Complaint about someone's behavior — wish + would

  • I wish you would stop interrupting me. (a complaint/request for change, annoyance)

Not used for yourself: "I wish I would..." isn't standard — use "I wish I could" or "I wish I did/had."

Examples

See It in Action

  • I wish I had more free time.
  • If only I spoke French.
  • I wish I had studied harder.
  • If only I hadn't said that.
  • I wish you would stop interrupting me.
  • She wishes she could travel more.

Common Mistakes

Watch Out For

AvoidI wish I have more time.

Use insteadI wish I had more time.

A present wish needs the past simple form (had), not the present.

AvoidI wish I studied harder. (regretting a past choice)

Use insteadI wish I had studied harder.

A past regret needs the past perfect (had studied), not the simple past.

AvoidI wish I would speak French.

Use insteadI wish I spoke French. / I wish I could speak French.

"Wish + would" isn't used for your own ability/state — use the past simple or "could" instead.

AvoidI wish you stop interrupting me.

Use insteadI wish you would stop interrupting me.

A complaint about someone else's behavior needs "wish + would."
