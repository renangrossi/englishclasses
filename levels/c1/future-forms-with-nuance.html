Introduction

Grammar Rules

The Rules

a) Future-in-the-past — used to talk about something that was going to happen (or was planned/expected to happen) viewed from a point in the past. This is essential for storytelling.

  • was/were going to + bare infinitive: She was going to call me, but she forgot.
  • would + bare infinitive: He didn't know it yet, but this decision would change his life forever.
  • was/were about to + bare infinitive: I was about to leave when the phone rang.
  • was/were to + bare infinitive (formal/literary, often for events later described as fated or officially scheduled): They were to meet again only years later.

b) Complex future reference in narratives — combining future-in-the-past with other tenses to build a timeline within a story, often mixing:

  • past simple for the main narrative events,
  • past continuous for background,
  • future-in-the-past for what was still to come,
  • past perfect for what had already happened before the story's "now." By the time she boarded the plane, she had already decided she wasn't coming back. She was going to start a completely new life.

c) Nuance differences:

  • "was going to" often implies the plan changed or didn't happen (unfulfilled).
  • "would" (future-in-the-past) is common in narration for planned or inevitable future events, often without any sense of failure.
  • "was about to" emphasizes something on the very brink of happening, often interrupted.

Examples

See It in Action

  • She was going to study abroad, but the scholarship fell through.
  • Little did he know that this stranger would become his best friend.
  • I was about to knock when the door swung open.
  • The treaty, signed in 1919, was to shape the next century of European politics.
  • He promised he would call as soon as he landed.
  • We were going to have a picnic, but it started raining.
  • She had no idea that the interview would change her career forever.
  • Just as I was about to give up, I found the missing file.
  • The young officer, then unknown, was later to become a national hero.
  • He said he would finish the project by Friday.
  • They were going to sell the house, but they changed their minds at the last moment.
  • As she packed her bags, she realized she was about to leave her old life behind.

Common Mistakes

Watch Out For

AvoidShe was going to called me later that day.

Use insteadShe was going to call me later that day.

"Was going to" is always followed by the bare infinitive, not the past form.

AvoidHe didn't know that this decision will change everything.

Use insteadHe didn't know that this decision would change everything.

Inside a past narrative, future-in-the-past requires "would," not "will."

AvoidI was about to leaving when she called.

Use insteadI was about to leave when she called.

"Was about to" takes the bare infinitive, not the gerund.

AvoidThey was going to travel next month.

Use insteadThey were going to travel next month.

Subject-verb agreement — "they" needs "were," not "was."

AvoidThe treaty was signing to shape the future.

Use insteadThe treaty was to shape the future.

"Was/were to + infinitive" (formal, meaning "was destined/scheduled to") doesn't take a gerund.
