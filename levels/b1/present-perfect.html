B1

Present Perfect

Connect the past to now — experiences, changes, and unfinished time.

ObjectivesRulesExamplesCommon MistakesPracticeSummary

Introduction

The present perfect is one of the trickiest tenses for Portuguese speakers, because Portuguese doesn't have a direct equivalent. It connects something from the past to the present moment.

By the end of this lesson you can…

  • Form the present perfect (have/has + past participle)
  • Use it for life experiences, without saying exactly when
  • Use it for a recent action with a present result
  • Use already, yet, just, never, and ever correctly

Grammar Rules

The Rules

a) Form

have/has + past participle. I/you/we/they have finished. He/she/it has finished.

b) Life experience (no specific time)

  • She has visited Paris three times. (at some point in her life, we don't say exactly when)
  • I have never eaten sushi.

c) Recent action, present result

  • I have lost my keys. (result: I don't have them now)
  • They have just arrived. (very recently, relevant now)

d) Key signal words

already (before now, often in affirmatives), yet (up to now, in negatives/questions), just (very recently), never/ever (at any point in life)

Examples

See It in Action

  • I have finished my homework.
  • She has visited Paris three times.
  • They have already eaten.
  • Have you finished yet?
  • He has just arrived.
  • I have never been to Japan.

Common Mistakes

Watch Out For

AvoidI have visited Paris last year.

Use insteadI visited Paris last year.

"Last year" is a finished, specific time — that needs the simple past, not the present perfect.

AvoidShe has went to the store.

Use insteadShe has gone to the store.

The present perfect needs the past participle (gone), not the simple past form (went).

AvoidI have finish my homework.

Use insteadI have finished my homework.

The main verb needs the past participle form after "have."

AvoidHave you finished already?

Use insteadHave you finished yet?

"Yet" is used in questions and negatives; "already" is more typical in affirmative statements.

Summary

Review

  • Have/has + past participle.
  • Use it for life experiences (no specific time) and recent actions with a present result.
  • Never use it with a specific past time word (yesterday, last year) — use the simple past instead.
  • Already/just fit affirmatives; yet fits questions and negatives; never/ever describe life experience.
