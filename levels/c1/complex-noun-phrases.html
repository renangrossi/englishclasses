Grammar Rules

The Rules

a) Multiple modifiers — stacking adjectives, participles, and prepositional phrases before and after a noun.

a rapidly growing, community-led initiative to reduce plastic waste

b) Nominalization — turning a verb or adjective into a noun (often with -tion, -ment, -ness, -ity) to make writing more compact, formal and objective. This is very common in academic and journalistic style.

They decided to reduce costs. → Their decision to reduce costs...

The company failed unexpectedly. → The company's unexpected failure...

c) Expanded relative clauses — relative clauses with extra detail, sometimes non-defining (set off by commas) and stacked with other modifiers.

The report, which was released last Friday and which contradicts earlier findings, sparked controversy.

d) Reduced relative clauses — relative clauses shortened by dropping the relative pronoun and auxiliary, using a participle instead.

The man who is standing by the door → The man standing by the door

The results that were published last year → The results published last year

Reduced relative clauses use the -ing form for active meaning (the man standing...) and the past participle for passive meaning (the results published...). They make sentences more compact and are especially common in written and journalistic English.

Examples

See It in Action

  • The recently renovated, award-winning museum attracts thousands of visitors a year.
  • Her decision to resign surprised everyone on the board.
  • The rapid deterioration of the local ecosystem alarmed environmentalists.
  • The proposal, which had been debated for months, was finally rejected.
  • The scientist leading the research presented her findings yesterday.
  • Documents found at the scene confirmed the timeline of events.
  • The government's growing concern about inflation shaped its policy.
  • A heavily criticized, poorly funded healthcare system struggled to cope.
  • The book, written during the author's exile, became an instant classic.
  • Employees affected by the layoffs will receive severance packages.
  • The committee's reluctance to act delayed the reforms significantly.
  • The bridge connecting the two islands took a decade to build.

Common Mistakes

Watch Out For

AvoidThe man who standing by the door is my uncle.

Use insteadThe man standing by the door is my uncle.

A reduced relative clause drops both the relative pronoun ("who") and the auxiliary ("is") — keep only the participle.

AvoidThe report wrote last year was inaccurate.

Use insteadThe report written last year was inaccurate.

For a passive meaning, use the past participle (written), not the past simple (wrote).

AvoidHis decide to leave shocked the team.

Use insteadHis decision to leave shocked the team.

Nominalization requires the noun form of the verb ("decision"), not the bare verb.

AvoidThe company success unexpected surprised analysts.

Use insteadThe company's unexpected success surprised analysts.

Word order matters — possessive + adjective + noun, not noun + adjective in that order.

AvoidThe employees affecting by the cuts were notified.

Use insteadThe employees affected by the cuts were notified.

"Affected" (past participle, passive meaning: they were affected) is needed, not "affecting" (active meaning).
