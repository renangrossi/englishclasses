Rain and Snow

Four seasons in Chicago, and what you wear for each one.

Play the audio and follow along, then read it again on your own. Tap or hover over a highlighted word to see what it means.

Chicago has four seasons, and they are all very different.

Winter is long and cold. There is a lot of snow, and the wind from the lake is bad. I wear a big coat, a hat, and boots. Some mornings the street is white and quiet.

Spring is wet. It rains a lot, so I take an umbrella to work. The park near our apartment is green again, and the days are longer.

Summer is hot. People sit outside, the kids play in the water, and nobody wants to cook. Jack and I eat on the step in front of the building.

Fall is my favorite. It is not hot and it is not cold. The trees are red and yellow for three weeks, and then the wind takes the leaves away.

Then it is cold again. In Chicago, you always have a coat near the door.

Key Vocabulary

  • season one of the four parts of the year
  • snow the soft white water that falls when it is very cold
  • wind air that moves
  • to wear to have clothes on your body
  • coat the warm clothes you put on to go outside
  • boots strong shoes that cover your foot and leg
  • wet covered with water
  • to rain when water falls from the sky
  • umbrella the thing you hold over your head in the rain
  • outside not in a building
  • favorite the one you like best
  • leaves the green parts of a tree
