PRE-A1

Numbers, Time & Dates

Count from 0 to 100, tell the time, and say the days and months.

ObjectivesRulesExamplesCommon MistakesPracticeSummary

Introduction

Numbers, time and dates are some of the most useful words you'll ever learn — you'll use them every single day.

By the end of this lesson you can…

  • Count from 0 to 20, then by tens to 100
  • Say and ask the time
  • Name the seven days of the week and twelve months

Grammar Rules

The Rules

Numbers 0–20

zero, one, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten, eleven, twelve, thirteen, fourteen, fifteen, sixteen, seventeen, eighteen, nineteen, twenty

Tens, up to 100

twenty, thirty, forty, fifty, sixty, seventy, eighty, ninety, one hundred

For numbers in between, add the second number: twenty-one, thirty-five, forty-two.

Telling the time

  • What time is it? — It's three o'clock. (3:00)
  • It's half past three. (3:30) / It's three thirty.
  • It's a quarter past three. (3:15) / It's a quarter to four. (3:45)

Days and months

Days: Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday, Saturday, Sunday

Months: January, February, March, April, May, June, July, August, September, October, November, December

Examples

See It in Action

  • I have two brothers and one sister.
  • What time is it? — It's ten o'clock.
  • My birthday is in July.
  • Today is Monday.
  • The meeting is at half past two.
  • There are seven days in a week and twelve months in a year.

Common Mistakes

Watch Out For

AvoidI have twenty and five years.

Use insteadI am twenty-five years old.

Ages use "to be," and compound numbers use a hyphen with no "and": twenty-five, not twenty and five.

AvoidToday is monday.

Use insteadToday is Monday.

Days of the week are always capitalized in English.

AvoidIt's three and half.

Use insteadIt's half past three. / It's three thirty.

"Half past" is the standard way to say 30 minutes past the hour.

Summary

Review

  • Numbers 0–20 have their own words; from 20 on, combine the tens word with the ones word (twenty-five).
  • What time is it? → It's [hour] o'clock / half past [hour] / a quarter past/to [hour].
  • Days and months are always capitalized in English.
