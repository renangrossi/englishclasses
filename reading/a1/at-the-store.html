At the Store

Emma needs milk, bread and coffee — and the line is long.

Play the audio and follow along, then read it again on your own. Tap or hover over a highlighted word to see what it means.

After work I go to the store. We need milk, bread, and coffee for the shop at home.

The store is near my apartment, so I walk. It is cheap, and it is open until ten.

I take a basket. The milk is two dollars. The bread is three. The coffee is expensive — nine dollars — but it is good coffee, and Jack drinks a lot of it.

I look for apples too, but today they are not fresh. I don't buy them.

At the front there is a long line. I wait for ten minutes. A woman in front of me has two kids, and they are tired and loud.

“Fourteen dollars,” the man says. I pay with a card. He gives me a bag.

“Have a good night,” he says.

Key Vocabulary

  • cheap not costing a lot of money
  • basket the thing you carry your shopping in
  • to look for to try to find something
  • fresh new and good to eat, not old
  • line people standing one behind the other and waiting — in American English
  • to wait to stay in a place until something happens
  • in front of before somebody, nearer the front
  • to pay to give money for something
  • card the small piece of plastic you pay with
  • bag the thing you put your shopping in to carry it
  • Have a good night a friendly way to say goodbye in the evening
