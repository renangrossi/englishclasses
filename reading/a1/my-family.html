My Family

Emma's parents, her older sister, her younger brother — and one week in December.

Play the audio and follow along, then read it again on your own. Tap or hover over a highlighted word to see what it means.

My family is not big. I have one brother and one sister.

My parents live in Ohio, in the same house for thirty years. My father, Dan, is sixty-two. He works in a store. My mother, Laura, is sixty, and she is a nurse. She works at night.

My sister, Kate, is older than me. She is thirty-five, and she has two kids, a son and a daughter. Her husband is a teacher. They live near my parents, so my mother sees the kids every week.

My brother, Nick, is younger. He is twenty-eight and he is not married. He lives in Denver and works for a small company. We talk on the phone every Sunday.

I live far from all of them. But every December we go to Ohio, and for one week the house is full and loud and very warm.

Key Vocabulary

  • parents your mother and your father — careful: not all your relatives
  • store a place where you buy things
  • nurse a person who helps sick people at a hospital
  • older with more years
  • kids children, in everyday English
  • son somebody's male child
  • daughter somebody's female child
  • younger with fewer years
  • married having a husband or a wife
  • far from a long way away from
  • full with no empty space
  • loud with a lot of noise
