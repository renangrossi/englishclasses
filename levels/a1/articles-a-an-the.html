Articles: A, An, The

Know when to say a, an, the — or nothing at all.

ObjectivesExplanationRulesExamplesCommon MistakesPracticeSummaryTest YourselfRelated

Introduction

Articles are small words with a big job: they tell the listener whether you mean any example of something, or one specific thing they already know about.

By the end of this lesson you can…

  • Choose between “a” and “an” based on sound
  • Use “the” for something specific or already mentioned
  • Recognize when no article is needed

Explanation

Explanation

A / An introduce something for the first time, or something non-specific — use a before a consonant sound and an before a vowel sound (listen to the sound, not just the letter: an hour, but a university).

The is used for something specific: it was already mentioned, or both speakers know exactly which one is meant. I saw a dog. The dog was brown. — the second mention uses “the” because we now know which dog.

Grammar Rules

The Rules

  • A / An → first mention, singular, non-specific
  • A before a consonant sound; An before a vowel sound
  • The → specific thing, already mentioned, or unique (the sun, the internet)
  • No article before plural or uncountable nouns used in general (I like coffee, not I like the coffee)
A, an, or the?
SituationArticle
First mention, singulara / an
Before a consonant sounda book, a university
Before a vowel soundan apple, an hour
Already mentioned / specificthe
General statement, plural/uncountable(no article)

Examples

See It in Action

  • I saw a dog in the park. The dog was brown.
  • She is an engineer.
  • We waited for an hour.
  • The sun is bright today.
  • I like coffee in the morning.

Common Mistakes

Watch Out For

AvoidI saw an dog.

Use insteadI saw a dog.

“Dog” starts with a consonant sound, so it needs “a,” not “an.”

AvoidShe is a honest person.

Use insteadShe is an honest person.

“Honest” starts with a vowel sound (the h is silent), so it needs “an.”

AvoidI like the coffee.

Use insteadI like coffee.

A general statement about coffee (not one specific cup) uses no article.

Tip

Listen for the sound, not the spelling: “an hour” (silent h) but “a university” (sounds like “you-”).
