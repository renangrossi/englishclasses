Portal Presentation & Defense

This page is only for Manoelito. It helps you present the Core Medical Group Patient Portal to the military board, and answer their questions with short, clear, confident English.

Before You Start

How to Use This Page

Every exercise on this page is about your real project: the Patient Portal. Do each exercise, then click Submit to check your answer and see a short explanation — this is your answer key, built into every exercise. Work through the page in order: Vocabulary → Grammar → Presentation Scripts → Defense Practice → Role-Play → Checklist.

Remember: your Portal is a prototype. It is completely normal — and honest — to say so. You do not need to know every answer today. You only need to answer clearly, or say that you will check and confirm.

Warm-Up

Key Vocabulary

These are the words you will use again and again. Read them first.

Portal

The website where the patient fills out the form and sees the result. Example: "The Portal shows every step."

Verify / Identity

To check that a person really is who they say they are. Example: "ID.me verifies military status."

Form

A page with questions the patient must answer. Example: "The patient fills out the form."

Consent

Saying "yes, I agree" — here, with a checkbox. Example: "Consent is always the patient's choice."

Doctor / Physician

The person who reviews the form and writes the prescription. Example: "A doctor checks every request."

Prescription

A document from the doctor that allows the next step. Example: "The prescription goes to Open Loop."

Test Kit

A small box sent to the patient's home for the health test. Example: "Open Loop mails the test kit."

Result

The answer from the test. Example: "The patient sees the result in the Portal."

Journey

All the steps from start to finish, in order. Example: "You can follow your journey on one page."

Prototype

An early, test version of a product — not the final version. Example: "This is a prototype. We are still testing it."

Salesforce

The system that connects the Portal and the doctor automatically. Example: "Salesforce sends the form to the doctor."

Open Loop

The company that ships the test kit and returns the result. Example: "Open Loop mails the kit to the patient's home."

Military Status

Proof that a person is really in the military. Example: "ID.me checks military status first."

Checkbox

A box on a form that you click to say "yes." Example: "The patient clicks a checkbox to give consent."

Milestone

An important point in the journey, like "Prescription" or "Results." Example: "Each milestone shows your progress."

Guidance

Information and instructions that help the patient understand the process. Example: "Read the guidance before you submit."

Submit

To send your form to the Portal. Example: "The patient fills out the form and clicks Submit."

Review

To look at something carefully before deciding. Example: "The doctor reviews every form."

Deliver / Ship

To send something to the patient's home. Example: "Open Loop ships the test kit."

Confirm

To say that something is correct or finished. Example: "I will check and confirm."

Controlled Practice

Grammar for Your Presentation

Every sentence below is about your Portal. The exercises are ordered by grammar topic. Complete each one, then click Submit.

1. Match the Word to the Meaning

Warm up with the vocabulary above.

2. Pronouns

Talk about the patient, the doctor, and the Portal correctly.

3. Present Continuous: What Is Happening Now

Use present continuous to describe the demo, or work happening right now.

4. Simple Past: How the Project Started

Use simple past to talk about the history of the Portal.

5. Future Forms: Plans and Promises

Choose the correct future form. Use "will" for a promise, and "going to" for a plan.

6. Simple Present: General Facts

Use simple present for things that are always true about the Portal.

7. Can / Can't / Could: Capabilities and Limits

Use these words to explain what the Portal can and cannot do.

8. Because / So / But: Giving Reasons

Guided Speaking

Presentation Scripts

Short model paragraphs. Read them, practice them out loud, then change a few words to make them yours.

1. Opening

"Good morning. My name is Manoelito. I represent Core Medical Group. Today, I will present our new Patient Portal."

2. The Six Steps

"Let me explain the process. First, the patient verifies their identity with ID.me. Second, the patient fills out a form on the Portal. Third, a doctor reviews the form and writes a prescription. Fourth, we send a test kit to the patient's home. Fifth, the patient takes the test and sends the sample back. Finally, the patient sees the result in the Portal."

3. Why This Helps

"This Portal will help military members in a simple way. Patients can do the first steps from home. They always know the next step. A doctor checks every request. We are still testing the system, and we will keep making it better."

4. Closing

"Thank you very much for your time today. This is a prototype, and I am ready for your questions."

Speaking Practice

Role-Play: Teacher as Board Member

Instructions: The teacher reads the "Board" lines. Manoelito reads the "You" lines. Read the two examples first, then role-play using the extra questions below — without reading, if possible.

Example 1: Explaining the Process

Board: "Good morning. Please explain your Patient Portal."
You: "Good morning. Our Portal has six steps. First, we verify identity with ID.me."
Board: "Why do you check identity first?"
You: "Because security is very important for military members."
Board: "What happens after that?"
You: "The patient fills out a form, and a doctor reviews it."

Example 2: A Hard Question

Board: "Is this data 100% safe?"
You: "That's a very important question. I don't have every detail today, but I will check with my team and confirm."
Board: "When can you send that answer?"
You: "I will send it to you this week, by email."

Your Turn: More Questions to Practice

The teacher asks these questions in a different order. Answer with short, honest sentences. It is always OK to say: "That's a good question. I will check and confirm."

  • What is Open Loop?
  • What does "consent" mean here?
  • How long is the form valid?
  • Can a member stop the process after starting it?
  • Is this only for active duty members?
  • What should you do if you don't understand a question?
  • Who built this Portal?
  • Is there a phone number for help?
  • What happens to the test kit if the patient moves?
  • Why is the guidance document so long?

Final Check

Presentation Checklist

  • I can say the six steps without reading.
  • I know the vocabulary: Portal, verify, consent, prescription, journey.
  • I practiced the opening and the closing out loud.
  • I practiced saying "I will check and confirm" for hard questions.
  • I know this is a prototype, and it's OK to say so.
  • I did one full role-play with my teacher.

Confidence Tips

Before You Speak

  • Speak slowly. Slow and clear is better than fast.
  • Look at the board members when you speak.
  • Breathe before you answer a hard question.
  • It's OK to say: "That's a good question. I will check."
  • Confidence is not the same as perfect English.
  • Practice today, tomorrow, and the day before the presentation.
