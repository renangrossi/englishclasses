Flying to the United States

Booking a flight, reconfirming it, and getting through passport control — with the words you actually need.

Play the audio and follow along, then read it again on your own. Tap or hover over a highlighted word to see what it means.

Three short conversations you are likely to have on the way to the United States. The traveler's lines are filled in here as models — read them, then cover them and try your own.

1. Booking a flight by phone

Clerk: Northwind Airlines, good morning. May I help you?

You: Good morning. Do you have any flights to Chicago next Tuesday afternoon?

Clerk: One moment, please… Yes. There's a flight at 4:45 p.m. and one at 6:00 p.m.

You: How much is the 4:45, and how long does it take?

Clerk: Economy, business or first class?

You: Economy, please.

Clerk: That would be three hundred and forty-six dollars, round trip.

You: All right. I'd like to book the 4:45 flight, please.

Clerk: Certainly. Could I have your name, please?

You: It's Karen Jones. That's J-O-N-E-S.

Clerk: And how would you like to pay, Ms. Jones?

You: By credit card. Can I give you the number now?

Clerk: Yes, but please note you'll need to check in at least two hours before departure.

You: Two hours. Got it, thank you.

Clerk: You're booked, Ms. Jones. The flight leaves at 4:45 p.m. and arrives in Chicago at 9:25 p.m. local time. The flight number is NWA 476.

You: Could you repeat the flight number, please?

2. Reconfirming a reservation

Clerk: Northwind Airlines. Can I help you?

You: Yes — I'd like to reconfirm my flight, please.

Clerk: May I have your name and flight number?

You: David Adams, flight NWA 118.

Clerk: When are you leaving?

You: On the fourteenth, next Friday.

Clerk: And your destination?

You: Miami.

Clerk: Hold the line, please… All right, your seat is confirmed, Mr. Adams. You'll arrive in Miami at 4:00 p.m. local time.

You: Thank you. Is there anything else I need to do?

Clerk: Just check in at least an hour before departure.

3. At passport control

Officer: Good evening. Where have you come from?

You: From São Paulo, on flight NWA 476.

Officer: May I see your passport, please?

You: Here you are.

Officer: What's the purpose of your visit — business or pleasure?

You: Business. I'm attending a conference in Chicago.

Officer: How long are you going to stay in the United States?

You: Ten days. I'm flying home on the twenty-fourth.

Officer: What do you do for a living?

You: I'm a software engineer.

Officer: Do you have a return ticket?

You: Yes, I do. Would you like to see it?

Officer: That's fine. Thank you — enjoy your trip.

You: Thank you very much.

Key Vocabulary

  • round trip there and back (American English; British: return)
  • to book / a reservation to arrange a seat or room in advance
  • to reconfirm to check again that your booking is still valid
  • to check in to register at the airport before a flight, or at a hotel on arrival
  • departure / arrival leaving / getting there
  • the purpose of your visit the official phrase for why you are traveling
  • business or pleasure the standard choice officers offer: for work, or for a vacation
  • Hold the line, please. wait on the telephone
  • Here you are. what you say as you hand something to someone
  • What do you do for a living? a natural way to ask someone's occupation

Speaking & Writing

Discussion

There are no right answers here — use these to practice speaking, or write a short answer to each.

  • Role-play dialogue 1 with a partner: one of you is the clerk, the other books a flight to a city of your choice.
  • Have you ever been asked questions at passport control? What did they ask, and how did you answer?
  • Officers ask short, direct questions. Why do you think short, complete answers work better than long explanations?
  • Write a telephone dialogue (about 10 lines) in which you change a booking you already made.
