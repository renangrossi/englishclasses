Past or Perfect?

Finished time vs. time connected to now.

46

Simple past

Finished · specific time

  • I visited Lima last year.
  • They bought a house in 2021.
  • He lost his wallet yesterday.
  • We met two weeks ago.
yesterday · last week · in 2020 · ___ ago · when I was a child

Present perfect

No date · still open

  • I’ve visited Lima twice.
  • They’ve just bought a house.
  • He’s lost his wallet again!
  • We’ve met this week.
already · yet · ever · never · so far · this week / year (not over)

For + a period · Since + a starting point

for three years · for two weeks · for agesI’ve worked here for three years.
since 2020 · since Monday · since I was a kidI’ve worked here since 2022.

Mixed conversation

MayaHow long have you known Tom?LeoI’ve known him since school. We met in 2010.MayaHave you ever worked together?LeoYes — we started a small business last year.

The conversation opens in the present perfect and switches to the simple past once a specific time appears.

Repeated actions and actions still in progress.

English Classes · B1 · Intermediate

Result or Activity?

Present perfect vs. present perfect continuous.

48

Present perfect

The result · how many

have/has+participle
  • I’ve read three books this month.
  • She’s painted the bedroom. Look!
  • We’ve walked 12 km today.
  • He’s written the report — it’s on your desk.

Present perfect continuous

The activity · how long

have/has been+-ing
  • I’ve been reading this book for weeks.
  • She’s been painting — there’s paint on her hands.
  • We’ve been walking since 8am.
  • He’s been writing the report all morning.

Ask yourself

Am I focusing on what is done or how many?→ present perfect
Am I focusing on the process or how long?→ present perfect continuous
Is the verb a state (know, own, love)?→ present perfect only

Used To · Be Used To

Past habits, things that feel normal — and when vs. while.

49

Past habit

USED TO + verb

true before · not true now
  • I used to live by the sea.
  • They didn’t use to come here.
  • Did you use to play the piano?

WAS / WERE USED TO + -ing

it felt normal then
  • I was used to working nights back then.
  • They were used to traveling light.
  • She wasn’t used to the noise at first.

Present habit

USUALLY + verb

a routine
  • He usually visits his parents on Sundays.
  • We usually travel in September.

AM / IS / ARE USED TO + noun / -ing

it’s normal for me
  • I’m used to speaking in public.
  • She is used to spicy food.
  • Are you used to driving on the left?

When vs. while

  • When I got home, it started to snow. short action
  • I was cooking while he was setting the table. background
get used to = the process of becoming familiar: You’ll get used to the cold.

Past Perfect

The past of the past — which of two past events came first.

50

Affirmative

the same for every subject

  • The detective had interviewed every witness before the trial.

had not (hadn’t)
  • She hadn’t noticed the mistake until you pointed it out.

Modal Verbs

Probability, advice and obligation — no to, no -s.

51

Probability

100%
WILL
MAY
MIGHT · MAYBE
0%
  • It’ll be sunny tomorrow.
  • We may go — I’m not sure yet.
  • It might not be that easy…
  • Maybe we’ll meet again someday.

SHOULD

opinion · advice · expectation
  • You should see a doctor.
  • We shouldn’t waste more time.
  • The package should arrive today.

MUST

obligation · strong opinion · deduction
  • Passengers must wear a seatbelt.
  • You mustn’t touch the paintings.
  • She’s been working all day — she must be tired.

Would

Conditions, past situations and polite requests.

52

Polite requests

  • Would you send me the file, please?
  • Would you mind closing the door?
  • I’d like a table for two.

Conditions

  • I’d eat more, but I’m full.
  • If he weren’t driving, he’d have a glass of wine.
  • What would you do with a million dollars?

Past habits (actions)

  • As a kid, I would spend summers at the farm.
  • Every night, Dad would read us a story.
  • The old printer wouldn’t work properly.

Would or used to?

Repeated actionsWe would / used to play in the street.✓ both
States (be, have, know, like)I used to have long hair.✗ not would
wouldn’t can also mean “refused to”: The door wouldn’t open.

Question Tags

A little question at the end — to confirm.

53
POSITIVE,NEGATIVE ?
  • We’re still meeting later, aren’t we?
  • This is your bike, isn’t it?
  • You like spicy food, don’t you?
  • They’ll call us, won’t they?
  • She could have tried harder, couldn’t she?
NEGATIVE,POSITIVE ?
  • It can’t be serious, can it?
  • He wasn’t driving, was he?
  • Tom won’t come, will he?
  • You didn’t tell her, did you?
  • She wouldn’t lie, would she?

How it works

  • Repeat the same helper verb: is → isn’t, can → can’t, will → won’t, did → didn’t.
  • No helper verb? Use do / does / did: She works here, doesn’t she?
  • Use a pronoun in the tag: Tom is late, isn’t he?
Special cases: I’m right, aren’t I? · Let’s go, shall we?

Second Conditional

Hypothetical situations — imaginary now or in the future.

54

Second conditional — imaginary present / future

IF+simple past,would · could · might + verb
  • If I won the lottery, I’d travel for a year.
  • If she lived closer, we could meet more often.
  • Our team might win if we trained harder.
  • If I were you, I would apologize.
If I were you… — use were for every subject in the second conditional.

Passive Voice

When what happened matters more than who did it.

55
Something+be + actionparticiple+by + actoroptional

Active Voice

Passive Voice

She writes the reports.→The reports are written by her. · present
They are repairing the road.→The road is being repaired. · present continuous
Someone stole my bike.→My bike was stolen. · past
The chef has chosen the menu.→The menu has been chosen. · present perfect
They will announce the winner.→The winner will be announced. · future
Someone had locked the door.→The door had been locked. · past perfect

When to use it

  • The doer is unknown: My wallet was stolen.
  • The doer is obvious: The thief was arrested.
  • The result matters more: The bridge was built in 1930.
  • Add by + doer only if it’s useful: This song was written by Adele.

Reported Speech

Explaining a dialogue — what someone said, in your own words.

56

Direct → reported

Lucy: “I went out yesterday.”→Lucy said she had gone out the day before.
Sam: “Where are my glasses?”→Sam wondered where his glasses were.
Rita: “I can lend you ten dollars.”→Rita said she could lend me ten dollars.
Captain: “You have a meeting tomorrow.”→The captain told me I had a meeting the next day.
Doctor: “I’ll travel next month.”→The doctor said he would travel the following month.

One step back

am / is → waswork → worked
am working → was workingworked → had worked
have done → had donewill → would
can → couldmay → might

Time & place words

today → that daynow → then
tomorrow → the next dayhere → there
yesterday → the day beforethis → that
next week → the following weekago → before
say has no listener (or “say to me”); tell always needs one: She told me… — never She said me…

Relative Clauses

Essential or extra information — and why commas matter.

57

Defining — essential

No commas

Tells you which one. who · which · that

  • The nurse who helped me was very kind.
  • The laptop that I bought keeps crashing.
  • The café where we met has closed.

Non-defining — extra

Commas

Just adds a fact. who · which · whose — never that

  • My dad, who is 70, still surfs.
  • Lisbon, which is built on hills, is lovely.
  • The Nile, which flows north, is very long.

Whose — possession

  • That’s the singer whose album won.
  • A company whose products last.
  • Ms. Park, whose son studies here, is a judge.

Prepositions

informalthe man (that) I spoke to
formalthe man to whom I spoke
informalthe company I work for

Intensifiers

Make words — and comparisons — stronger or softer.

58

Everyday intensifiers

A LOT
We enjoyed it a lot.
A BIT
It needs a bit more salt.
REALLY
You really know how to cook!
QUITE
This is quite nice!
KIND OF
It’s kind of hard to explain.
DO / DOES
He does play well! emphasis
SO
This is so good!
EXTREMELY
It was extremely cold.

Stronger comparison

much · far · a lot · way (informal)

  • This phone is much more expensive.
  • She’s far more experienced than me.
  • Today is way colder than yesterday.

Softer comparison

slightly · a bit · a little

  • This route is slightly longer.
  • I feel a little better today.
  • He’s a bit taller than his brother.

Double comparatives

THE + comparative,THE + comparative
  • The more you read, the more you learn.
  • The sooner we leave, the better.
  • The older I get, the less I worry.

Reflexive Pronouns

When the same person creates and suffers the action.

59
SubjectReflexiveExample
ImyselfI cut myself while cooking.
youyourselfTake care of yourself.
he, she, ithimself, herself, itselfThe door closes by itself.
weourselvesWe enjoyed ourselves at the party.
youyourselvesHelp yourselves to some cake!
theythemselvesThey built the cabin themselves.

Emphasis — nobody else

  • I painted it myself.
  • The CEO herself answered the call.
  • Did you make this yourself?

By + reflexive = alone

  • She lives by herself.
  • He traveled by himself.
  • I prefer to work by myself.
each other is different: They looked at themselves (in the mirror) ≠ They looked at each other (A → B, B → A).
