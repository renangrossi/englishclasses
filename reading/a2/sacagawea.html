Sacagawea and the Journey West

A sixteen-year-old with a baby on her back, and the moment she recognized her brother.

Play the audio and follow along, then read it again on your own. Tap or hover over a highlighted word to see what it means.

In August 1805, high in the Rocky Mountains, a young woman looked at the chief of a Shoshone group and started to cry. The chief was her brother. She had not seen him for about five years.

Her name was Sacagawea. She was born around 1788 in a Shoshone community in today's Idaho.

When she was about twelve, men from another nation, the Hidatsa, attacked her village and took her away. Later she became the wife of a French-Canadian trader called Toussaint Charbonneau.

In the winter of 1804, the Lewis and Clark expedition stopped near her village. They needed somebody who could speak with the Shoshone, because they would need horses to cross the mountains.

Charbonneau was hired, and Sacagawea went with him. In February 1805 she had a baby boy, Jean Baptiste. She carried him on her back for the whole journey.

People often call her a guide. That is not quite right. She did not know most of the country, and she did not lead the expedition.

But she did things that nobody else could do. She translated. She found plants that the men could eat. One day, when a boat turned over in a river, she saved the books and instruments that were floating away.

There was something else. In those years, a war party never traveled with a woman and a baby. When people saw Sacagawea, they understood that this group had not come to fight.

And in August 1805 she met her brother, Cameahwait, and the Shoshone sold the expedition the horses it needed. Without those horses the men could not have crossed the mountains.

We do not know much about her life after 1806. Most historians think she died in 1812, when she was about twenty-four. Today her face is on an American dollar coin — with her baby on her back.

Key Vocabulary

  • a chief the leader of a group or community
  • a trader a person who buys and sells things
  • to hire to pay somebody to work for you
  • a guide a person who shows other people the way
  • to translate to say in one language what somebody said in another
  • an instrument a tool for measuring or doing careful work
  • a war party a group of people traveling together to fight
  • a historian a person who studies history
  • a coin a round piece of metal money
