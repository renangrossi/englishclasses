My Job

Emma has worked at the coffee shop for six years. This is what the job is really like.

Play the audio and follow along, then read it again on your own. Tap or hover over a highlighted word to see what it means.

I work at a coffee shop. I am there six days a week, from six in the morning to two in the afternoon.

My job is not difficult, but it is not easy either. I stand all day. I make coffee, I take orders, I clean the tables, and I talk to a lot of people.

I work with Carlos. He is twenty-three and he is a student, so he works in the afternoon. My boss, Ray, opens the shop with me on Saturday.

Some of our customers are regular. A woman comes every morning at seven for the same thing: a large coffee, no sugar. I know her face, but I don't know her name.

The money is not good. But I like the mornings, I like the people, and at two o'clock I am free.

Jack says I should look for a better job. Maybe. But not this year.

Key Vocabulary

  • difficult hard to do
  • easy not difficult
  • to take an order to write down what a customer wants
  • to clean to take the dirt off something
  • student a person who studies at a school or a university
  • boss the person who tells you what to do at work
  • regular happening again and again, in the same way
  • customer a person who buys something from a shop
  • sugar the sweet white thing you put in coffee
  • free with no work to do
  • should used to say what is a good idea
