Simple or Continuous?

Two present tenses — a habit, or something happening now.

27

Present simple

Habits · routines · facts

subject+verb (+s)
  • I cycle to work every day.
  • The shop opens at nine.
  • Cats hate water.
  • We visit Grandma on Sundays.
Signal words: always, usually, often, sometimes, never, every day, on Mondays

Present continuous

Now · temporary

am/is/are+verb-ing
  • Look! The baby is walking!
  • I’m reading a great book this month.
  • Shh — they’re recording.
  • She’s staying with friends this week.
Signal words: now, right now, at the moment, look!, listen!, this week / month / year

Same verb, different meaning

He works in a hotel.his job — a routine
He’s working late this week.temporary — only this week
It rains a lot in April.a general fact
It’s raining — take an umbrella!happening now

Frequency

How often? From always to never.

28

Where does it go?

before the main verbI always eat breakfast.
after the verb to beShe is usually calm.
after the first helper verbI can never sleep on buses. / He doesn’t often call.

Counting

once = 1 time
twice = 2 times
three times, four times…
almost softens: almost always · almost never

How often / How frequently + do / does …?

  • I always brush my teeth before bed.
  • They usually have lunch at noon.
  • You almost always arrive on time.
  • In summer I swim twice or three times a week.
  • He never drinks coffee.
  • We sometimes eat out on Fridays.
  • She almost never takes the bus.
  • I call my grandmother once a month.

While & When

A long action in the background — and a short one that interrupts it.

34

WHILE

+ -ing
simultaneous · long actions
  • I was reading while she was painting.
  • While we were eating, the lights went out.
  • He fell asleep while he was watching TV.
  • While I was jogging, I saw an old friend.

WHEN

+ past
short · finished actions
  • She was cooking when the phone rang.
  • When I opened the door, I saw the cake.
  • They were leaving when it started to snow.
  • When he arrived, we were having dinner.
Pattern: While + past continuous, simple past. · Simple past + when + past continuous… or the other way round: past continuous + when + simple past.

Future

Will vs. going to — affirmative and negative.

35

WILL

100% · decided now

BE GOING TO

am · is · are · plans & evidence

Affirmative

’ll · ’m going to · ’s going to · ’re going to

Personal decision · offer · promise

  • The phone’s ringing — I’ll get it.
  • Don’t worry, I’ll help you move.

Fact · belief · opinion

  • She will be forty next month.
  • I think people will love this idea.

Plan · already decided

  • We’re going to repaint the house.
  • I’m going to learn to drive this year.

Prediction · with evidence

  • Look at that sky — it’s going to storm.
  • He’s going to fall — catch him!

Negative

will not (won’t) · be not going to
  • I won’t tell anyone, I promise.
  • He won’t accept that offer.
  • The shop won’t open on Sunday.
  • We’re not going to cook tonight.
  • She isn’t going to apply for the job.
  • It’s not going to be easy.

Comparative Forms

Compare two things — or pick the most or least of a group.

39

Comparative — two things

LONG ADJ.→more ___ (than)
  • This course is more expensive than that one.
  • She is more patient than her brother.
SHORT ADJ.→___-er (than)
  • My bag is lighter than yours.
  • Today is hotter than yesterday.

Inferiority −

less ___ than
  • This sofa is less comfortable than the old one.
  • Tea is less sweet than juice.

Equality =

as ___ as
  • He is as tall as his dad.
  • My car isn’t as fast as yours.

Superlative — three or more

LONG ADJ.→the most ___
  • It’s the most beautiful beach I know.
  • This is the most modern phone yet.
SHORT ADJ.→the ___-est
  • She’s the youngest in the team.
  • It’s the cheapest hotel in town.
Inferiority −− the least ___ : That was the least interesting film of the festival.

Present Perfect

Actions that started in the past with some connection to the present.

40

Affirmative

I’ve · you’ve · she’s · we’ve · they’ve
Iyouhe, she, itweyouthey
+HAVEHAS+VERB IN PARTICIPLEregular · irregular
  • I have fixed the kitchen sink.
  • She has visited twelve countries.
  • We’ve known each other for years.
  • They have grown so much!
  • It has rained all week — the garden is flooded.

Negative

haven’t · hasn’t
Iyouhe, she, itweyouthey
+HAVEHAVEN’THASHASN’T+NOT+PARTICIPLE
  • He hasn’t eaten since breakfast.
  • I haven’t seen that movie yet.
  • The printer hasn’t worked for days.
  • You haven’t heard the best part!

When do we use it?

  • Life experience, no date: I’ve been to Japan.
  • Recent action, present result: I’ve lost my keys. (I don’t have them now)
  • Unfinished time: She’s called three times today.

Conditions I

Zero and first conditionals — facts and real possibilities.

42

Zero conditional — present

General truths

IF+simple present,simple present
  • If you heat ice, it melts.
  • If I drink coffee late, I don’t sleep.
  • Plants die if you don’t water them.

First conditional — present / future

Real possibilities

IF+simple present,will · can · could + verb
  • If it rains tomorrow, we’ll stay home.
  • If you hurry, you can catch the train.
  • If she practices, she could win the contest.
  • Your back will feel better if you stretch every day.
The if-clause never takes will: If it rains…, not If it will rain…. The two halves can swap places — drop the comma when if is in the middle.

Who · Which · That

Relative pronouns add information about a person or thing.

43

WHO

people
  • She’s the chef who bakes the bread.
  • I need someone who speaks German.
  • The man who lives upstairs is a pilot.

WHICH

things · extra info · options
  • My car, which is twenty years old, still runs.
  • The hotel, which has a pool, is full.
  • We can’t decide which movie to watch.

THAT

people or things · one of many
  • He took the jacket that was on the chair.
  • These are the shoes that I told you about.
  • It’s the song that made her famous.

Commas matter

No commas — tells you which oneThe book that you lent me is great.
Commas — just extra informationThe book, which you lent me, is great.
Never use that with commas — use which for things and who for people.
