B1

Future Continuous & Future Perfect

Two ways to describe the future from a specific point in time: in progress, or already finished.

ObjectivesRulesExamplesCommon MistakesPracticeSummaryTest Yourself

Introduction

Picture a specific moment in the future — say, next Friday at noon. Will something still be happening then, or will it already be finished? That's the choice between these two tenses.

By the end of this lesson you can…

  • Review the future continuous (in progress at a future moment)
  • Form and use the future perfect (will have + past participle)
  • Tell the two apart based on whether the action is finished or ongoing at that future point

Grammar Rules

The Rules

a) Future continuous (review)

will be + -ing = still happening at that future moment.

  • At noon, I will be traveling. (in progress at that moment)

b) Future perfect

will have + past participle = already finished by that future moment.

  • By noon, I will have arrived. (finished before or exactly at that moment)
  • By next year, she will have graduated.

c) Telling them apart

Future continuous answers "what will be happening?"; future perfect answers "what will already be done?" — the key signal word is often "by" (future perfect) vs. a moment in time alone (future continuous).

Examples

See It in Action

  • At noon, I will be traveling.
  • By noon, I will have arrived.
  • This time next week, we will be relaxing on the beach.
  • By next year, she will have graduated.
  • By the time you read this, I will have left.
  • At 8pm, they will still be working.

Common Mistakes

Watch Out For

AvoidBy tomorrow, I will finishing the report.

Use insteadBy tomorrow, I will have finished the report.

"By" + a future point signals completion — use the future perfect (will have + past participle).

AvoidAt noon, I will have traveling.

Use insteadAt noon, I will be traveling.

An action still in progress at that moment needs the future continuous (will be + -ing).

AvoidBy next year, she will have graduate.

Use insteadBy next year, she will have graduated.

The future perfect needs the past participle (graduated), not the base form.
