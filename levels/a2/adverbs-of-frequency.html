A2

Adverbs of Frequency

Say how often something happens, and put the word in the right place.

ObjectivesRulesExamplesCommon MistakesPracticeSummaryTest Yourself

Introduction

These small words appear in almost every conversation about routines and habits — and their position in the sentence follows a clear, simple rule.

By the end of this lesson you can…

  • Order adverbs of frequency from 100% to 0%
  • Place the adverb correctly with the verb "to be", auxiliary verbs, and other verbs
  • Use adverbs of frequency naturally in everyday sentences

Grammar Rules

The Rules

The scale

always (100%) → usually (80–90%) → often (60–70%) → sometimes (30–50%) → rarely (10–20%) → never (0%)

Word order

  • After the verb "to be" (am/is/are/was/were): She is always late.
  • Before other (ordinary) verbs: I always eat breakfast.
  • After the first auxiliary verb: I can never sleep on planes. / She doesn't often call.

Examples

See It in Action

  • I always eat breakfast before I go to work.
  • She is usually tired after work.
  • They often watch movies on the weekends.
  • He rarely forgets his homework.
  • We never go to the beach in winter.
  • My brother sometimes helps me with my homework.

Common Mistakes

Watch Out For

AvoidI eat always breakfast.

Use insteadI always eat breakfast.

With an ordinary verb (eat), the adverb goes before it, not after.

AvoidShe always is late.

Use insteadShe is always late.

With "to be," the adverb goes after it, not before.

AvoidHe usually wake up early.

Use insteadHe usually wakes up early.

The adverb doesn't change the need for -s on third person singular verbs.

AvoidShe always go to the gym on Mondays.

Use insteadShe always goes to the gym on Mondays.

Third person singular (she) still needs -s, even with the adverb in front.

Summary

Review

  • Scale: always (100%) → usually → often → sometimes → rarely → never (0%).
  • The adverb goes after "to be," but before an ordinary verb.
  • With an auxiliary verb (can, do, will), the adverb goes right after the first auxiliary.
