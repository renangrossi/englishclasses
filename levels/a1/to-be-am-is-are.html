To Be (Am, Is, Are)

The most important verb in English — master it first.

ObjectivesExplanationRulesExamplesCommon MistakesPracticeSummaryTest YourselfRelated

Introduction

“To be” is the single most frequent verb in English. It changes form depending on the subject, and it's the foundation for descriptions, identity, location and much more.

By the end of this lesson you can…

  • Use am, is and are correctly with every subject pronoun
  • Form negative sentences with “to be”
  • Form yes/no questions with “to be”

Explanation

Explanation

“To be” has three present-tense forms: am (with I), is (with he/she/it and singular nouns), and are (with you/we/they and plural nouns).

To make a question, simply move the verb before the subject: Is he a teacher? To make it negative, add “not” right after the verb: He is not (isn't) a teacher.

Grammar Rules

The Rules

  • Affirmative: I am, He/She/It is, You/We/They are
  • Negative: I am not, He/She/It is not (isn't), You/We/They are not (aren't)
  • Question: Am I...? / Is he...? / Are they...?
  • Contractions: I'm, he's, she's, it's, you're, we're, they're

Examples

See It in Action

  • I am a student.
  • She is my friend.
  • They are at the park.
  • The book is on the table.
  • Is it a sunny day?

Common Mistakes

Watch Out For

AvoidI is a teacher.

Use insteadI am a teacher.

“I” always pairs with “am,” never “is” or “are.”

AvoidThey am friends.

Use insteadThey are friends.

“They” pairs with “are,” not “am.”

AvoidShe are happy.

Use insteadShe is happy.

Singular subjects like “she” pair with “is,” not “are.”

Tip

Memorize the three forms as a set — am/is/are — and drill them with every pronoun until the pairing is automatic.

Summary

Review

  • Am goes with I; is goes with he/she/it; are goes with you/we/they.
  • Negatives add “not” after the verb.
  • Questions move the verb before the subject.
