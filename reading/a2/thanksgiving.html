Thanksgiving

The biggest meal of the American year, and the busiest travel day.

Thanksgiving is on the fourth Thursday of November. For many Americans it is a bigger day than Christmas.

People travel to be with family. The Wednesday before Thanksgiving is one of the busiest travel days of the year: the airports are full, the roads are slow, and everybody is tired before the holiday starts.

The meal is almost always the same. There is turkey, and there are potatoes, and there is a sauce made from a small red fruit called a cranberry. At the end there is pumpkin pie. Families argue about the details — what goes inside the turkey, whose recipe is correct — and the argument is part of the day.

Cooking takes a long time. A big turkey needs four or five hours in the oven, so somebody gets up very early. Everybody else arrives at one or two o'clock and says the kitchen smells wonderful.

People eat at about three in the afternoon, which is late for lunch and early for dinner. After the meal, some of the family watch American football on television and some of them fall asleep in a chair.

The next day is called Black Friday. The stores open early and everything is cheap, and some people go shopping at five in the morning. Other people stay at home and eat the same meal again, cold, which is better.

Not every family is happy. Thanksgiving puts relatives in one room for six hours, and not all of them agree about everything. But most people go. It is that kind of day.

Vocabulary

  • holiday — a special day when people do not work
  • turkey — a large bird Americans eat at Thanksgiving
  • sauce — a thick liquid you put on food
  • recipe — the instructions for cooking something
  • to argue — to disagree in words
  • oven — the closed hot box you cook food inside
  • wonderful — very good
  • to fall asleep — to start sleeping
  • store — a place where you buy things — American; British say shop
  • cheap — not costing a lot of money
  • relative — a person in your family
  • to agree — to have the same opinion
