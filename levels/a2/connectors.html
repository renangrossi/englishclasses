A2

Connectors

Link your ideas smoothly: addition, contrast, reason, and result.

ObjectivesRulesExamplesCommon MistakesPracticeSummaryTest Yourself

Introduction

You already know "and," "but," and "or." This lesson expands your set of connecting words, organized by what job each one does.

By the end of this lesson you can…

  • Use connectors of addition (also, as well as)
  • Use connectors of contrast (but, however, although)
  • Use connectors of reason and result (because, so, therefore)

Grammar Rules

The Rules

Addition

and, also, too, as well as, moreover, furthermore — add a similar idea.

Contrast

but, however, although, though, whereas, on the other hand — show a difference or surprise.

Reason & Result

because/since/as (reason) → so/therefore (result). "Because" gives the cause; "so" gives the effect.

Examples

See It in Action

  • I like coffee, but I don't like tea.
  • She studied hard, so she passed the exam.
  • He is tired, and he wants to rest.
  • Although it was raining, we went for a walk.
  • I stayed home because I was sick.
  • She's very talented; however, she doesn't have much experience.

Common Mistakes

Watch Out For

AvoidI like coffee, so I don't like tea.

Use insteadI like coffee, but I don't like tea.

"So" shows result, not contrast — a contrast between two preferences needs "but."

AvoidShe studied hard, but she passed the exam.

Use insteadShe studied hard, so she passed the exam.

Studying causing success is a result, not a contrast — use "so."

AvoidAlthough it was raining, but we went for a walk.

Use insteadAlthough it was raining, we went for a walk.

Don't combine "although" with "but" — the contrast is already built into "although."

Summary

Review

  • Addition: also, as well as, moreover. Contrast: but, however, although. Reason: because/since. Result: so/therefore.
  • Don't combine "although" (or "whereas") with "but" — the contrast is already built in.
  • "Because" introduces the cause; "so" introduces the effect — keep the order clear.
