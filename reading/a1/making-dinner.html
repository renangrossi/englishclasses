Making Dinner

Rice, chicken, and one thing Emma always forgets.

Play the audio and follow along, then read it again on your own. Tap or hover over a highlighted word to see what it means.

Tonight it is my turn to cook. I am not a good cook, but I make three or four things well, and chicken with rice is one of them.

First I look in the refrigerator. I have chicken, rice, onions, and half a lemon. I do not have bread. I always forget the bread.

I put water in a pot for the rice. While the water gets hot, I cut the onions. My eyes hurt a little, but only for a minute.

I put oil in a pan and I cook the chicken. It takes about fifteen minutes. The kitchen smells very good.

Jack comes in. “Can I help?”

“Yes. Put the plates on the table, please. And open the window — it's hot in here.”

We eat at half past seven. It is not a special dinner, but it is hot, we are hungry, and there is enough for tomorrow.

Key Vocabulary

  • my turn the time when it is my job to do something
  • refrigerator the cold machine where you keep food
  • onion a strong white or red vegetable that makes your eyes water
  • to forget to not remember
  • pot a deep round container for cooking food in water
  • while during the same time as
  • to cut to divide something with a knife
  • to hurt to give you pain
  • oil the liquid fat you cook with
  • pan a wide flat container for cooking on the stove
  • to smell to have a smell
  • plate the flat dish you eat from
  • enough as much as you need
