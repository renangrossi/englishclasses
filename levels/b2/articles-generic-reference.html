Articles — Generic Reference & Common L1 Mistakes

Know when English uses no article at all — one of the most common sources of mistakes for Portuguese speakers.

ObjectivesExplanationRulesExamplesCommon MistakesPracticeSummary

Introduction

You already know a/an/the for specific, countable, first-mention nouns. At B2, the harder skill is knowing when English uses no article at all — especially for generic statements, which is exactly where Portuguese speakers tend to add one by mistake.

By the end of this lesson you can…

  • Use no article for generic plural/uncountable nouns and abstract concepts
  • Use no article with meals, means of transport and most institutions in fixed expressions
  • Recognize the specific pattern of L1 transfer from Portuguese, which usually does use an article in these cases
  • Contrast generic reference (no article) with a specific instance (the)

Explanation

Explanation

Register

This is one of the most persistent errors for Portuguese speakers, since Portuguese almost always uses an article where English generic statements use none — the mistake often survives even at advanced levels, precisely because it doesn't affect comprehension, only correctness.

Grammar Rules

The Rules

a) Generic reference — no article

When you talk about something in general (not one specific example), plural and uncountable nouns take no article in English.

  • Coffee is popular in Brazil. (coffee in general — not "the coffee")
  • Dogs are loyal animals. (dogs in general — not "the dogs," unless you mean specific dogs already mentioned)
  • Compare: The coffee on the table is cold. (one specific coffee)

In Portuguese this is different: "O café é popular no Brasil" / "Os cães são leais" both use an article — English does not, for generic statements.

b) Abstract nouns — no article

Abstract concepts (love, life, happiness, freedom, information, advice, music, art) usually take no article when talking about the concept generally.

  • Life is unpredictable. (not "The life")
  • Music helps me relax. (not "The music," unless referring to specific music already mentioned)

c) Meals, transport and fixed expressions

No article in common fixed expressions:

  • Meals: have breakfast / lunch / dinner (not "the breakfast")
  • Transport: by car / by bus / by plane / on foot (not "by the car")
  • Places used for their normal purpose: go to school / go to work / go to bed / in bed / at home

Examples

See It in Action

  • Water is essential for life.
  • The water in this glass is warm.
  • Cats sleep most of the day.
  • The cats next door make a lot of noise at night.
  • Money can't buy happiness.
  • I usually have lunch at noon.
  • We went to Paris by train.
  • Children learn languages faster than adults.
  • Honesty is the best policy.
  • He goes to bed early on weekdays.

Common Mistakes

Watch Out For

AvoidThe coffee is my favorite drink.

Use insteadCoffee is my favorite drink.

A generic statement about coffee in general takes no article — “the coffee” would mean one specific coffee (e.g., the one in front of you).

AvoidThe life is short.

Use insteadLife is short.

Abstract nouns used generically take no article.

AvoidI go to the school by the bus.

Use insteadI go to school by bus.

Institutions used for their normal purpose (school) and means of transport (by bus) take no article in these fixed expressions.

AvoidThe dogs are loyal animals.

Use insteadDogs are loyal animals.

A generic statement about dogs in general — "the dogs" would only be correct if referring to specific, already-identified dogs.

AvoidI like the music and the art.

Use insteadI like music and art.

Talking about music and art as general concepts/interests, not one specific piece — no article.

Summary

Review

  • Generic statements about plural/uncountable nouns take no article: Coffee is popular, not The coffee is popular.
  • Abstract nouns used generically (life, love, happiness, music) take no article.
  • Fixed expressions with meals, transport and everyday institutions take no article: have breakfast, by car, go to school.
  • Add “the” only when referring to one specific, already-identified instance.
