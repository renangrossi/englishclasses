A2

Present Simple & Present Continuous Review

Know exactly which present tense to use, and why.

ObjectivesRulesExamplesCommon MistakesPracticeSummaryTest Yourself

Introduction

You already learned each tense separately at A1. The real skill at A2 is choosing correctly between them — most mistakes come from mixing them up, not from forming them wrong.

By the end of this lesson you can…

  • Use the present simple for habits, routines and general facts
  • Use the present continuous for actions happening right now or temporary situations
  • Recognize the time words that signal each tense
  • Choose correctly between the two when both are grammatically possible

Grammar Rules

The Rules

a) Present simple — habits, routines, facts

Use it for things that are generally or repeatedly true, not just happening at this moment.

  • I go to school every day. (a routine)
  • Water boils at 100°C. (a general fact)
  • Signal words: always, usually, often, sometimes, every day/week, on Mondays.

b) Present continuous — now, or temporary

Use it for an action in progress at this exact moment, or a temporary situation around now.

  • She is reading a book right now. (happening now)
  • I'm studying English this year. (temporary, even if not this exact second)
  • Signal words: now, right now, at the moment, look!, listen!, this week/month/year.

c) Choosing between them

Ask: is this a repeated habit, or is it happening right now/temporarily? "He works in a bank" (his job, a routine) is different from "He is working late this week" (temporary, this week only).

Examples

See It in Action

  • I go to school every day.
  • Look! She is running in the park.
  • They play basketball every Saturday.
  • He is watching TV right now.
  • We go to the gym after school.
  • It's raining outside at the moment.

Common Mistakes

Watch Out For

AvoidI am go to school every day.

Use insteadI go to school every day.

A routine (every day) needs the present simple, not the continuous.

AvoidLook! She runs in the park.

Use insteadLook! She is running in the park.

"Look!" signals something happening right now — use the present continuous.

AvoidShe reading a book now.

Use insteadShe is reading a book now.

The present continuous always needs the verb "to be" (is/am/are) before the -ing form.

AvoidHe watch TV right now.

Use insteadHe is watching TV right now.

"Right now" needs the present continuous, and third person (he) needs "is," not the bare verb.

Summary

Review

  • Present simple = habits, routines, general facts (always, usually, every day).
  • Present continuous = happening now, or temporary (now, right now, this week, Look!).
  • The present continuous always needs am/is/are before the -ing verb.
  • Third person singular (he/she/it) adds -s only in the present simple, never in the continuous.
