--------------------------------------------------
LEVEL: C1
TOPIC: Complex Subordinate Clauses
--------------------------------------------------

1. BRIEF EXPLANATION

Subordinate clauses let you link a main idea to a secondary one with precision. At C1 you need to control three families beyond simple "because" / "although":

a) Concessive clauses (contrast) — even though, although, though, whereas, while, in spite of / despite (+ noun or -ing).
   - "Even though / although / though" + clause show a surprising contrast: Even though it was raining, we went for a walk.
   - "Whereas / while" contrast two facts neutrally, often side by side: She loves cities, whereas he prefers the countryside.
   - "In spite of / despite" + noun/gerund (never a full clause directly): Despite the rain, we went for a walk. / Despite working hard, she failed.

b) Purpose clauses — so as to, in order to/that, for the purpose of.
   - "So as to" / "in order to" + bare infinitive express purpose formally: We left early so as to avoid the traffic.
   - "In order that" / "so that" + clause (with can/could/will/would) are used when the subject changes: I gave her my number so that she could contact me.

c) Result clauses — such...that, so...that.
   - "So" + adjective/adverb + "that": The exam was so difficult that half the class failed.
   - "Such" + (a/an) + adjective + noun + "that": It was such a difficult exam that half the class failed.

Register note: "in order that," "so as to," and "whereas" belong to formal/written English; "so," "although," and "but" are fine in speech.

2. EXAMPLES

1. Even though she was exhausted, she finished the marathon.
2. Whereas the north of the country is mountainous, the south is flat.
3. Despite having no experience, he got the job.
4. In spite of the criticism, the director refused to change the ending.
5. We arrived early so as to get good seats.
6. She whispered so that no one else would hear.
7. He studied hard in order to pass the bar exam.
8. The music was so loud that we couldn't hear each other talk.
9. It was such a compelling argument that the jury changed its mind.
10. While I understand your concerns, I still disagree with the decision.
11. The report was so poorly written that it had to be redone.
12. He left the company in order that his rival could take over.

3. COMMON MISTAKES

1. Incorrect: Despite he was tired, he kept working.
   Correct: Despite being tired, he kept working. / Although he was tired, he kept working.
   Why: "Despite/in spite of" take a noun or gerund, not a subject + verb clause.

2. Incorrect: The film was such interesting that I watched it twice.
   Correct: The film was so interesting that I watched it twice.
   Why: "So" goes with an adjective alone; "such" needs a noun (such an interesting film).

3. Incorrect: She works hard so as she can support her family.
   Correct: She works hard so that she can support her family. / She works hard in order to support her family.
   Why: "So as to" takes an infinitive, not a clause; use "so that" for a full clause with a modal.

4. Incorrect: Whereas he is generous, but she is quite mean.
   Correct: Whereas he is generous, she is quite mean.
   Why: Don't combine "whereas" with "but" — the contrast is already built into "whereas."

5. Incorrect: It was so a boring lecture that half the room fell asleep.
   Correct: It was such a boring lecture that half the room fell asleep.
   Why: With "adjective + noun," use "such (a/an)," not "so."

4. EXERCISES

A. Gap-fill (so, such, whereas, despite, in order to/that, so as to)
1. ___ the traffic, we arrived right on time.
2. He whispered the plan ___ no one would overhear.
3. It was ___ a long meeting that people started checking their phones.
4. The coffee here is excellent, ___ the tea is rather weak.
5. She left the party early ___ catch the last train.
6. The instructions were ___ confusing that nobody followed them correctly.
7. He trained every day ___ improve his stamina.
8. ___ having a fever, she still went to work.
9. He spoke slowly and clearly ___ everyone in the room could understand.
10. It was ___ a generous offer that we couldn't refuse it.

B. Rewrite / Transformation
1. He is rich, but he lives very simply. (rewrite with "whereas")
2. She failed the exam, but she didn't lose confidence. (rewrite with "even though")
3. He turned down the promotion. He wanted more free time. (join with "in order to")
4. It rained heavily. We still finished the hike. (join with "despite")
5. The test was extremely hard. Nobody finished it in time. (join with "so...that")
6. He apologized publicly. He wanted to repair his reputation. (join with "so that")
7. It was a very persuasive speech. It changed several votes. (join with "such...that")

C. Error Correction
1. Despite he arrived late, no one seemed to mind.
2. It was so an emotional film that everyone cried.
3. She practiced every day so as she could improve faster.
4. Whereas I respect his opinion, but I think he's wrong.
5. The meeting was such long that we had to order dinner.
6. In spite of he lost the match, he stayed positive.

D. Guided Production
1. Write one sentence about your own life using "even though."
2. Write one sentence using "whereas" to contrast two people or places you know.
3. Write one sentence using "so...that" describing a memorable experience.
4. Write one sentence using "in order to" about a goal you're working toward.

B. Rewrite / Transformation
1. He is rich, whereas he lives very simply.
2. Even though she failed the exam, she didn't lose confidence.
3. He turned down the promotion in order to have more free time.
4. Despite the heavy rain, we still finished the hike.
5. The test was so hard that nobody finished it in time.
6. He apologized publicly so that he could repair his reputation.
7. It was such a persuasive speech that it changed several votes.

C. Error Correction
1. Despite arriving late / Although he arrived late, no one seemed to mind.
2. It was such an emotional film that everyone cried.
3. She practiced every day so as to improve faster / so that she could improve faster.
4. Whereas I respect his opinion, I think he's wrong. (remove "but")
5. The meeting was so long that we had to order dinner.
6. In spite of losing the match / Although he lost the match, he stayed positive.

D. Guided Production (sample answers)
1. Even though I was scared of heights, I went skydiving for my birthday.
2. My sister loves spontaneous trips, whereas I prefer planning every detail in advance.
3. The concert was so loud that my ears were ringing for hours afterward.
4. I've been saving money in order to travel around South America next year.
