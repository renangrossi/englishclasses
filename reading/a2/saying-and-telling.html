My First Week at Work

Four verbs that give learners trouble — say, tell, speak and talk — in a situation where you actually need them.

ReadingPracticeDiscussion

Reading

Play the audio and follow along, then read it again on your own. Tap or hover over a highlighted word to see what it means.

On my first day at the store, my manager told me to arrive at eight. I got there at ten to eight, because I did not want to be late on the first morning.

My manager's name is Diane. She said good morning, and then she talked to me for about twenty minutes about how the store works. She told me where the break room was, she told me when my lunch break was, and she said I could ask her anything at all.

Then she introduced me to Ray, who has worked there for nine years. Ray does not talk very much, but when he speaks, people listen. He said only one thing to me that morning: "Ask before you guess." I have thought about that a lot since then.

On Tuesday a customer asked me where the batteries were, and I had no idea. I remembered what Ray said, so I told the customer to wait one moment and I went and asked. She thanked me for checking instead of pointing her in the wrong direction.

On Wednesday Diane spoke to me about the register. I was slow, and I knew it. She did not say I was doing badly. She said, "You are careful, and careful is the right thing to be in your first week. Speed comes later."

By Friday I could talk to customers without feeling nervous. I still could not answer everything, but I had learned to say "Let me find out for you" instead of guessing.

On Friday evening, Diane told me I had done well. Then she said something I did not expect: "Next week you can start training the new person." I laughed and told her I had only been there five days. She said that was exactly why — I still remembered what was confusing.

Key Vocabulary

  • to say something say + the words. You do not need a person: 'She said good morning.'
  • to tell someone tell + a person. 'She told me where the break room was.'
  • to speak to someone slightly more formal than 'talk'; often about one serious thing
  • to talk to someone to have a conversation, usually informal and two-way
  • a break room the room at work where employees eat and rest
  • the register the machine where customers pay (British English: the till)
  • to guess to give an answer you are not sure about
  • to find out to get information you do not have yet

Speaking & Writing

Discussion

There are no right answers here — use these to practice speaking, or write a short answer to each.

  • Describe your first day in a job, at a school, or in a new city. What did someone tell you that helped?
  • Ray says, "Ask before you guess." Is that good advice in your own work? Why or why not?
  • Diane praised him for being careful rather than fast. Which matters more when you are new at something?
  • Write about 70 words about someone who taught you something at work or at school. Use 'said', 'told me' and 'talked to me' at least once each.
