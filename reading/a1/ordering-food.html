A1 · Food & Dining

Lunch on Saturday

Emma and Jack don't cook on Saturday. They go out — and this is what you say.

ReadingPracticeDiscussion

Reading

Play the audio and follow along, then read it again on your own. Tap or hover over a highlighted word to see what it means.

On Saturday, Jack and I go out for lunch. We don't cook on Saturday.

We go to a small place near the park. It is not expensive and the food is good. We are hungry.

The server comes to our table. “Are you ready to order?”

“Yes,” I say. “I'd like the chicken sandwich, please. And a glass of water.”

“And for you, sir?”

“Can I have the chicken too? With rice, not fries.” Jack is always hungry.

“Of course. Anything else?”

“No, that's all, thank you.”

The food comes in ten minutes. We eat and we talk. At the end, Jack asks for the check. It is twenty-four dollars. He pays, and he leaves a tip on the table.

Key Vocabulary

  • to go out to leave home to do something you enjoy
  • expensive costing a lot of money
  • hungry wanting to eat
  • server the person who brings your food in a restaurant
  • to order to ask for the food you want in a restaurant
  • I'd like a polite way to say “I want”
  • Can I have a polite way to ask for something
  • fries long pieces of potato cooked in hot oil
  • Of course yes, certainly
  • Anything else do you want more?
  • that's all nothing more; I am finished
  • the check the paper that shows how much you pay — in American English
  • tip extra money you leave for the server
