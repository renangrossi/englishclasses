--------------------------------------------------
LEVEL: C2
TOPIC: Complex Ellipsis and Substitution
--------------------------------------------------

1. BRIEF EXPLANATION

Ellipsis (omitting words that are recoverable from context) and substitution (replacing repeated words with a short pro-form) make advanced English tighter and more natural, avoiding clumsy repetition.

a) Ellipsis in reduced clauses — omitting a repeated subject, auxiliary, or verb when the meaning stays clear.
   - After "and/but/or" in parallel structures: She ordered coffee and [she ordered] a croissant.
   - In comparative/contrastive clauses: He works harder than she [works]. / Some like tea; others [like] coffee.
   - After auxiliaries, when the main verb is recoverable: He hasn't finished yet, but he will [finish] soon.
   - In subordinate clauses with the same subject as the main clause: While [he was] waiting, he read the newspaper.

b) Omission patterns common in academic and literary writing — dropping relative pronouns and "that" in defining clauses, and omitting "which is/who is" before a reduced participle or adjective phrase.
   The book [that] I mentioned is finally out. / The results, [which were] published last year, remain controversial.

c) Pro-forms in complex texts — using "do so," "one," "the same," "such," or simple auxiliaries to stand in for a whole verb phrase or idea already mentioned, avoiding repetition across sentences.
   He asked her to leave, and she did so immediately. (= she left immediately)
   If you need a pen, take one from my desk. (= a pen, not a specific one already mentioned)
   The results confirmed the hypothesis; subsequent studies have done the same.

These devices are essential for sounding genuinely native-like: overusing full repetition, even when grammatically correct, sounds stilted at C2.

2. EXAMPLES

1. She likes jazz, and her brother does too.
2. He hasn't called yet, but he probably will soon.
3. Some prefer working from home; others don't.
4. While waiting for the bus, she finished her book.
5. The report [that] we discussed yesterday has been revised.
6. The proposal, [which was] submitted last month, is still under review.
7. He asked me to stay quiet, and I did so, reluctantly.
8. If you need an umbrella, take one from the stand by the door.
9. The first study confirmed the theory; the second one did the same.
10. She can speak French fluently, and so can her sister.
11. He wanted to leave early, but couldn't.
12. Whatever the outcome, we'll accept it.

3. COMMON MISTAKES

1. Incorrect: She likes jazz, and her brother does it too.
   Correct: She likes jazz, and her brother does too. (or: ...and so does her brother.)
   Why: "Does too" alone functions as the substitute verb phrase — adding "it" creates redundancy.

2. Incorrect: He hasn't called yet, but he will do it soon.
   Correct: He hasn't called yet, but he will soon. / ...but he will do so soon.
   Why: Use the bare auxiliary or "do so," not "will do it," which is redundant/informal here.

3. Incorrect: While she was waiting, was reading a magazine.
   Correct: While waiting, she was reading a magazine. / While she was waiting, she was reading a magazine.
   Why: If you omit the subject after "while," you must also omit "was" (ellipsis applies to subject + auxiliary together); otherwise, keep both.

4. Incorrect: If you need a pen, take the one from my desk. (when no specific pen has been mentioned)
   Correct: If you need a pen, take one from my desk.
   Why: "One" (indefinite substitution) is used for an unspecified item; "the one" implies a specific, previously identified item.

5. Incorrect: He asked her to apologize, and she did so it.
   Correct: He asked her to apologize, and she did so.
   Why: "Did so" already replaces the whole verb phrase — don't add another object after it.

4. EXERCISES

A. Gap-fill (complete with the correct ellipsis/substitution or write "—" if a word should be omitted)
1. He plays the guitar, and his sister ___ too.
2. She hasn't finished the report, but she ___ by Friday.
3. ___ waiting for the results, they kept refreshing the page.
4. If you need a chair, grab ___ from the next room.
5. The plan worked in the first city; it ___ in the second.
6. He told them to wait, and they ___.
7. Some people love spicy food; ___ can't stand it.
8. The book ___ you recommended was excellent.

B. Rewrite / Transformation (rewrite each pair using ellipsis or substitution to avoid repetition)
1. My father can cook well. My mother can cook well too.
2. He hasn't decided yet, but he will decide soon.
3. She was reading while she was waiting for the train.
4. The first experiment succeeded. The second experiment succeeded too.
5. If you want a coffee, take a coffee from the machine.
6. He told me to relax, and I relaxed.

C. Error Correction
1. She likes hiking, and her friend does it too.
2. While she was cooking, was listening to music.
3. He asked me to stop, and I did so it.
4. If you need a towel, take the one from the shelf. (no towel mentioned before)
5. He hasn't replied yet, but he will do it tomorrow.
6. The first plan failed; the second plan did the same it.

D. Guided Production
1. Write two sentences about two people with a shared habit, using "does/did too" or "so does/did."
2. Write one sentence with a reduced clause after "while" or "when," omitting the repeated subject.
3. Write one sentence using "do so" to substitute for a whole action already mentioned.
4. Write one sentence using indefinite "one" to substitute for an unspecified item.
