Grammar Rules

The Rules

a) Passive reporting structures — used to report general beliefs, rumors or claims without naming the source.

  • It + is/was + reporting verb (said, believed, thought, reported, understood, rumored, alleged) + that-clause e.g. It is believed that the fire started in the kitchen.
  • Subject + is/was + reporting verb + to-infinitive (simple infinitive for the same time; perfect infinitive "to have + past participle" for an earlier time) e.g. He is believed to be abroad. / He is believed to have left the country.

b) Passive with modals — modal + be + past participle for present/future obligation or possibility; modal + have been + past participle (modal perfect passive) for something that should/might/must have happened (or not) in the past.

e.g. The report must be submitted by Friday.

e.g. The deadline should have been extended.

c) Get-passive — get + past participle, mainly in informal/spoken registers. It often adds a sense of something happening suddenly, unexpectedly, or with some difficulty/responsibility involved, and it can sound more dynamic than the neutral be-passive.

e.g. Our flight got cancelled at the last minute.

Compare: The flight was cancelled. (neutral, factual) vs. The flight got cancelled. (something happened to us, more personal/dramatic)

Use the reporting passive to distance yourself from a claim (useful in journalism and academic writing), the modal passive to talk about obligations/expectations, and the get-passive for informal, event-focused narration.

Examples

See It in Action

  • He is said to have worked as a spy during the war.
  • It is thought that the vase dates back to the Ming dynasty.
  • She is rumored to be leaving the company next month.
  • The missing files are believed to have been destroyed.
  • All applications must be reviewed before Friday.
  • The bridge should have been repaired years ago.
  • My laptop got stolen on the train yesterday.
  • He got promoted after just one year in the role.
  • It is widely reported that the negotiations collapsed.
  • The contract is understood to have been signed under pressure.
  • Mistakes like this shouldn't be tolerated in a hospital.
  • Their car got towed while they were at the concert.

Common Mistakes

Watch Out For

AvoidHe is said writing the letter himself.

Use insteadHe is said to have written the letter himself.

The reporting passive needs a to-infinitive; a completed past action needs the perfect infinitive "to have + past participle."

AvoidThe task should finished by now.

Use insteadThe task should have been finished by now.

The modal perfect passive requires "have been," not just "been" or the bare verb.

AvoidThe window got broke during the storm.

Use insteadThe window got broken during the storm.

Get-passive still needs the past participle (broken), not the past simple form (broke).

AvoidIt is said he was rich, that he owned three houses.

Use insteadIt is said that he was rich and owned three houses.

Don't mix the that-clause pattern with a stray comma splice; join ideas with "and" or restructure into one clean that-clause.

AvoidThe report is said to written by an intern.

Use insteadThe report is said to have been written by an intern.

Missing "have been" — needed for a passive action that happened before now.
