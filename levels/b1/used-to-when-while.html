B1

Used To / Be Used To / When vs. While

Talk about finished past habits, being accustomed to something, and connect two past events precisely.

ObjectivesRulesExamplesCommon MistakesPracticeSummary

Introduction

"Used to" and "be used to" look almost identical but mean completely different things — this lesson also reviews when to use "when" versus "while" in past narratives.

By the end of this lesson you can…

  • Use "used to" for finished past habits
  • Use "be used to" for being accustomed/familiar with something
  • Choose between "when" (short/interrupting action) and "while" (longer/background action)

Grammar Rules

The Rules

a) Used to — a finished past habit

Subject + used to + infinitive. Describes something true repeatedly in the past, not true now.

  • Affirmative: I used to live in a small village.
  • Negative: She didn't use to like vegetables.
  • Question: Did you use to play the piano?

b) Be used to — accustomed/familiar with

Subject + am/is/are (+not) + used to + noun/-ing. Describes something normal/familiar for someone — not a past habit at all.

  • Affirmative: He is used to driving on the left.
  • Negative: They aren't used to the hot weather here.
  • Question: Are you used to eating spicy food?

c) When vs. while

  • When + past simple = a shorter or interrupting action.
  • While + -ing = a longer, background action happening at the same time as another.

Examples

See It in Action

  • I used to live in a small village. Now I live in a big city.
  • He is used to driving on the left — it's normal for him.
  • Did you use to play the piano as a child?
  • Are you used to eating spicy food?
  • When I arrived home, it started to rain.
  • I was cooking while my wife was watching TV.

Common Mistakes

Watch Out For

AvoidI used to living in a small village.

Use insteadI used to live in a small village.

"Used to" is followed by the infinitive (live), not the -ing form.

AvoidHe is used to drive on the left.

Use insteadHe is used to driving on the left.

"Be used to" is followed by a noun or the -ing form, not the infinitive.

AvoidI use to play soccer when I was young.

Use insteadI used to play soccer when I was young.

The affirmative form is "used to," not "use to" — "use to" only appears after "did/didn't."

AvoidWhile I arrived home, it started to rain.

Use insteadWhen I arrived home, it started to rain.

A short, sudden action (arriving) needs "when," not "while."

Summary

Review

  • Used to + infinitive = a finished past habit, not true now.
  • Be used to + noun/-ing = accustomed/familiar with something, in any tense.
  • When + past simple = short/interrupting; while + -ing = longer/background.
