Introduction

Beyond the four basic conditionals, C1 English uses mixed conditionals, conditional inversion, and alternative "if" structures to talk about hypothetical situations with more precision.

By the end of this lesson you can…

  • Understand and use: Mixed conditionals — combine a condition and result from different time frames.
  • Understand and use: Conditional inversion — in formal/written English, "if" can be dropped and the auxiliary moved before the subject.
  • Understand and use: Alternatives to "if" — "if it hadn't been for" / "but for" + noun phrase, express that something (a person, event, factor) prevented a different outcome.
  • Understand and use: Hypothetical structures — "suppose/supposing," "what if," "imagine" + past tense (present hypothetical) or past perfect (past hypothetical) function like conditionals in conversation.

Grammar Rules

The Rules

a) Mixed conditionals — combine a condition and result from different time frames.

  • Past condition → present result: If + past perfect, ... would + bare infinitive. If she had studied medicine, she would be a doctor now.
  • Present/general condition → past result: If + past simple, ... would have + past participle. If he weren't so stubborn, he wouldn't have lost his friends.

b) Conditional inversion — in formal/written English, "if" can be dropped and the auxiliary moved before the subject.

  • Had + subject + past participle (= if + past perfect): Had I known, I would have come earlier.
  • Were + subject (+ to + infinitive) (= if + past simple/were): Were she to accept, the deal would close tomorrow.
  • Should + subject + bare infinitive (= if + present, for a less likely future event): Should you need anything, just call me.

c) Alternatives to "if" — "if it hadn't been for" / "but for" + noun phrase, express that something (a person, event, factor) prevented a different outcome.

If it hadn't been for your help, I would have failed. = But for your help, I would have failed.

d) Hypothetical structures — "suppose/supposing," "what if," "imagine" + past tense (present hypothetical) or past perfect (past hypothetical) function like conditionals in conversation.

Suppose you lost your job tomorrow — what would you do?

Examples

See It in Action

  • If I had taken that job, I would be living in Tokyo now.
  • If he weren't so careless, he wouldn't have broken his arm.
  • Had she arrived earlier, she would have caught the train.
  • Were the company to collapse, thousands of jobs would be lost.
  • Should you have any questions, feel free to contact us.
  • If it hadn't been for the storm, the flight would have left on time.
  • But for his quick thinking, the situation could have been much worse.
  • Suppose you won the lottery — what would you do first?
  • If I were a bit more patient, I wouldn't have shouted at him.
  • Had the warning been taken seriously, the accident might have been avoided.
  • Were I in your position, I would resign immediately.
  • If she hadn't missed the meeting, she would know about the changes now.

Common Mistakes

Watch Out For

AvoidIf I would have known, I would have told you.

Use insteadIf I had known, I would have told you.

Don't use "would have" in the if-clause; use the past perfect ("had known").

AvoidHad I known it, I would tell you.

Use insteadHad I known it, I would have told you.

Inversion with "Had + subject + past participle" still needs "would have + past participle" in the result for a past outcome — match the time frames.

AvoidIf it wasn't for him, I would have failed.

Use insteadIf it hadn't been for him, I would have failed.

"If it hadn't been for" refers to something in the past that prevented a result; "wasn't" is present, which breaks the time logic.

AvoidWere she accept the offer, we would proceed.

Use insteadWere she to accept the offer, we would proceed.

This inversion pattern needs "to + infinitive" after the subject.

AvoidShould you will need help, call me.

Use insteadShould you need help, call me.

After "should" in this inversion, use the bare infinitive, not "will."
