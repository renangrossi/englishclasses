B2

Mixed Conditionals

Combine a past condition with a present result, or a present condition with a past result.

ObjectivesRulesExamplesCommon MistakesPracticeSummary

Introduction

Real regrets and hypotheticals often mix time frames — a past decision affecting the present, or a permanent truth affecting a past event. Mixed conditionals let you express that precisely.

By the end of this lesson you can…

  • Combine a third-conditional "if" clause with a second-conditional result
  • Combine a second-conditional "if" clause with a third-conditional result
  • Recognize why real life often needs this mix, not a "pure" conditional

Grammar Rules

The Rules

a) Past condition → present result

If + past perfect, would + base verb. A past action (or its opposite) explains a present situation.

  • If I had studied medicine, I would be a doctor now. (I didn't study medicine, so I'm not a doctor now)

b) Present condition → past result

If + past simple, would have + past participle. A permanent truth/characteristic explains a past event.

  • If she weren't so shy, she would have spoken up at the meeting. (she is shy — a permanent trait — and that's why she didn't speak up, in the past)

Examples

See It in Action

  • If I had studied medicine, I would be a doctor now.
  • If she weren't so shy, she would have spoken up at the meeting.
  • If he hadn't missed the flight, he would be here with us now.
  • If I were more organized, I wouldn't have forgotten the meeting.
  • If they had saved more money, they wouldn't be struggling now.
  • If you weren't so stubborn, you would have accepted their help.

Common Mistakes

Watch Out For

AvoidIf I had studied medicine, I would have been a doctor now.

Use insteadIf I had studied medicine, I would be a doctor now.

"Now" signals a present result — use "would be," not "would have been."

AvoidIf she wasn't so shy, she would have spoken up.

Use insteadIf she weren't so shy, she would have spoken up.

The second-conditional "if" clause uses "were," not "was," for all subjects.

AvoidIf he hadn't missed the flight, he would have been here now.

Use insteadIf he hadn't missed the flight, he would be here now.

A present result ("now") needs "would be," not "would have been."

Summary

Review

  • Past condition → present result: If + past perfect, would + base verb.
  • Present condition → past result: If + past simple, would have + past participle.
  • Look for a time-clue word like "now" to tell you the result is present, not past.
