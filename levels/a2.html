A2

Speaking

Five guided speaking tasks covering past experiences, future plans, comparisons and two short role-plays. Speak out loud — alone, with a partner, or with your instructor — then use the notes box to keep a short written record.

1. Talking About the Past

Answer each question out loud in full sentences, using the simple past.

  • What did you do last weekend?
  • Where did you go on your last vacation?
  • What was the last movie you watched? Did you like it?
  • What did you have for dinner yesterday?

2. Future Plans

Talk about your plans using “going to” for decisions already made, and “will” for quick decisions or predictions.

  • What are you going to do this weekend?
  • Where are you going to be in five years?
  • Do you think it will rain tomorrow?
  • What is one thing you will change about your routine next month?

3. Making Comparisons

Choose two cities, two jobs, or two friends, and compare them out loud using comparatives and superlatives.

  • Which city is bigger, your hometown or the capital of your country?
  • Who is more patient, you or a family member?
  • What is the most interesting place you have visited?
  • Is your current home cheaper or more expensive than your last one?

4. Role-Play: At a Clothes Shop

Practice this short conversation out loud with a partner (A = customer, B = shop assistant). Then swap roles and try it again with different items.

SpeakerLine
BHi! Can I help you with anything?
AYes, please. I'm looking for a jacket.
BSure. What size are you?
AMedium, I think. Do you have this one in blue?
BLet me check… Yes, we do. Would you like to try it on?
AYes, please. How much is it?
BIt's $45.

5. Role-Play: Asking for Directions

Practice asking for and giving simple directions. Use a real place near you if that helps.

SpeakerLine
AExcuse me, how do I get to the train station?
BGo straight, then turn left at the bank.
AIs it far from here?
BNo, it's about five minutes on foot.
AThank you very much!
BYou're welcome. Have a good trip!

Revision

A quick reference grid, the most common A2 mistakes, and two mixed-review exercises pulling together everything from this level.

Quick Reference: Key Structures

StructureFormExample
Comparatives / Superlatives-er/-est; more/mostShe is taller than me. He is the tallest in class.
Past Simpleverb + -ed / irregular formsI visited Paris. She went home early.
Past Continuouswas/were + verb-ingI was walking when it started to rain.
Quantifiersmany/a few + countable; much/a little + uncountableI have many books but little time.
First ConditionalIf + simple present, will + base verbIf it rains, I will stay home.
Question Tagspositive statement + negative tag (and vice versa)You like tea, don't you?
Can / Could / Mayability, possibility, permissionIt may rain later. Could you help me?
Relative Pronounswho (people), which (things), that (both)The man who called is my uncle.

Common Mistakes at A2

AvoidUse InsteadWhy
I speaked to him yesterday.I spoke to him yesterday.“Speak” is irregular: speak → spoke, not speaked.
She is more tall than me.She is taller than me.Short adjectives take -er, not “more.”
If it will rain, I will stay home.If it rains, I will stay home.The if-clause of a first conditional uses the simple present, never “will.”
You like tea, do you?You like tea, don't you?A positive statement takes a negative question tag.
How much people came?How many people came?“People” is countable, so it needs “many,” not “much.”
