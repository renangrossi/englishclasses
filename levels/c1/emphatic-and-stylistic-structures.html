Introduction

Cleft sentences split one idea into two clauses to put spotlight on a specific piece of information. They are a hallmark of fluent, natural-sounding advanced English.

By the end of this lesson you can…

  • Understand and use: It-clefts — "It is/was + the emphasized element + who/that + rest of the sentence." Used to highlight a subject, object, time or place.
  • Understand and use: Pseudo-clefts (wh-clefts) — start with a wh-word (what, where, why, how, the thing/reason/place that...) + be + the emphasized element. Used to highlight an action, reason, or entire idea, often at the end for maximum impact.

Grammar Rules

The Rules

a) It-clefts — "It is/was + the emphasized element + who/that + rest of the sentence." Used to highlight a subject, object, time or place.

Neutral: Sarah broke the vase.

Emphasized: It was Sarah who broke the vase. (emphasis on who did it)

It was the vase that Sarah broke. (emphasis on what was broken)

It was yesterday that Sarah broke the vase. (emphasis on when)

b) Pseudo-clefts (wh-clefts) — start with a wh-word (what, where, why, how, the thing/reason/place that...) + be + the emphasized element. Used to highlight an action, reason, or entire idea, often at the end for maximum impact.

What I found surprising was his reaction. (= His reaction was what I found surprising.)

What bothers me most is his constant lateness.

The thing that annoys me is his tone.

Where we went wrong was underestimating the competition.

c) All-cleft (a variation): All + relative clause + is/was + emphasized element, used to minimize/emphasize simplicity.

All I want is a bit of peace and quiet.

Use clefts to control which part of a sentence the listener/reader focuses on — a key stylistic tool in speeches, essays, and persuasive writing.

Examples

See It in Action

  • It was her honesty that impressed the interviewers most.
  • It is the lack of funding that's holding the project back.
  • What surprised me most was how calm she stayed.
  • What we need now is a clear plan of action.
  • It was in Lisbon that they first met.
  • The reason I called was to check on you.
  • What annoys me is when people interrupt.
  • It was not the money but the recognition that motivated him.
  • All she wants is a bit of appreciation for her work.
  • What made the difference was their attention to detail.
  • It was only after the accident that he changed his habits.
  • Where the plan failed was in its complete lack of funding.

Common Mistakes

Watch Out For

AvoidIt was Sarah that broke who the vase.

Use insteadIt was Sarah who broke the vase.

Choose either "who" (for people) or "that," and place it right after the emphasized element — don't split or double it.

AvoidWhat I found it surprising was his reaction.

Use insteadWhat I found surprising was his reaction.

Don't add a redundant object pronoun ("it") — the wh-clause already functions as the subject.

AvoidWhat bothers me most it's his constant lateness.

Use insteadWhat bothers me most is his constant lateness.

Use "is/was" directly, not a contracted "it's," which duplicates the subject.

AvoidIt was her the one who impressed the interviewers.

Use insteadIt was her/she who impressed the interviewers. / She was the one who impressed the interviewers.

Don't mix the it-cleft and the "the one who" structure in the same sentence — pick one pattern.

AvoidAll what I want is some peace and quiet.

Use insteadAll I want is some peace and quiet.

"All" already functions like "the only thing that," so don't add "what" as well.
