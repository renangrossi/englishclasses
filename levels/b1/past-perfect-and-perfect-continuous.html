B1

Past Perfect & Past Perfect Continuous

Add duration to your storytelling: how long something had been happening before another past event.

ObjectivesRulesExamplesCommon MistakesPracticeSummaryTest Yourself

Introduction

The past perfect continuous does for the past what the present perfect continuous does for the present — it adds a sense of ongoing duration before another past moment.

By the end of this lesson you can…

  • Review the past perfect (had + past participle)
  • Form and use the past perfect continuous (had been + -ing)
  • Choose between them based on result vs. duration, just like present perfect vs. continuous

Grammar Rules

The Rules

a) Past perfect (review)

had + past participle = the earlier of two past events, focused on the result.

  • She had finished the report before the meeting.

b) Past perfect continuous

had been + -ing = emphasizes the duration of an activity happening before another past moment.

  • She had been working on the report for hours before the meeting. (the activity, and how long)
  • He was tired because he had been running.

Examples

See It in Action

  • She had finished the report before the meeting.
  • She had been working on the report for hours before the meeting.
  • He was tired because he had been running.
  • They had been waiting for an hour when the bus finally came.
  • I had already eaten when they arrived.
  • We had been driving for six hours before we stopped.

Common Mistakes

Watch Out For

AvoidHe was tired because he had run for an hour. (emphasizing the ongoing activity)

Use insteadHe was tired because he had been running for an hour.

Emphasizing the duration/activity that caused the tiredness needs the past perfect continuous.

AvoidThey had been wait for an hour.

Use insteadThey had been waiting for an hour.

The continuous form needs the -ing verb, not the base form.

AvoidI had ate already when they arrived.

Use insteadI had already eaten when they arrived.

The past perfect needs the past participle (eaten), not the simple past form (ate).

Summary

Review

  • Past perfect (had + past participle) = result, the earlier of two past events.
  • Past perfect continuous (had been + -ing) = duration/activity before another past moment.
  • Same logic as present perfect vs. continuous, just shifted one step further into the past.
