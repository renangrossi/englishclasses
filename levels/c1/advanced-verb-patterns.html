Advanced Verb Patterns

Introduction

C1 English requires precise control of complex verb patterns that go beyond simple "verb + infinitive" or "verb + gerund" choices.

By the end of this lesson you can…

  • Understand and use: Verb + object + infinitive/gerund variations — some verbs change meaning depending on the pattern that follows.
  • Understand and use: Perfect infinitives and perfect gerunds — used to refer to an earlier action relative to the main verb.
  • Understand and use: Causatives — have/get + object + past participle, used when someone else performs an action for you (arranged by you).

Grammar Rules

The Rules

a) Verb + object + infinitive/gerund variations — some verbs change meaning depending on the pattern that follows.

  • remember/forget + to-infinitive (a task you need/needed to do) vs. + gerund (a memory of something already done) I remembered to lock the door. (I did it, then remembered.) / I remember locking the door. (I have a memory of doing it.)
  • stop + to-infinitive (pause in order to do something else) vs. + gerund (quit doing something) He stopped to smoke. (paused his activity in order to smoke) / He stopped smoking. (quit the habit)
  • try + to-infinitive (make an effort) vs. + gerund (experiment with something as a possible solution) I tried to open the jar. (made an effort) / I tried using hot water to open the jar. (experimented with a method)

b) Perfect infinitives and perfect gerunds — used to refer to an earlier action relative to the main verb.

  • Perfect infinitive: seem/appear/claim/pretend + to have + past participle He claims to have met the president. (= he claims that he met the president, earlier)
  • Perfect gerund: after prepositions or certain verbs, to emphasize completion before another point in time. She admitted having lied about her age.

c) Causatives — have/get + object + past participle, used when someone else performs an action for you (arranged by you).

have something done: I had my car repaired. (I arranged for someone to repair it.)

get something done: I got my hair cut. (more informal/dynamic version of "have")

Compare with "make/have + person + bare infinitive" (to force/arrange for a person to do something): The manager had the staff redo the report.

Examples

See It in Action

  • Did you remember to send the invoice?
  • I remember meeting him at a conference years ago.
  • She stopped to answer her phone in the middle of the meeting.
  • He finally stopped biting his nails after years of trying.
  • Try turning it off and on again — it usually fixes the problem.
  • He seems to have forgotten our appointment.
  • She claims to have finished the report already.
  • They admitted having made a mistake in the calculations.
  • I had my kitchen renovated last spring.
  • We got the documents translated before the meeting.
  • He had his assistant confirm all the bookings.
  • She pretended not to have noticed the comment.

Common Mistakes

Watch Out For

AvoidI remember to lock the door yesterday, so it's definitely locked.

Use insteadI remember locking the door yesterday, so it's definitely locked.

This describes a memory of a completed action, so it needs the gerund, not the to-infinitive.

AvoidHe stopped to smoke five years ago.

Use insteadHe stopped smoking five years ago.

"Stopped to smoke" would mean he paused another activity in order to smoke — the intended meaning here is quitting the habit, which needs the gerund.

AvoidHe seems to met her before.

Use insteadHe seems to have met her before.

To refer to an earlier event, the perfect infinitive "to have + past participle" is required.

AvoidI had my car to fix last week.

Use insteadI had my car fixed last week.

The causative "have something done" uses the past participle, not a to-infinitive.

AvoidShe admitted to lying to the police, before that day.

Use insteadShe admitted to having lied to the police before that day.

To show the lying happened earlier than the admission, the perfect gerund is needed after "admitted to."
