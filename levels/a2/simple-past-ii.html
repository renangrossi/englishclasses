A2

Simple Past II — Mixing With the Present

Practice choosing between the simple past and the simple present in the same conversation.

ObjectivesRulesExamplesCommon MistakesPracticeSummaryTest Yourself

Introduction

Real conversations mix tenses constantly — "He usually walks to work, but yesterday he took the bus." This lesson is focused practice on making that switch correctly, in both directions.

By the end of this lesson you can…

  • Choose correctly between the simple past and the simple present in mixed contexts
  • Recognize the specific time signal that tells you which tense to use
  • Get more practice with irregular verbs

Grammar Rules

The Rules

The key question: when does it happen?

  • A routine, true generally or right now → simple present (he walks to work every day)
  • A specific, finished moment in the past → simple past (yesterday he took the bus)

Watch for both signals in the same sentence: "Every day" and "yesterday" can appear together, each controlling a different verb.

Examples

See It in Action

  • Every day, he walks to work, but yesterday he took the bus.
  • She doesn't like coffee, but she enjoyed the tea this morning.
  • Did you finish your project last night?
  • Last week, we visited the museum, and it was very interesting.
  • He doesn't understand the lesson, so he asked for help.

Common Mistakes

Watch Out For

AvoidYesterday, I (not go) to the gym because I (feel) tired. → Yesterday, I not go to the gym because I feel tired.

Use insteadYesterday, I didn't go to the gym because I felt tired.

Both verbs describe yesterday, so both need the past — "didn't go" and "felt," not the present forms.

AvoidHe (not understand) the lesson, so he (ask) for help. → He not understand the lesson, so he ask for help.

Use insteadHe didn't understand the lesson, so he asked for help.

Without a routine signal word, and describing one specific past event, both verbs need the simple past.

AvoidEvery day, he (walk) to work, but yesterday he (take) the bus. → Every day, he walked to work, but yesterday he take the bus.

Use insteadEvery day, he walks to work, but yesterday he took the bus.

"Every day" needs the present (walks); "yesterday" needs the past (took) — read each time word carefully, one at a time.

Summary

Review

  • Read every time word in a sentence carefully — different clauses can need different tenses.
  • Routine/general → simple present. Specific, finished past moment → simple past.
  • The two tenses often appear together in the same sentence when contrasting a habit with one exception.
