This, That, These, Those

Point to things that are near or far, singular or plural.

ObjectivesExplanationRulesExamplesCommon MistakesPracticeSummaryRelated

Introduction

Demonstrative pronouns let you point at something without naming it, and they tell your listener both how close it is and how many there are.

By the end of this lesson you can…

  • Use “this/that” for singular things, near or far
  • Use “these/those” for plural things, near or far
  • Combine demonstratives with nouns to point things out clearly

Explanation

Explanation

This and these point to something near the speaker; that and those point to something farther away.

The singular/plural distinction matters too: this/that for one thing, these/those for more than one.

Grammar Rules

The Rules

  • This / These → near the speaker
  • That / Those → far from the speaker
  • This, that → singular
  • These, those → plural
Demonstratives by distance and number
SingularPlural
Nearthisthese
Farthatthose

Examples

See It in Action

  • This is my book (in my hand).
  • That is your car (across the street).
  • These are my shoes (on my feet).
  • Those are their bags (over there).
  • Is this seat free?

Common Mistakes

Watch Out For

AvoidThis are my keys.

Use insteadThese are my keys.

“Keys” is plural, so it needs “these,” not “this.”

AvoidThose is my friend.

Use insteadThat is my friend.

A single friend needs the singular form “that,” and the verb “is.”

AvoidI like this shoes.

Use insteadI like these shoes.

“Shoes” is plural, so the demonstrative must agree: these.

Tip

Match the demonstrative to the noun's number first (this/that = one, these/those = many), then to its distance.

Summary

Review

  • This/these = near; that/those = far.
