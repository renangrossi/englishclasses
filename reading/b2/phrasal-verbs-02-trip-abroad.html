Setting Off

A trip from the airport to New York to Washington, told almost entirely in phrasal verbs.

Play the audio and follow along, then read it again on your own. Tap or hover over a highlighted word to see what it means.

Anna and Mark had been planning their trip to the United States for months, and the day had finally come to set off. They had packed up their suitcases the night before and made sure to check in online for the flight, so that the morning would be one thing less to worry about.

At the airport they checked in their luggage and went through security without any trouble. That left a couple of hours before boarding, so they browsed around the duty-free shops. Anna found a perfume she liked and picked it up as a souvenir.

Once they had boarded, they settled in for the long flight. Mark pulled out his tablet to watch a film; Anna dozed off almost before the plane had levelled out. The flight was smooth, and they touched down in New York in the early afternoon.

After they got through customs they picked up their rental car and headed out to the hotel. They were keen to check out Times Square, so once they had freshened up they headed over to it on foot. The lights and the noise were overwhelming in the best way, and they stood there a while taking in the sights and sounds of the city.

The next day they drove down to Washington, D.C. They set out early to get ahead of the traffic and made good time on the road. When they arrived they checked into their new hotel and set off again to explore the National Mall, walking around the monuments and taking in the history behind each one.

Over the rest of the trip they ate out at one local restaurant after another, picked up a few more souvenirs, and met up with an old friend who had moved to the States some years earlier. When the time came to pack up and head back home they were sorry to go, but they had a great many photographs to look back on.

On the last morning they checked out of the hotel, dropped off the rental car and headed to the airport. As they boarded the flight home, they found themselves already looking forward to the next trip.

Key Vocabulary

  • to set off to begin a journey
  • to pack up to put your belongings into bags before leaving
  • to check in to register your arrival at an airport or hotel, or hand over your luggage
  • to go through to pass through a process, such as security or customs
  • to browse around to look at things without any intention of buying
  • to settle in to make yourself comfortable in a new place
  • to pull out to take something out of where it was stored
  • to doze off to fall lightly asleep without meaning to
  • to touch down to land, said of an aircraft
  • to get through to complete a process such as customs and come out the other side
  • to check out to go and see something; separately, to leave a hotel after paying
  • to freshen up to wash and tidy yourself after traveling
  • to head over to go across to a nearby place
  • to take in to look at something and absorb it
  • to set out to begin a journey, especially at a stated time
  • to make good time to travel faster than you had expected
  • to eat out to have a meal in a restaurant rather than at home
  • to meet up with to meet someone by arrangement
  • to head back to return to where you came from
  • to look back on to remember and reflect on something past
  • to drop off to take something or someone somewhere and leave it there
  • to look forward to to feel pleased about something that is going to happen

Speaking & Writing

Discussion

There are no right answers here — use these to practice speaking, or write a short answer to each.

  • Describe your last journey using at least eight phrasal verbs from this text.
  • Which is better on a short trip — planning every day in advance, as Anna and Mark did, or deciding as you go?
  • “Check out” means two quite different things in this text. Find another English verb that changes meaning with its particle, and explain both senses.
  • Is a souvenir worth buying? What do you actually keep from a trip?
  • Write about 150 words describing a journey you would like to make, from setting off to heading back.
