B1

Passive Voice

Focus on the action or the receiver, not on who did it.

ObjectivesRulesExamplesCommon MistakesPracticeSummaryTest Yourself

Introduction

In an active sentence, the subject does the action ("The chef cooked the meal"). In a passive sentence, the subject receives the action ("The meal was cooked"). English uses this a lot when the doer doesn't matter.

By the end of this lesson you can…

  • Form the passive voice (to be + past participle) in the present and past
  • Use the passive when the doer is unknown, unimportant, or obvious
  • Add "by" only when the doer is genuinely important information

Grammar Rules

The Rules

Form

to be (in the right tense) + past participle.

  • Present simple passive: The cake is baked every Sunday.
  • Past simple passive: The house was built in 1990.

When to use it

  • The doer is unknown: My phone was stolen. (we don't know who)
  • The doer is obvious/unimportant: The suspect was arrested. (obviously the police)
  • The action/result matters more than who did it: The bridge was built in 1930.

Add "by + doer" only if that information is genuinely useful: The painting was created by Picasso.

Examples

See It in Action

  • The cake is baked every Sunday by my mother.
  • The house was built in 1990.
  • The letters were sent yesterday.
  • The results will be announced tomorrow.
  • The song was sung by a famous artist.
  • My car is being repaired right now.

Common Mistakes

Watch Out For

AvoidThe cake is baking by my mother.

Use insteadThe cake is baked by my mother.

The passive needs "to be + past participle," not the -ing form (unless it's also continuous: "is being baked").

AvoidThe house builded in 1990.

Use insteadThe house was built in 1990.

The passive always needs "to be" before the past participle.

AvoidThe letters sent yesterday.

Use insteadThe letters were sent yesterday.

Without "were," this isn't a complete passive sentence.

AvoidThe book was written by somebody.

Use insteadThe book was written.

If the doer isn't known or important, just drop the "by" phrase entirely — don't force a vague "by somebody."

Summary

Review

  • Passive = to be + past participle, in whatever tense you need.
  • Use it when the doer is unknown, obvious, or less important than the action/result.
  • Only add "by + doer" when that information genuinely matters.
