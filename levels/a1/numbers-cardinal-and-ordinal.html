Numbers (Cardinal and Ordinal)

Count things and put them in order.

ObjectivesExplanationRulesExamplesCommon MistakesPracticeSummaryTest YourselfRelated

Introduction

English has two families of numbers: cardinal numbers for counting (one, two, three) and ordinal numbers for order or position (first, second, third).

By the end of this lesson you can…

  • Use cardinal numbers to count and give quantities
  • Use ordinal numbers to describe order, dates and floors
  • Spell and pronounce numbers 1–31 confidently

Explanation

Explanation

Cardinal numbers answer the question “how many?” — one, two, three, four... They're used for counting, prices, ages and phone numbers.

Ordinal numbers answer the question “in what order?” — first, second, third, fourth... Most ordinals are formed by adding -th to the cardinal number, but 1st, 2nd and 3rd are irregular.

Grammar Rules

The Rules

  • Cardinal numbers = how many (1, 2, 3…)
  • Ordinal numbers = order, position (1st, 2nd, 3rd…)
  • Most ordinals add -th: four → fourth, six → sixth
  • 1st (first), 2nd (second) and 3rd (third) are irregular
  • Ordinals ending in -y change to -ieth: twenty → twentieth
Cardinal vs. ordinal
CardinalOrdinal
onefirst (1st)
twosecond (2nd)
threethird (3rd)
fivefifth (5th)
nineninth (9th)
twelvetwelfth (12th)
twentytwentieth (20th)

Examples

See It in Action

  • I have three brothers.
  • This is my first trip to the United States.
  • She lives on the fifth floor.
  • Today is the twelfth of May.
  • He came in second place in the race.

Common Mistakes

Watch Out For

AvoidI am the two in the line.

Use insteadI am the second in the line.

Position needs the ordinal form (second), not the cardinal (two).

AvoidShe lives on the five floor.

Use insteadShe lives on the fifth floor.

Floors use ordinal numbers: fifth, not five.

AvoidMy birthday is on May two.

Use insteadMy birthday is on May second.

Dates in English are usually read with ordinal numbers.

Tip

Say dates and floors out loud with the ordinal ending (-th, -st, -nd, -rd) — it becomes automatic with practice.

Summary

Review

  • Cardinals count; ordinals order.
  • 1st, 2nd, 3rd are irregular — the rest mostly add -th.
  • Ordinals are used for dates, floors and rankings.
