Z is “zee” in American English and “zed” in British English. R is never rolled.

Spelling out loud

  • How do you spell your name? — L-U-C-A-S.
  • How do you spell “dog”? — D-O-G.
  • It’s spelled M-O-N-D-A-Y.
  • Can you spell that, please?

Double letters

  • book → B, double O, K
  • apple → A, double P, L, E
  • coffee → C, O, double F, double E
  • Anna → A, double N, A
Practice: say every letter out loud, every day. Listening to a recording helps more than reading any guide.

Introducing yourself

My name is…

  • My name is Sofia.
  • I’m Daniel. (I’m = I am)
  • Nice to meet you!
  • Nice to meet you, too!
  • How are you? — I’m fine, thank you. And you?
  • How are you doing? — Pretty good, thanks!

I Am · I Have · I Like · I Want

Four patterns that say a lot with very little grammar.

6
I+AM+adjective / noun
  • I am hungry.
  • I am a nurse.
  • I am from Chile.
  • I am 25 years old.
I+HAVE+noun
  • I have a bike.
  • I have three cousins.
  • I have an idea.
  • I have a small apartment.
I+LIKE+noun
  • I like tea.
  • I like football.
  • I like this movie.
  • I like rainy days.
I+WANT / NEED+noun
  • I want a sandwich, please.
  • I need a taxi.
  • I need more information.
  • I want a new phone.

Survival English

Signs you will see everywhere — and how to train your ear.

7

Signs

Read them without translating

STOP
Stop here

The way out

The way in

The shop is open
CLOSED
The shop is not open
PUSH / PULL
How to open the door
RESTROOM
Toilet / bathroom
NO SMOKING
You can’t smoke here
  • The door says PULL, so I pull it.
  • The pharmacy is CLOSED on Sundays.
  • Where is the EXIT?

Training your ear

  • Every day — five minutes is enough. Short and often beats long and rare.
  • Don’t chase every word — focus on the words you do recognize.
  • Listen again — the second and third time, you notice more.
  • Start small — a short video, a song, a podcast intro.
