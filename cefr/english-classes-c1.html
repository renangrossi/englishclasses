Advanced Passive Structures

Reporting beliefs, modal passives and the get-passive.

77

a) Reporting — without naming the source

It+is / was + said / believed / thought…+that…
  • It is believed that the fire started in the kitchen.
  • It was reported that talks had collapsed.
Subject+is / was + said…+to + infinitive
  • He is said to be abroad. same time
  • She is thought to have left in May. earlier
  • The files are believed to have been destroyed.

b) Modal passives

modal + be + participleAll bags must be checked.
modal + have been + participleThe road should have been repaired years ago.
Errors like this shouldn’t be tolerated.

c) Get-passive — informal, eventful

  • Our flight got cancelled at the last minute.
  • He got promoted after a year.
  • Their car got towed during the concert.

was cancelled = neutral fact · got cancelled = it happened to us

Register: reporting passives distance you from a claim (journalism, academic writing); modal passives express rules and expectations; the get-passive belongs to informal narration.

Complex Subordinate Clauses

Concession, purpose and result — linking ideas with precision.

78

a) Concession — contrast

even though · although · though + clauseEven though she was exhausted, she finished the race.
whereas · while + clauseHe loves cities, whereas she prefers the coast.
despite · in spite of + noun / -ingDespite having no experience, he got the job.

b) Purpose

so as to · in order to + infinitiveWe left early so as to avoid the traffic.
so that · in order that + clause (can / could / would)I wrote it down so that I wouldn’t forget.
so as not to + infinitiveShe whispered so as not to wake the baby.

c) Result

SO+adjective / adverb+THAT
  • The music was so loud that we couldn’t talk.
SUCH (a/an)+adj. + noun+THAT
  • It was such a strong argument that the jury changed its mind.

Advanced Conditionals

Mixed times, inversion, and alternatives to “if”.

79

a) Mixed conditionals

past condition → present resultIf I had taken that job, I would be in Tokyo now.
present condition → past resultIf he weren’t so careless, he wouldn’t have broken his arm.

b) Inversion — formal: drop “if”, move the auxiliary

If I had known…→Had I known, I would have come earlier.
If the company collapsed…→Were the company to collapse, jobs would be lost.
If you need anything…→Should you need anything, just call.

c) Alternatives to “if”

  • If it hadn’t been for your help, I would have failed.
  • But for his quick thinking, it could have been worse.

d) Hypothetical openers

  • Suppose you lost your job — what would you do?
  • What if we had missed the plane?
  • Imagine you could live anywhere.

Modality with Nuance

Deduce, criticize and regret — about the past.

80
modal+HAVE+participle

Deduction

must have
almost certain it happened
She must have forgotten — she’s never late.
can’t / couldn’t have
almost certain it didn’t
He can’t have finished already.
might / may / could have
possible
They may have taken another route.

Criticism

shouldn’t have
it happened, but was wrong
You shouldn’t have lied to them.
could have
it was possible — and better
You could have called me!

Missed chances

should have
the ideal didn’t happen
I should have saved more.
needn’t have
it was unnecessary
You needn’t have brought a gift.
Tone: should have = regret or gentle criticism · shouldn’t have = clear disapproval · must have = confident deduction · can’t have = confident denial.

Inversion for Emphasis

Negative and restrictive openers that flip the word order.

81
negative / restrictive adverbial+auxiliary+subject+verb
TriggerExample
Never · Rarely · SeldomSeldom have I felt so nervous.
Only after · Only when · Only thenOnly when I moved abroad did I appreciate home.
Not untilNot until midnight did the power come back.
No sooner … thanNo sooner had we sat down than the phone rang.
Hardly … whenHardly had I opened the door when the dog ran out.
Little (= not at all)Little did they know the storm was coming.
Under no circumstances · On no accountUnder no circumstances should you share this code.
Not only … but alsoNot only did he win, but he also broke the record.
Add do / does / did when there is no auxiliary. Formal, literary and dramatic — not casual speech.

Complex Noun Phrases

Pack a lot of information into a single noun phrase.

82

a) Multiple modifiers

  • a recently renovated, award-winning museum
  • a fast-growing, community-led project to cut plastic waste

b) Nominalization

  • They decided to cut costs. → Their decision to cut costs…
  • The firm failed suddenly. → The firm’s sudden failure…

-tion · -ment · -ness · -ity — compact, formal, objective

c) Expanded relative clauses

  • The report, which was released on Friday and which contradicts earlier data, sparked debate.

d) Reduced relative clauses

  • The scientist leading the study… active → -ing
  • Documents found at the scene… passive → participle

From several sentences to one

The committee was reluctant. It didn’t act. The reforms were delayed.

→ The committee’s reluctance to act delayed the reforms.

Reported Speech

Reporting verbs that capture the speaker’s intention.

83

a) Reporting verb patterns

PatternVerbsExample
verb + object + tourge · persuade · encourage · warnShe urged him to see a doctor.
verb + object + not towarn · tell · adviseHe warned us not to touch it.
verb + -ingdeny · admit · suggestHe denied taking the money.
verb + preposition + -inginsist on · apologize for · accuse ofShe insisted on paying.
verb + that + (should) + basesuggest · recommend · insistThe doctor recommended that she rest.
verb + topromise · refuse · offer · agreeShe promised to call back.

b) When NOT to backshift

  • General truths: He said that water boils at 100°C.
  • Still true now: She said she’s working in Berlin. she still is
  • would · could · might · should · ought to never change.
c) Report the meaning, not every word: “Sorry, sorry — I’ll never do it again!” → He apologized and promised not to do it again.

Advanced Verb Patterns

Infinitive or gerund — when the meaning changes.

84

a) Same verb, different meaning

Verb+ to-infinitive+ -ing
remembera task: I remembered to lock the door.a memory: I remember locking it.
forgetDon’t forget to call Mom.I’ll never forget meeting her.
stoppause to do: He stopped to answer the phone.quit: He stopped smoking.
trymake an effort: I tried to open the jar.experiment: Try using hot water.

b) Perfect infinitive & gerund

to have + participleHe claims to have met the president.
She seems to have forgotten.
having + participleThey admitted having made a mistake.

c) Causatives

have + thing + participleI had my car repaired.
get + thing + participleI got my hair cut.
have + person + base verbShe had her assistant confirm it.
make + person + base verbHe made us wait.

Future Forms with Nuance

The future seen from the past — for storytelling.

85

a) Future-in-the-past

FormNuanceExample
was / were going toplan — often unfulfilledShe was going to study abroad, but the grant fell through.
wouldwhat turned out to happenLittle did he know this stranger would become his best friend.
was / were about toon the brink — interruptedI was about to knock when the door swung open.
was / were toformal · fated or scheduledThe treaty was to shape the next century.

Promises and reports

  • He promised he would call as soon as he landed.
  • She said she would finish the project by Friday.
  • We were told the results were to be announced at noon.

b) A narrative timeline

By the time she boarded the plane, she had already decided (past perfect) she wasn’t coming back. Rain was falling (past continuous) as the plane took off (past simple). She was going to start a completely new life — a life that would surprise everyone.

Emphatic Structures

Clefts that control where the reader looks.

86

a) It-clefts

Neutral: Sofia broke the vase.

who?→It was Sofia who broke the vase.
what?→It was the vase that Sofia broke.
when?→It was yesterday that Sofia broke the vase.
contrast→It was not the money but the recognition that motivated him.

b) Pseudo-clefts (wh-clefts)

  • What surprised me most was her calm.
  • What we need now is a plan.
  • The reason I called was to check on you.
  • Where the plan failed was in its funding.

c) All-clefts

  • All I want is some peace and quiet.
  • All she asked for was a little respect.

Minimizes: “only this, nothing more”.
