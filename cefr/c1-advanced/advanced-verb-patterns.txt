LEVEL: C1
TOPIC: Advanced Verb Patterns
--------------------------------------------------

1. BRIEF EXPLANATION

C1 English requires precise control of complex verb patterns that go beyond simple "verb + infinitive" or "verb + gerund" choices.

a) Verb + object + infinitive/gerund variations — some verbs change meaning depending on the pattern that follows.
   - remember/forget + to-infinitive (a task you need/needed to do) vs. + gerund (a memory of something already done)
     I remembered to lock the door. (I did it, then remembered.) / I remember locking the door. (I have a memory of doing it.)
   - stop + to-infinitive (pause in order to do something else) vs. + gerund (quit doing something)
     He stopped to smoke. (paused his activity in order to smoke) / He stopped smoking. (quit the habit)
   - try + to-infinitive (make an effort) vs. + gerund (experiment with something as a possible solution)
     I tried to open the jar. (made an effort) / I tried using hot water to open the jar. (experimented with a method)

b) Perfect infinitives and perfect gerunds — used to refer to an earlier action relative to the main verb.
   - Perfect infinitive: seem/appear/claim/pretend + to have + past participle
     He claims to have met the president. (= he claims that he met the president, earlier)
   - Perfect gerund: after prepositions or certain verbs, to emphasize completion before another point in time.
     She admitted having lied about her age.

c) Causatives — have/get + object + past participle, used when someone else performs an action for you (arranged by you).
   have something done: I had my car repaired. (I arranged for someone to repair it.)
   get something done: I got my hair cut. (more informal/dynamic version of "have")
   Compare with "make/have + person + bare infinitive" (to force/arrange for a person to do something): The manager had the staff redo the report.

2. EXAMPLES

1. Did you remember to send the invoice?
2. I remember meeting him at a conference years ago.
3. She stopped to answer her phone in the middle of the meeting.
4. He finally stopped biting his nails after years of trying.
5. Try turning it off and on again — it usually fixes the problem.
6. He seems to have forgotten our appointment.
7. She claims to have finished the report already.
8. They admitted having made a mistake in the calculations.
9. I had my kitchen renovated last spring.
10. We got the documents translated before the meeting.
11. He had his assistant confirm all the bookings.
12. She pretended not to have noticed the comment.

3. COMMON MISTAKES

1. Incorrect: I remember to lock the door yesterday, so it's definitely locked.
   Correct: I remember locking the door yesterday, so it's definitely locked.
   Why: This describes a memory of a completed action, so it needs the gerund, not the to-infinitive.

2. Incorrect: He stopped to smoke five years ago.
   Correct: He stopped smoking five years ago.
   Why: "Stopped to smoke" would mean he paused another activity in order to smoke — the intended meaning here is quitting the habit, which needs the gerund.

3. Incorrect: He seems to met her before.
   Correct: He seems to have met her before.
   Why: To refer to an earlier event, the perfect infinitive "to have + past participle" is required.

4. Incorrect: I had my car to fix last week.
   Correct: I had my car fixed last week.
   Why: The causative "have something done" uses the past participle, not a to-infinitive.

5. Incorrect: She admitted to lying to the police, before that day.
   Correct: She admitted to having lied to the police before that day.
   Why: To show the lying happened earlier than the admission, the perfect gerund is needed after "admitted to."

4. EXERCISES

A. Gap-fill (choose infinitive or gerund, simple or perfect, as needed)
1. Please remember ___ (turn off) the lights before you leave.
2. I'll never forget ___ (see) the northern lights for the first time.
3. We stopped ___ (take) photos at the viewpoint before continuing the hike.
4. He finally stopped ___ (interrupt) people in meetings.
5. She claims ___ (invent) the recipe herself, years ago.
6. They seem ___ (know) each other already.
7. I had my suit ___ (tailor) for the wedding.
8. We got the roof ___ (repair) before winter.
9. He pretended ___ (not/notice) the mistake in the report.
10. Try ___ (restart) the router if the connection drops.

B. Rewrite / Transformation
1. Someone repaired my laptop for me last week. (rewrite with a causative) → I had ___
2. It's possible that he already left. He seems... → He seems ___
3. She confessed that she had broken the vase. → She admitted ___
4. Someone is cutting our lawn every Friday now. (rewrite with a causative) → We get ___
5. I have a clear memory of visiting that castle as a child. → I remember ___
6. He paused his work in order to answer the phone. → He stopped ___

C. Error Correction
1. I remember to visit Paris when I was ten.
2. She stopped answering the door — I think she's finally home again.
3. He claims meeting the queen once.
4. I had my hair to cut yesterday.
5. They admitted to lose the documents last year.
6. Try to turning the dial slowly.

D. Guided Production
1. Write one sentence with "remember" (gerund) about a childhood memory.
2. Write one sentence with a causative ("have/get something done") about a service you've arranged.
3. Write one sentence with "seems/claims to have + past participle."
4. Write one sentence contrasting "stop + to-infinitive" and "stop + gerund" (two clauses).
