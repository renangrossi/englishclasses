1. BRIEF EXPLANATION

Beyond the four basic conditionals, C1 English uses mixed conditionals, conditional inversion, and alternative "if" structures to talk about hypothetical situations with more precision.

a) Mixed conditionals — combine a condition and result from different time frames.
   - Past condition → present result: If + past perfect, ... would + bare infinitive.
     If she had studied medicine, she would be a doctor now.
   - Present/general condition → past result: If + past simple, ... would have + past participle.
     If he weren't so stubborn, he wouldn't have lost his friends.

b) Conditional inversion — in formal/written English, "if" can be dropped and the auxiliary moved before the subject.
   - Had + subject + past participle (= if + past perfect): Had I known, I would have come earlier.
   - Were + subject (+ to + infinitive) (= if + past simple/were): Were she to accept, the deal would close tomorrow.
   - Should + subject + bare infinitive (= if + present, for a less likely future event): Should you need anything, just call me.

c) Alternatives to "if" — "if it hadn't been for" / "but for" + noun phrase, express that something (a person, event, factor) prevented a different outcome.
   If it hadn't been for your help, I would have failed. = But for your help, I would have failed.

d) Hypothetical structures — "suppose/supposing," "what if," "imagine" + past tense (present hypothetical) or past perfect (past hypothetical) function like conditionals in conversation.
   Suppose you lost your job tomorrow — what would you do?

2. EXAMPLES

1. If I had taken that job, I would be living in Tokyo now.
2. If he weren't so careless, he wouldn't have broken his arm.
3. Had she arrived earlier, she would have caught the train.
4. Were the company to collapse, thousands of jobs would be lost.
5. Should you have any questions, feel free to contact us.
6. If it hadn't been for the storm, the flight would have left on time.
7. But for his quick thinking, the situation could have been much worse.
8. Suppose you won the lottery — what would you do first?
9. If I were a bit more patient, I wouldn't have shouted at him.
10. Had the warning been taken seriously, the accident might have been avoided.
11. Were I in your position, I would resign immediately.
12. If she hadn't missed the meeting, she would know about the changes now.

3. COMMON MISTAKES

1. Incorrect: If I would have known, I would have told you.
   Correct: If I had known, I would have told you.
   Why: Don't use "would have" in the if-clause; use the past perfect ("had known").

2. Incorrect: Had I known it, I would tell you.
   Correct: Had I known it, I would have told you.
   Why: Inversion with "Had + subject + past participle" still needs "would have + past participle" in the result for a past outcome — match the time frames.

3. Incorrect: If it wasn't for him, I would have failed.
   Correct: If it hadn't been for him, I would have failed.
   Why: "If it hadn't been for" refers to something in the past that prevented a result; "wasn't" is present, which breaks the time logic.

4. Incorrect: Were she accept the offer, we would proceed.
   Correct: Were she to accept the offer, we would proceed.
   Why: This inversion pattern needs "to + infinitive" after the subject.

5. Incorrect: Should you will need help, call me.
   Correct: Should you need help, call me.
   Why: After "should" in this inversion, use the bare infinitive, not "will."

4. EXERCISES

A. Gap-fill
1. If I ___ (study) harder at university, I would have a better job now.
2. ___ you need any assistance, don't hesitate to ask.
3. If it ___ (not/be) for her advice, I would have made a huge mistake.
4. ___ he arrived on time, he would have caught his connection.
5. Were I ___ (be) in charge, I would change the whole policy.
6. If she weren't so shy, she ___ (speak) up in the meeting yesterday.
7. But for the quick response of the paramedics, he ___ (not/survive).
8. Suppose you ___ (lose) your passport abroad — what would you do?
9. Had they listened to the experts, the crisis ___ (avoid).
10. If I ___ (not/be) allergic to cats, I would have one by now.

B. Rewrite / Transformation (use inversion or an alternative "if" structure)
1. If you need any help, just ask. → Should ___
2. If I had known about the traffic, I would have left earlier. → Had ___
3. If we were to lose this contract, the whole team would suffer. → Were ___
4. If it hadn't been for the rain, the match would have finished. → But for ___
5. If she hadn't warned us, we would have walked into the trap. → If it hadn't ___
6. If you were to ask him, he would probably say yes. → Were ___
7. If I had left earlier, I wouldn't be stuck in this traffic now. (identify: mixed or standard?) → rewrite keeping the same meaning with "Had..."

C. Error Correction
1. If I would have seen him, I would have said hello.
2. Had she study harder, she would have passed.
3. If it weren't for your support, I would have gave up.
4. Were he to knows the truth, he would be furious.
5. Should you needed anything, just let us know.
6. If I was you, I would have accepted the offer.

D. Guided Production
1. Write one mixed conditional sentence about a decision from your past that affects your present.
2. Write one sentence using conditional inversion with "Had."
3. Write one sentence using "but for" or "if it hadn't been for."
4. Write one sentence using "Suppose" or "What if" to pose a hypothetical question to a friend.
