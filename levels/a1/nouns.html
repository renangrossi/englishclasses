Nouns & Plurals

Name people, places and things — and make them plural correctly.

ObjectivesExplanationRulesExamplesCommon MistakesPracticeSummaryRelated

Introduction

Nouns name people, places, animals, things and ideas. In English, most nouns follow predictable rules to become plural, but a handful of very common ones are irregular.

By the end of this lesson you can…

  • Form regular plurals correctly
  • Apply spelling changes for nouns ending in -s, -sh, -ch, -x, -z and consonant+y
  • Recognize common irregular plurals

Explanation

Explanation

Most English nouns become plural by adding -s: cat → cats. Nouns ending in a hissing sound (-s, -sh, -ch, -x, -z) add -es instead, because “-s” alone would be hard to pronounce: bus → buses.

Nouns ending in a consonant + y change the y to -ies: lady → ladies. But if a vowel comes before the y, just add -s: boy → boys. A few nouns are simply irregular and must be memorized: child → children, man → men.

Grammar Rules

The Rules

  • Most nouns → add -s (cat → cats)
  • Nouns ending in -s, -sh, -ch, -x, -z → add -es (bus → buses)
  • Consonant + y → change y to -ies (lady → ladies)
  • Vowel + y → just add -s (boy → boys)
  • Some plurals are irregular: child → children, man → men, woman → women

Examples

See It in Action

  • I have two cats and one dog.
  • There are three buses at the station.
  • The ladies are talking in the garden.
  • My boys love football.
  • The children are playing outside.

Common Mistakes

Watch Out For

AvoidI have two childs.

Use insteadI have two children.

“Child” has an irregular plural: children, not childs.

AvoidThere are three bus.

Use insteadThere are three buses.

Words ending in -s add -es for the plural: buses.

AvoidThe ladys are here.

Use insteadThe ladies are here.

Consonant + y changes to -ies: lady → ladies.

Tip

When in doubt, say the plural out loud — if “-s” alone is hard to pronounce after the word, you probably need “-es.”
