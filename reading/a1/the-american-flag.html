The American Flag

Thirteen stripes, fifty stars, and one teenager who changed the design.

Play the audio and follow along, then read it again on your own. Tap or hover over a highlighted word to see what it means.

The American flag has three colors: red, white and blue.

It has thirteen stripes. Seven are red and six are white. The thirteen stripes are the first thirteen states.

In the corner there are fifty small white stars on blue. One star is one state. There are fifty states in the United States today.

The flag is not old in this form. The first flag with thirteen stars is from 1777. Then the country got bigger, and the flag changed many times.

When a new state arrived, the country put a new star on the flag. The stripes stayed at thirteen.

Alaska became a state in 1959. Hawaii became a state in 1959 too. So in 1960 the country needed a new flag with fifty stars.

A student from Ohio, Robert Heft, made a fifty-star design for a school project in 1958. His teacher gave him a B.

Then the government chose his design. The teacher changed the mark to an A.

Americans see this flag every day: in schools, in front of houses, at sports games. There is also a flag on the moon, from 1969.

People call the flag “the Stars and Stripes”. Now you know why.

Key Vocabulary

  • a flag a piece of cloth with colors or a design, for a country or a group
  • a stripe a long line of color
  • a star a shape with points, like a light in the night sky
  • a corner the place where two sides meet
  • a design the plan or the shape of something
  • a state one of the fifty parts of the United States
  • to change to become different, or to make something different
  • a mark a letter or a number for your work at school, like A or B
