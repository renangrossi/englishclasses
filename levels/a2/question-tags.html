Question Tags

Add a short question at the end of a sentence to check or confirm something.

ObjectivesRulesExamplesCommon MistakesPracticeSummaryTest Yourself

Introduction

Question tags are the short "...isn't it?" / "...don't you?" you add to the end of a sentence — very common in spoken English, to check information or invite agreement.

By the end of this lesson you can…

  • Form a negative tag after a positive statement
  • Form a positive tag after a negative statement
  • Handle the special cases: "I am" and "Let's"

Grammar Rules

The Rules

The basic rule

  • Positive statement → negative tag: You like it, don't you?
  • Negative statement → positive tag: You don't like it, do you?
  • The tag repeats the same auxiliary/modal verb as the main sentence (is → isn't, can → can't, will → won't).

Special cases

  • I am → aren't I? (not "amn't I")
  • Let's... → shall we?

Examples

See It in Action

  • You're coming to the party, aren't you?
  • She likes coffee, doesn't she?
  • They won't be late, will they?
  • We can start the meeting now, can't we?
  • It's a beautiful day, isn't it?
  • I'm right, aren't I?

Common Mistakes

Watch Out For

AvoidYou like it, do you?

Use insteadYou like it, don't you?

A positive statement needs a negative tag.

AvoidYou don't like it, don't you?

Use insteadYou don't like it, do you?

A negative statement needs a positive tag.

AvoidI am right, amn't I?

Use insteadI am right, aren't I?

"Amn't" isn't standard English — the special tag for "I am" is "aren't I?"

AvoidShe likes coffee, doesn't he?

Use insteadShe likes coffee, doesn't she?

The pronoun in the tag must match the subject of the sentence (she → she, not he).
