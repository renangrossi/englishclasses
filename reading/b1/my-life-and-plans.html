My Life and Plans

One student, six tenses — and the reason English needs all of them.

Every day I start the morning with a coffee. I love the smell of it more than the taste, honestly, and it makes me feel ready for whatever the day contains. That is the routine, and it has not changed in about four years.

Yesterday I tried a new place near the station. It was better than my usual one and slightly more expensive, which is how these things generally work. While I was sitting there I was reading a book about travelling in Japan, and I stayed about forty minutes longer than I had intended, because the chapter was good and nobody asked me to leave.

Right now I am working on a project for my course. My classmates and I are meeting every Wednesday to plan it, which sounds more organised than it is. We have finished about half of it already and it has gone better than any of us expected, mostly because we divided the work early instead of arguing about it for three weeks first.

Last month we presented an outline to our tutor. She had read it beforehand — which not all of them do — and she came with actual questions, two of which we could not answer. That was uncomfortable and it improved the project considerably.

I have been studying English for six years now, on and off. The “off” parts are the problem: I stopped twice for about a year each time, and both times I lost more than I expected and had to go back over old ground. Since January I have kept it going without a break, which is the longest I have managed.

Next summer I am going to travel, and this time it is arranged rather than imagined — the flights are booked. I will be somewhere in Southeast Asia for six weeks, and by the time I come back I will have finished my course. I am slightly afraid of the moment when both of those things are over at once and I have to decide what happens next.

But that is next year's problem. This evening I am meeting the group again, and we are going to argue about the final section, and somebody is going to suggest starting it from the beginning, and we are not going to do that.

Vocabulary

  • routine — the fixed pattern of what you usually do
  • to intend — to plan to do something
  • organised — arranged in a planned and orderly way
  • to divide — to share something out between people
  • outline — a short plan showing the main points of something
  • tutor — a teacher who supervises a student's work
  • beforehand — in advance of the event
  • uncomfortable — causing unease or embarrassment
  • considerably — by a large amount
  • on and off — with interruptions; not continuously
  • to go back over — to repeat work you have already done
  • old ground — material you have covered before
  • to keep something going — to continue something without letting it stop
  • arranged — fixed and agreed in advance
  • to suggest — to put an idea forward for consideration

Speaking & Writing

Discussion

There are no right answers here — use these to practice speaking, or write a short answer to each.

  • The writer says the breaks cost her more than she expected. Have you lost a skill by stopping, and how long did it take to recover?
  • Her group divided the work early rather than arguing first. Which approach do groups you have worked in actually take?
  • “That was uncomfortable and it improved the project considerably.” When has that been true for you?
  • Describe your own life in six sentences, using a different tense in each.
  • Write about 150 words about your plans for next year, distinguishing what is arranged from what you only intend.
