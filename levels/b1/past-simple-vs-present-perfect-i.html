B1

Past Simple vs. Present Perfect I

The single most common tense confusion at B1 — finished time vs. connected-to-now.

ObjectivesRulesExamplesCommon MistakesPracticeSummary

Introduction

This is the question that trips up almost every B1 learner: "visited" or "have visited"? The answer always depends on one thing: is the time finished, or still open/unspecified?

By the end of this lesson you can…

  • Recognize the specific time words that require the simple past
  • Recognize the words that signal the present perfect
  • Choose correctly between the two in everyday sentences

Grammar Rules

The Rules

Simple past — finished, specific time

Use it when the time is over and often stated: yesterday, last year, in 2020, two days ago, when I was a child.

  • I visited Paris last year.
  • They bought a new car yesterday.

Present perfect — unspecified or still-open time

Use it when you don't say exactly when, or the time period is still going: already, yet, ever, never, so far, this week/month/year (if not over).

  • She has finished her homework already.
  • They have eaten all the cake this week.

Examples

See It in Action

  • I visited Paris last year.
  • She has finished her homework already.
  • They bought a new car yesterday.
  • I have read this book before.
  • He lost his keys last night.
  • We have found your wallet.

Common Mistakes

Watch Out For

AvoidI have visited Paris last year.

Use insteadI visited Paris last year.

"Last year" is a specific, finished time — that always needs the simple past.

AvoidShe finished her homework already.

Use insteadShe has finished her homework already.

"Already" typically signals the present perfect, since no specific time is given.

AvoidI have read this book yesterday.

Use insteadI read this book yesterday.

"Yesterday" is a specific finished time — never combine it with the present perfect.

Summary

Review

  • Specific, finished time word (yesterday, last year, ___ ago) → simple past.
  • No specific time, or still-open period (already, yet, ever, never, this week) → present perfect.
  • Never combine a specific finished-time word with the present perfect.
