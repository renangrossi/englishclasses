At the Hotel

Booking a room by phone, and checking out when the bill has something on it you did not expect.

Play the audio and follow along, then read it again on your own. Tap or hover over a highlighted word to see what it means.

Two conversations at a hotel. The guest's lines are written out here as models. Read them, then cover them and try the dialogue yourself.

1. Booking a room

Receptionist: Good afternoon, the Lakeview Hotel. May I help you?

You: Good afternoon. I'd like to book a room, please.

Receptionist: Certainly. For which dates?

You: For the nights of June 3rd and 4th.

Receptionist: And how long will you be staying?

You: Two nights. I'll check out on the morning of the 5th.

Receptionist: What kind of room would you like?

You: A double room with a private bathroom, if you have one.

Receptionist: Let me check what's available… Yes, we have a room on the fourth floor with a view of the lake.

You: That sounds perfect. How much is it per night?

Receptionist: A hundred and forty dollars, including breakfast.

You: Does that include tax?

Receptionist: Tax is extra — it comes to about sixteen dollars a night.

You: All right, I'll take it. The name is Karen Jones.

Receptionist: Thank you, Ms. Jones. We'll see you on the third.

2. Checking out

Receptionist: Good morning. May I help you?

You: Good morning. I'd like to check out, please. Room 412.

Receptionist: One moment, please, ma'am… Here's your bill. Would you like to check that the amount is correct?

You: Thank you. Excuse me — what's this charge here, for eighteen dollars?

Receptionist: That's for the phone calls made from your room.

You: I see. I didn't realize the room phone was charged separately.

Receptionist: It is, I'm afraid. Would you like me to print the call log?

You: No, that's all right. And could I pay by card?

Receptionist: Certainly. May I see some ID?

You: Here's my passport.

Receptionist: Thank you. Could you sign here for me?

You: Of course. Could I also get a receipt for my company?

Receptionist: Here are your receipt and your card, ma'am. Thank you for staying with us.

You: Thank you — we had a very comfortable stay.

Key Vocabulary

  • to check in / to check out to arrive and register / to pay and leave
  • a double room a room with a bed for two people (compare: a single room)
  • per night for each night
  • a charge an amount of money added to your bill for something
  • a bill the paper showing what you owe (in restaurants, American English prefers 'the check')
  • a receipt the paper proving you paid
  • tax money added by the government; in US hotels it is usually not in the advertised price
  • I'm afraid… a polite way to introduce bad news

Speaking & Writing

Discussion

There are no right answers here — use these to practice speaking, or write a short answer to each.

  • Role-play dialogue 1: one of you is the receptionist, the other books a room for a specific weekend.
  • Have you ever found a charge on a bill you did not expect? What did you do?
  • In the US, hotel prices usually exclude tax, and restaurant prices exclude the tip. Is that the same where you live?
  • Write a dialogue (about 10 lines) in which you check into a hotel and ask two questions about the room.
