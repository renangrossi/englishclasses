Jack Works from Home

No commute, no boss in the room — and nobody to have lunch with.

Play the audio and follow along, then read it again on your own. Tap or hover over a highlighted word to see what it means.

My husband Jack works from home. He is a web designer. He makes websites for small companies.

He starts at nine. His desk is in the bedroom, next to the window, with a computer, a big screen, and a cup of coffee that is always cold.

In the morning he has meetings on the computer. He wears a good shirt and old pants, because people only see the top half.

He has no boss in the room, so nobody tells him what to do. But he has nobody to talk to either, and some days he does not leave the apartment.

At one o'clock he has lunch alone. Then he works until six.

“Do you like it?” I ask him.

“I like the work,” he says. “I don't like the quiet.”

On Wednesday he takes his computer to a café. It is noisy and the coffee is expensive, but there are people, and he comes home happier.

Key Vocabulary

  • website a place on the internet with pages you can read
  • desk a table for working or writing
  • screen the flat part of a computer you look at
  • meeting a time when people talk about work together
  • to wear to have clothes on your body
  • pants the clothes you wear on your legs — American; British say trousers
  • top half the part of you above the middle
  • boss the person who tells you what to do at work
  • alone with nobody else
  • quiet with little or no noise
  • noisy with a lot of noise
  • happier more happy than before
