Snowy Days

An honest account of winter, from someone who actually lives in it.

Play the audio and follow along, then read it again on your own. Tap or hover over a highlighted word to see what it means.

Snowy days sound a lot better in theory than they actually are. People who don't live in cold places love to romanticize snow, like it's magical or cozy all the time, but if you deal with it every winter, you know that isn't the full story. Snow does look beautiful when it's fresh and untouched, especially early in the morning when everything is quiet and white. For about five minutes it feels calm, almost peaceful, like the world hit pause. Then you remember you still have responsibilities, and that peaceful feeling disappears.

One of the worst parts is how much extra effort snow adds to your day. Nothing is simple anymore. You can't just leave the house — you have to bundle up, find your gloves, hope you didn't lose your hat, and spend forever scraping ice off your car. Your fingers go numb even through gloves, and you're already annoyed before the day has started. Driving is stressful, walking is dangerous, and the bus is usually late. Everyone is in a bad mood, even the people pretending they aren't.

That said, snowy days do come with a certain feeling. There's less pressure to be social or productive. If plans get canceled because of the weather, nobody questions it. You stay inside without feeling lazy, wear the same sweatpants all day, and live off coffee, soup, and whatever comfort food is in the house. You scroll on your phone, watch shows you've already seen three times, and lose track of the afternoon. Snow gives people permission to slow down, and honestly, that's fairly rare in American life.

But after a while it gets old. The snow stops being white and turns into gray slush along the side of the road. Your shoes are always wet, your jeans get dirty around the ankles, and everything feels colder than it should. By the middle of winter most people are completely over it. You wake up, check the weather app like it personally owes you good news, and feel let down every time you see more snow in the forecast.

Snowy days can feel isolating, too. The streets are empty, people stay inside, and the days are short and dark. It's cozy at first, but after weeks of it, cabin fever sets in. You miss sunlight and fresh air and being able to walk outside without thinking about it first. People start talking about spring like it's a distant dream, counting down the days until they can open their windows again.

In the end, snowy days are a mix of nice moments and constant frustration. You complain about them the entire time, and then, when winter is finally over, you strangely miss parts of it. Not the cold or the stress — but that quiet feeling, the slower pace, and the excuse to stop rushing for once.

Key Vocabulary

  • to romanticize to imagine something as better or more beautiful than it really is
  • to bundle up to put on a lot of warm clothes
  • to scrape to remove something by rubbing a hard edge against a surface
  • numb unable to feel anything, usually from cold
  • sweatpants loose, soft trousers worn for comfort or exercise
  • comfort food simple, familiar food eaten to feel better
  • to lose track of to stop noticing how much time is passing
  • slush snow that has partly melted into dirty, wet mush
  • to be over something to have had enough of it and stopped caring
  • to let someone down to disappoint them
  • isolating making you feel cut off from other people
  • cabin fever the restlessness that comes from being stuck indoors too long
