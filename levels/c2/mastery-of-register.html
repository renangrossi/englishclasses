Introduction

Register is the level of formality and the specific conventions of grammar and vocabulary appropriate to a context. C2 speakers can recast the same content across academic, legal, journalistic, casual and technical styles at will.

By the end of this lesson you can…

  • Understand and use: Academic register — nominalization, passive voice, hedging modals, precise connectors, avoidance of contractions and first person where possible.
  • Understand and use: Legal register — highly formal, precise, often uses archaic connectors (hereinafter, notwithstanding, pursuant to, shall for obligation), long compound sentences, and avoids ambiguity through repetition of exact terms rather than pronouns.
  • Understand and use: Journalistic register — concise, front-loaded with the most important information (lead paragraph), attributed claims ("according to," "sources say"), active voice for readability, short paragraphs.
  • Understand and use: Casual/conversational register — contractions, phrasal verbs, ellipsis, informal connectors (so, anyway, kind of), direct address, shorter sentences.
  • Understand and use: Technical register — precise domain-specific terminology, imperative mood for instructions, passive voice for procedures, minimal hedging.

Grammar Rules

The Rules

a) Academic register — nominalization, passive voice, hedging modals, precise connectors, avoidance of contractions and first person where possible.

Casual: We found that people work better in teams.

Academic: The findings indicate that collaborative work environments enhance productivity.

b) Legal register — highly formal, precise, often uses archaic connectors (hereinafter, notwithstanding, pursuant to, shall for obligation), long compound sentences, and avoids ambiguity through repetition of exact terms rather than pronouns.

The Tenant shall, pursuant to Clause 4 of this Agreement, provide written notice no fewer than thirty (30) days prior to termination.

c) Journalistic register — concise, front-loaded with the most important information (lead paragraph), attributed claims ("according to," "sources say"), active voice for readability, short paragraphs.

Officials confirmed on Tuesday that the bridge would remain closed for repairs, according to a statement released by the transport authority.

d) Casual/conversational register — contractions, phrasal verbs, ellipsis, informal connectors (so, anyway, kind of), direct address, shorter sentences.

So basically, they're closing the bridge for repairs — no idea for how long, though.

e) Technical register — precise domain-specific terminology, imperative mood for instructions, passive voice for procedures, minimal hedging.

Insert the cable into port A before powering on the device. Do not exceed the rated voltage.

Recasting the same information across these registers means adjusting: sentence length and complexity, verb voice (active/passive), vocabulary formality, use of contractions/pronouns, and how directly claims are attributed or hedged.

Examples

See It in Action

  • Academic: The data suggest a strong correlation between sleep quality and cognitive performance.
  • Legal: The Purchaser shall remit payment in full within thirty (30) days of the invoice date.
  • Journalistic: Sales fell sharply in the third quarter, the company revealed on Monday.
  • Casual: Sales basically tanked last quarter — nobody really knows why yet.
  • Technical: Ensure the device is disconnected from the power supply before servicing.
  • Academic: It could be argued that the policy failed to address the underlying causes of inequality.
  • Legal: Notwithstanding the foregoing, either party may terminate this Agreement upon written notice.
  • Journalistic: Residents were left without power for several hours after Tuesday's storm, officials said.
  • Casual: We were literally stuck without power for hours after that storm — it was chaos.
  • Technical: Do not operate the equipment if the safety guard is missing or damaged.

Common Mistakes

Watch Out For

Avoid[academic] I think the results are pretty surprising.

Use insteadThe results are somewhat unexpected. / It could be argued that the results are surprising.

Academic register avoids first-person opinion markers ("I think") and casual intensifiers ("pretty") — use hedged, impersonal phrasing instead.

Avoid[legal] The company will has to pay the fine.

Use insteadThe Company shall be liable to pay the fine.

Legal register uses "shall" for binding obligation, formal capitalized defined terms (the Company), and correct modal grammar.

Avoid[journalistic] It is reported by some sources, allegedly, that the mayor did resign, according to reports.

Use insteadThe mayor resigned on Tuesday, according to sources close to city hall.

Journalistic style avoids redundant hedging/attribution stacked together — attribute once, clearly and concisely.

Avoid[technical] You should maybe try disconnecting the cable if it feels warm.

Use insteadDisconnect the cable immediately if it becomes warm to the touch.

Technical/instructional register uses direct imperatives, not hedged suggestions.

Avoid[casual] I would like to inform you that I am unable to attend due to a prior commitment.

Use instead[casual] Sorry, can't make it — something else came up.

This overly formal phrasing is mismatched with a casual context; casual register favors contractions and short, direct phrasing.
