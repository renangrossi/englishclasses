B1

Reported Speech — Statements & Basic Backshift

Report what someone said, without quoting their exact words.

ObjectivesRulesExamplesCommon MistakesPracticeSummary

Introduction

When you tell someone what another person said, you usually don't repeat their exact words — you report the idea, from your own point of view, later in time. This changes the verb tense and a few other words.

By the end of this lesson you can…

  • Use say and tell correctly (tell always needs a listener)
  • Backshift the verb tense one step into the past when reporting
  • Change time and place words (today, here, tomorrow) to fit the new point of view
  • Report simple statements without needing every reporting-verb pattern yet

Grammar Rules

The Rules

a) Say vs. tell

say (no listener needed, or use "to" + listener) and tell (always needs a listener, no "to")

  • She said, "I'm tired." → She said (that) she was tired.
  • She said to me, "I'm tired." → She told me (that) she was tired.
  • Never say “She said me” — use “told me” or “said to me.”

b) Backshifting the verb

When the reporting verb (said, told) is in the past, the reported verb usually moves one step further into the past too:

  • Present simple → past simple: "I work here." → He said he worked there.
  • Present continuous → past continuous: "I'm working." → He said he was working.
  • Past simple → past perfect: "I finished." → He said he had finished.
  • Present perfect → past perfect: "I have finished." → He said he had finished.
  • can → could, will → would: "I can help." → He said he could help.

c) Changing time, place and pronouns

Words that point to "now" or "here" from the original speaker's moment usually change too, since you're reporting later, from a different point of view:

  • today → that day  |  tomorrow → the next day / the following day  |  yesterday → the day before
  • here → there  |  now → then
  • this → that  |  my/I → his/her/etc. (the pronoun changes to match who is being reported)

Examples

See It in Action

  • "I'm hungry," she said. → She said (that) she was hungry.
  • "We live in Porto Alegre," they said. → They said (that) they lived in Porto Alegre.
  • "I saw him yesterday," he told me. → He told me (that) he had seen him the day before.
  • "I'll call you tomorrow," she said. → She said (that) she would call me the next day.
  • "I can't come today," he said. → He said (that) he couldn't come that day.
  • "I have already finished," she told us. → She told us (that) she had already finished.
  • "I'm studying here," he said. → He said (that) he was studying there.
  • "This is my book," she said. → She said (that) that was her book.

Common Mistakes

Watch Out For

AvoidShe said me she was tired.

Use insteadShe told me she was tired. / She said (to me) she was tired.

"Say" never takes a direct listener without "to" — use "tell" for that, or add "to" before the listener.

AvoidHe said he works there.

Use insteadHe said he worked there.

With a past reporting verb (said), the present tense usually backshifts to the past.

AvoidShe said she will call tomorrow.

Use insteadShe said she would call the next day.

"Will" backshifts to "would," and "tomorrow" (from her original moment) becomes "the next day" from your later point of view.

AvoidHe told that he was busy.

Use insteadHe told me (that) he was busy. / He said (that) he was busy.

"Tell" needs a listener (told me, told her); without one, use "said."

AvoidShe said she is here.

Use insteadShe said she was there.

"Here" (her location, at the time) becomes "there" from your point of view, and "is" backshifts to "was."

Summary

Review

  • Say has no listener (or “to” + listener); tell always needs a listener, with no “to.”
  • After a past reporting verb, tenses backshift one step into the past (am → was, finished → had finished, will → would).
  • Time/place words shift point of view: today → that day, tomorrow → the next day, here → there.
  • This is the foundation for more complex reported speech (questions, commands, and reporting-verb patterns like deny/urge/warn) taught later.
