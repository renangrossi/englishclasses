Your First Week on Reception

A manager explains the job to a new employee — and the four words for what is included in the price.

Play the audio and follow along, then read it again on your own. Tap or hover over a highlighted word to see what it means.

Manager: So you'll be working here at reception, by the entrance. Your main job is talking to guests and sorting out whatever they need. They're paying to be here, so whatever else happens, you stay polite.

New Employee: Understood. So when someone arrives and wants to check in, I take their details and give them the key?

Manager: Almost. First you check whether they have a reservation. Most people book online or by phone before they arrive, so the name will already be in the system. Some turn up without booking — we call those walk-ins. If we're fully booked, you apologize and say we've nothing available. If there is a room, take their details and check them in as normal.

New Employee: And what do I actually need from them?

Manager: Name, address, a card for the deposit, and photo ID. Check the ID against the booking name. Then the key.

New Employee: Does the price include meals?

Manager: That depends what they've booked, and this is the part people get wrong most often, so learn it properly. There are four options. Full board means breakfast, lunch and dinner are all included. Half board is breakfast and dinner, but not lunch. Bed and breakfast is breakfast only. And room only means exactly that — no meals at all, and if they want breakfast they pay for it on the morning.

New Employee: So room only is the same as self-catering?

Manager: No, and don't say that to a guest. Self-catering means the room has a kitchen and they cook for themselves. None of our rooms have kitchens, so we don't offer it. Room only just means we're selling you a bed and nothing else.

New Employee: Got it. What if someone arrives and the room isn't ready?

Manager: It happens most days, because check-in is at three and people land in the morning. Apologize, and offer them the lounge — that's the seating area just past reception, with the sofas. They can wait there, have a coffee, read something.

New Employee: And if they'd rather go out?

Manager: Then offer to store their luggage. We keep it in the room behind the desk, you give them a numbered tag, and they collect it when they come back. Always offer that — people don't like to ask, and nobody wants to walk around a strange city with a suitcase.

New Employee: What about when they leave?

Manager: Check-out is eleven. They bring the key down, you settle the bill — anything they've charged to the room, minibar, restaurant, phone — and you take payment. Ask whether everything was all right with the stay. If it wasn't, you want to hear it here rather than read it online a week later.

New Employee: And if they want to stay later than eleven?

Manager: Late check-out. We can usually do an hour or two free if we're not full, but check the system first, because housekeeping need the room. If we're busy, tell them the luggage store is available and they can use the lounge until their flight.

New Employee: One more. What if I don't know the answer to something?

Manager: Then you say you'll find out, and you find out. Don't guess. Guessing is how a guest ends up at a restaurant that closed two years ago.

Key Vocabulary

  • walk-in a guest who arrives without having booked
  • fully booked with no rooms left to sell
  • to check in to register on arrival and be given a room
  • deposit money held in advance against possible charges
  • photo ID official identification carrying the holder's photograph
  • full board a rate including breakfast, lunch and dinner
  • half board a rate including breakfast and dinner, but not lunch
  • bed and breakfast a rate including breakfast only
  • room only a rate including no meals at all
  • self-catering accommodation with a kitchen, where guests cook for themselves
  • lounge a comfortable seating area where guests can wait or relax
  • to store to keep something safely until it is wanted
  • tag a label attached to something to identify it
  • check-out the time by which guests must leave, and the act of leaving and paying
  • to settle the bill to total up what is owed and take payment
  • minibar the small fridge of drinks in a hotel room, charged to the bill
  • housekeeping the staff who clean and prepare the rooms

Speaking & Writing

Discussion

There are no right answers here — use these to practice speaking, or write a short answer to each.

  • The manager says never to guess. Why is a confident wrong answer worse than saying you do not know?
  • Guests are reluctant to ask for luggage storage, so staff must offer it. What else do customers not like to ask for?
  • Is it better to hear a complaint at the desk or to read it in a review? What does each one cost the business?
  • Work with a partner: one of you is a walk-in guest at a hotel that has one room left, on room-only. Handle the conversation.
  • Write about 120 words explaining a job you know to someone starting it tomorrow. What would you tell them first?
