Greetings & Introductions

Name, origin and age — and the polite formulas around them.

8

Greetings

TimeSay
Before 12pmGood morning
12pm – 6pmGood afternoon
After 6pmGood evening
Any timeHello · Hi
Goodbye at nightGood night

Fixed formulas

How are you?Fine, thanks. And you?
Nice to meet you.Nice to meet you, too.
Where are you from?I’m from Canada.
How old are you?I’m 32 years old.
What’s your name?My name is Priya.

Introduce yourself

  • Good afternoon! My name is Priya.
  • I am from India.
  • I am 32 years old.
  • I’m a teacher.
  • I live in Toronto.
  • Nice to meet you, Tom.
“How are you?” is a polite formula, not a real health question. “Fine, thanks” is the normal answer — even on an ordinary day.

Numbers

Cardinal numbers count. Ordinal numbers put things in order.

10

How many?

Cardinal

  • I have two sisters.
  • The ticket costs forty-five dollars.
  • She is nineteen.
  • My number is five-five-two, eight-one-oh.

In what order?

Ordinal

  • It’s my first visit to Japan.
  • We live on the fourth floor.
  • She finished third in the race.
  • Today is the twenty-first of March.
CardinalOrdinalCardinalOrdinal
onefirst · 1steighteighth · 8th
twosecond · 2ndnineninth · 9th
threethird · 3rdtententh · 10th
fourfourth · 4theleveneleventh · 11th
fivefifth · 5thtwelvetwelfth · 12th
sixsixth · 6thtwentytwentieth · 20th
sevenseventh · 7ththirty-onethirty-first · 31st

The rules

  • Most ordinals add -th: six → sixth, ten → tenth.
  • 1st, 2nd, 3rd are irregular: first, second, third (also 21st, 22nd, 23rd…).
  • Watch the spelling: five → fifth, nine → ninth, twelve → twelfth.
  • Tens ending in -y change to -ieth: twenty → twentieth, forty → fortieth.
  • Use ordinals for dates, floors and rankings.

Who does the action — and who receives it.

Action

The subject does the action. The object receives it.

Person+Verb+Person

Simple Present

Negative & questions — do and does do the work.

13

Negative

the main verb goes back to its base form
IDO NOTDON’T
  • drink tea.
  • clean the kitchen.
  • play the guitar.
  • read the news.
he, she, itDOES NOTDOESN’T
  • drink tea.
  • clean the kitchen.
  • play the guitar.
  • teach math.
  • do yoga.
  • have two dogs.
we, you, theyDO NOTDON’T

  • sleep late.
  • speak French.
  • bake bread.

Interrogative

DO / DOES + subject + base verb?
DOI
  • drink tea?
  • clean the kitchen?
  • play the guitar?
  • read the news?
DOEShe, she, it
  • drink tea?
  • clean the kitchen?
  • play the guitar?
  • teach math?
  • do yoga?
  • have two dogs?
DOwe, you, they

Generic

A / An singular only

Specific

The singular & plural

the schoolthe schools

one · near

That

one · far

These

many · near

Possessives

Who owns what: my / mine, your / yours… and ’s.

17

Complete review

SubjectObjectPossessive + nounPossessive alone
Imemy bookit’s mine
youyouyour bookit’s yours
hehimhis bookit’s his
sheherher bookit’s hers
ititits tail—
weusour bookit’s ours
theythemtheir bookit’s theirs

Whose?

  • The bike is Maria’s.
  • This is my phone. The phone is mine.
  • He is her brother.
  • The dog is hers.
  • This is our garden. It is ours.
  • The decision is theirs.

In conversation

  • “Is this jacket yours?” — “No, mine is black.”
  • “Can I use your pen? I forgot mine.”
  • “These seats are ours, not theirs.”
Noun after it? Use my / your / her. No noun? Use mine / yours / hers. And its (possessive) has no apostrophe — it’s means “it is”.

Prepositions

In, on, at for place and time — plus to and for.

20

IN

  • Inside — in the box, in Brazil
  • Months, years, seasons — in July, in 2026, in winter
  • in the morning / afternoon / evening

ON

  • Surface — on the table, on the wall
  • Days — on Friday
  • Dates — on March 3rd
  • Floors — on the 2nd floor

AT

  • A point — at the door, at the station
  • Clock time — at 8 o’clock, at noon
  • Holidays — at Easter
  • at night

At a glance

PlaceTime
inin the kitchen, in Parisin May, in 1999, in the evening
onon the sofa, on the 5th flooron Tuesday, on June 10th
atat home, at the bus stopat 7pm, at night, at Christmas

TO

= movement → · verbs (to have, to do, to be…)
  • She wants to learn Spanish.
  • I sent a letter to my grandmother.
  • They walk to school every day.

FOR

= a present · substitution
  • These flowers are for you.
  • Can you answer the phone for me?
  • He pays the bill for his friends.

And · But · Or

Add, contrast or choose.

21

AND

addition
  • She is smart and kind.
  • I bought bread and cheese.
  • We cooked and cleaned.

BUT

contrast
  • It’s cheap, but it’s good.
  • I called, but nobody answered.
  • He’s tired, but happy.

OR

choice
  • Tea or coffee?
  • We can walk or take a taxi.
  • Is it Monday or Tuesday?

Ask yourself

adding?→ANDcontrasting?→BUTchoosing?→OR
Put a comma before “but” when it joins two complete sentences: I studied a lot, but the test was hard.

Present Continuous

Right now, temporary actions — and future plans. It depends on the context.

25

Affirmative

am / is / are + verb-ing
IAMcooking dinnerright now. he, she, itISlearning Italianthis year. we, you, theyAREmoving housenext week.

Negative

am / is / are + not + -ing
  • I’m not sleeping, I’m thinking.
  • She isn’t working today.
  • They aren’t coming tonight.

Interrogative

am / is / are + subject + -ing?
  • Are you listening?
  • What is he watching?
  • Where are they going?

Spelling of -ing

most verbsread → reading
silent -emake → making, write → writing
short vowel + consonantrun → running, sit → sitting
-ielie → lying, die → dying

Simple present or continuous?

  • I work in a bank. routine
  • I’m working from home this week. temporary
  • Look! It’s snowing. right now
  • We’re flying to Lima on Friday. plan

Imperatives

Orders, instructions and requests — no subject needed.

26
VERB
DON’T+VERB
No subject, no to, no -s. Add please to sound polite.

Examples

  • Close the window, please.
  • Don’t forget your umbrella!
  • Turn left at the traffic lights.
  • Don’t feed the animals.
  • Call me when you arrive.
  • Please sit down and relax.

Attention

Don’t open the box!
an order
≠
I’m telling you not to open the box.
reporting the order

Review — a real scene mixes forms

  • Every morning, Leo drinks coffee and reads the news. present simple · routine
  • Today he is packing his suitcase. present continuous · now
  • He is flying to Madrid tonight. present continuous · plan
  • “Don’t forget your passport!” his sister shouts. imperative · instruction
  • “Relax, I have it!” he answers.
Time clues decide the form: every day / usually → simple present; now / look! / tonight → present continuous; direct instructions → imperative.
