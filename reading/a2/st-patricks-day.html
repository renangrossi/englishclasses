The Day Erica Forgot to Wear Green

A short St. Patrick's Day story — and the American custom behind it.

In the United States, March 17th is St. Patrick's Day. It began as an Irish holiday, and many Americans now celebrate it too — with parades, green decorations and, in a few cities, a river dyed bright green for the day. There is also a custom in American schools: if you forget to wear something green, your friends are allowed to pinch you.

"Oh no!" Erica said when she walked into school. Everyone was wearing green. Everyone except her. She had forgotten what day it was.

"No green? You get a pinch," said her best friend Sarah, and she walked over and pinched her arm.

"Ouch!" Erica cried. She knew it was going to be a long day if she did not find something green fast.

She went outside to look for a shamrock — a small green plant with three leaves — but she could not find one anywhere. She sat down on the steps and started to cry.

Suddenly a leprechaun appeared in front of her. In Irish stories, a leprechaun is a tiny man who guards a pot of gold.

"What's the matter, lass?" he said.

"Who are you?" Erica asked.

"I'm a leprechaun, and I'm here to cheer you up on St. Paddy's Day," he replied.

"I forgot to wear green," Erica said. "If I go back inside, everyone will pinch me all day long."

"No worries," the leprechaun said. "I'll find you some green."

A pot of gold appeared beside him. He took a handful of gold and threw it into the air. Then he took off his hat and pulled out of it the most perfect shamrock Erica had ever seen.

"Here you go," he said.

Erica thanked him, pinned the shamrock to her shirt, and walked back into school to enjoy the rest of her day — pinch-free.

Vocabulary

  • to pinch — to squeeze someone's skin between your finger and thumb
  • a shamrock — a small green plant with three leaves, a symbol of Ireland
  • a leprechaun — in Irish stories, a tiny man who guards a pot of gold
  • a parade — a public celebration where people walk through the streets
  • a custom — something people in a place usually do
  • to cheer someone up — to make a sad person feel happier
  • What's the matter? — a friendly way to ask why someone is upset
  • No worries. — a common informal way to say "that's not a problem"

Speaking & Writing

Discussion

There are no right answers here — use these to practice speaking, or write a short answer to each.

  • Does your country have a holiday where people wear a particular color, or a small custom like the pinching one? Describe it.
  • Erica's friend pinched her even though they are best friends. Is that kind of joke normal among friends where you live?
  • Tell the story again in your own words, in five or six sentences, using the simple past.
  • Write about 70 words about a day when you forgot something important. What happened?
