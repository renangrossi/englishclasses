Listening

For each one, try listening once for the overall argument, then again for specific detail and register — and shadow a short section out loud to work on rhythm and stress.

Listen for stance, not just content. At C1, the goal is to notice not only what a speaker says, but how they signal certainty, doubt, criticism or humor — tone of voice and word choice carry as much meaning as the words themselves.

C1

Speaking

Five tasks for nuanced, extended speech: defending a position under pressure, speculating about causes and outcomes, and handling professional or academic scenarios. Aim for precision as much as fluency — at C1, the grammar you choose should reflect exactly how certain, tentative or critical you actually are.

1. Defending a Position Under Challenge

State a position, then imagine a skeptical listener pushing back. Respond to each challenge out loud, conceding minor points where reasonable but holding your overall line.

  • “Remote work is, on balance, better for society than office work.” Defend or reject this, and respond to the objection: “But doesn't it damage collaboration and mentorship for younger employees?”
  • “Standardized testing is an unreliable measure of ability.” Defend or reject this, and respond to: “Isn't some objective measure better than none at all?”

2. Speculative Language: Causes and Outcomes

Speak for at least a minute, speculating about causes or future outcomes, using a range of modal and hedging structures (may well, is likely to, it stands to reason that, presumably, arguably).

  • Why might a highly qualified employee choose to leave a well-paid, stable job?
  • How is artificial intelligence likely to reshape which skills are valuable in the next decade?
  • What might explain why some countries recover from economic crises faster than others?

3. Professional Scenario: Giving Difficult Feedback

Practice this dialogue out loud with a partner (A = manager, B = team member whose report had serious errors). Then rewrite and perform your own version for a different professional situation.

SpeakerLine
AI wanted to talk through the quarterly report before it goes out. There are a few things I think need addressing.
BOf course — I did suspect the projections section might need another look.
AThat's exactly it. The figures don't appear to have been reconciled with finance, and as it stands, we'd be presenting numbers that can't be substantiated.
BThat's a fair point. I should have flagged that I was still waiting on their sign-off.
AUnderstood. Let's hold the report until that's confirmed — better a short delay than something we'd have to retract.

4. Academic Scenario: Presenting and Defending Research

Deliver a two-to-three-minute summary of a study, article, or piece of research you know (real or invented), then answer two follow-up questions a skeptical audience member might ask.

  • “What was the main limitation of your methodology?”
  • “How would you respond to a study that found the opposite result?”

5. Guided Discussion: Weighing Complex Trade-offs

Discuss each question out loud for two or three minutes, exploring more than one side before reaching a nuanced conclusion — avoid settling too quickly on a simple answer.

  • Is it ever justifiable for a government to restrict free speech, and if so, under what conditions?
  • Should artificial intelligence systems be held to the same ethical standards as human professionals?

Revision

A quick reference grid, the most common C1 mistakes, and two mixed-review exercises pulling together the ten C1 focus areas.

Quick Reference: Key Structures

StructureFormExample
Passive Reportingsubject + is said/believed + to + infinitiveHe is said to have left the country.
Concessive Clauseseven though / whereas / despite + nounEven though it was raining, we went out.
Advanced Conditionalsif it hadn't been for + noun, …If it hadn't been for your help, I would have failed.
Modal Perfect (Nuance)might have / must have / shouldn't have + past participleShe shouldn't have said that.
InversionNever / Not until / Only after + inverted clauseNot until later did I realize my mistake.
Reporting Verb Patternsdeny + gerund; urge sb + to-infinitiveHe denied taking the money.
Causativeshave/get + object + past participleI had my car repaired.
Cleft SentencesWhat + clause + is/was + emphasized ideaWhat bothered me was his tone.

Common Mistakes at C1

AvoidUse InsteadWhy
He is saying to be rich.He is said to be rich.The passive reporting pattern is “is said,” not “is saying.”
Despite it was raining, we went out.Despite the rain, we went out.“Despite” takes a noun phrase, not a clause — use “even though” for a full clause.
If it isn't for your help, I would have failed.If it hadn't been for your help, I would have failed.This fixed expression always takes the past form: “if it hadn't been for.”
He denied to take the money.He denied taking the money.“Deny” is followed by a gerund, not a to-infinitive.
I had my car to repair.I had my car repaired.The causative “have something done” needs the past participle, not a to-infinitive.
