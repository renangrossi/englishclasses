B2

Speaking

Five tasks that push toward longer turns: justifying opinions in depth, hypothesising, shifting register, giving a short presentation, and negotiating disagreement. Speak for longer than feels comfortable — B2 speaking is as much about sustaining an idea as it is about grammar.

1. Justifying an Opinion (Long Turn)

Speak for at least one minute without stopping. State your opinion, give two reasons, one example, and briefly acknowledge the opposite view before restating your position.

  • Should governments limit how much screen time children have?
  • Is it ethical to eat meat in the 21st century?
  • Should companies allow employees to work from anywhere in the world?

2. Hypothesising

Speak about each hypothetical situation using second and third conditionals (“If I were…, I would…”, “If I had…, I would have…”).

  • If you could live in any country, where would you live, and why?
  • If you had studied a completely different subject, what do you think your life would look like now?
  • What would you do if you found a wallet full of cash on the street?

3. Formal vs. Informal Register

Say the same message twice out loud: once informally (to a friend) and once formally (to a boss or a stranger). Notice how the vocabulary and grammar change, not just the tone.

  • Telling someone you can't come to an event.
  • Asking someone for a favor.
  • Complaining about a delayed delivery.

4. Short Presentation

Prepare and deliver a two-minute presentation on a topic you know well. Structure it clearly: introduction, two or three main points, and a conclusion.

  • A place you have visited that you would recommend.
  • A skill or hobby you are good at, and how someone could start learning it.
  • A change you would make to your city or workplace, and why.

5. Role-Play: Disagreeing Politely

Practice this dialogue out loud with a partner (A and B disagree about a work decision). Then invent your own disagreement and negotiate a compromise.

SpeakerLine
AI really think we should launch the product next month. Waiting any longer could hurt us.
BI see your point, but I'm not sure we're ready. Have we considered the risk of launching with unfinished testing?
AThat's fair, though I'd argue the market window matters more right now.
BMaybe there's a middle ground — what if we launched a limited version next month, and the full version later?
AActually, that could work. Let's propose that to the team.

Revision

A quick reference grid, the most common B2 mistakes, and two mixed-review exercises pulling together the core B2 grammar range.

Quick Reference: Key Structures

StructureFormExample
Mixed ConditionalsIf + past perfect, would + base (or reverse)If she had left earlier, she would be here now.
Modal Perfectmodal + have + past participleYou should have called me yesterday.
Reported Speechbackshift tenses; if/whether for yes-no questionsShe said (that) she was tired.
Future Perfectwill have + past participleBy June, I will have graduated.
Cleft SentencesWhat/It + clause + is/was + emphasized ideaWhat I need is a break.
Inversionnegative adverbial + auxiliary + subjectNever have I seen such a mess.
Wish / If Onlywish + past simple (present); wish + past perfect (past)I wish I were taller. I wish I had studied.
Advanced Passiveget-passive; modal + be + past participleMy phone got stolen. The work should be finished.

Common Mistakes at B2

AvoidUse InsteadWhy
If she left earlier, she would be here now.If she had left earlier, she would be here now.A past condition needs the past perfect, even with a present result.
You should call me yesterday.You should have called me yesterday.Criticism about the past needs modal perfect: should have + past participle.
She said she is tired.She said she was tired.Reported speech backshifts the present to the past.
By June, I will graduate.By June, I will have graduated.“By” + a future time point signals the future perfect, not the simple future.
Never I have seen this.Never have I seen this.A fronted negative adverbial triggers inversion: auxiliary before the subject.
