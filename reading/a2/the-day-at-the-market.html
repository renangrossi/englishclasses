A Saturday at the Market

A weekly routine at the farmers market — and why it is about more than the shopping.

Listen & ReadVocabularyPracticeDiscussion

Listen & Read

The Text

Play the audio and follow along, then read it again on your own. 2:02

Every Saturday, I go to the farmers market with my friend Grace. It is always busy and full of people. We like to go early in the morning, because it is not too hot yet and the best things are still there.

First, we look at the vegetables. There are so many colors: red tomatoes, green peppers, yellow corn. Grace loves to cook, so she buys a lot of these. She always says that good food starts with good ingredients.

Next, we go to the fruit stands. There are apples, bananas, peaches and strawberries. I love strawberries, so I always buy a small box. They are sweet and fresh, and they are perfect for breakfast.

After the fruit and vegetables, we walk to the meat and fish counter. There is chicken, beef and fish. Fish is my favorite. It is good for you, and I like to grill it for dinner.

We also stop at the bread stand. Fresh bread smells wonderful. We buy a loaf to eat with butter and jam. It is simple, but it is delicious.

The market is not only about food. There are clothes too, and sometimes there is live music. It makes the shopping fun. We see people we know, we say hello, and we talk for a few minutes. It feels like a community.

Before we leave, we get a cup of coffee. It is nice to sit down after walking around for two hours. We talk about our week, our plans, and what we are going to cook with everything we bought.

Going to the market is more than shopping. It is about enjoying the morning, seeing friends and trying something new. It is one of my favorite parts of the week.

Adapted from the original course material.

Useful Language

Vocabulary

  • a stand / a stall — a small open shop at a market
  • fresh — recently picked or made; not old
  • ingredients — the different foods you use to cook a dish
  • a loaf (of bread) — one whole piece of bread, before you cut it
  • to grill — to cook over direct heat, often outside
  • a counter — the long flat surface in a store where you are served
  • a community — the people who live in one place and know each other
  • to stop by / stop at — to visit a place for a short time

Speaking & Writing

Discussion

There are no right answers here — use these to practice speaking, or write a short answer to each.

  • Is there a market near you? What can you buy there, and how often do you go?
  • Do you prefer a market or a supermarket? Give two reasons.
  • Describe your own Saturday morning routine, using 'always', 'usually' and 'sometimes'.
  • Write a short paragraph (about 60 words) about a place near your home where people meet and talk.
