Everyday Service English

Three small situations: when the dish is unavailable, when you need another color, and when you need a stranger's help.

Three short conversations you will need sooner or later. Your lines are written out as models.

1. When what you wanted is not available

Waiter: Are you ready to order, ma'am?

You: Yes, I think so. I'd like the tomato soup to start, and my friend will have the beef stew.

Waiter: One beef stew, one tomato soup. And for the main course?

You: I'll have the trout, please.

Waiter: I'm sorry, we're out of the trout this evening.

You: Oh, that's too bad. What else would you recommend?

Waiter: The sole is very good tonight.

You: Then I'll have the sole. Do you have any coleslaw?

Waiter: No, I'm sorry, we don't.

You: That's fine — could I get a small mixed salad instead?

Waiter: Certainly. Would you like something to drink?

You: Just a glass of water, please. No ice.

2. Buying clothes

Sales clerk: May I help you, ma'am?

You: Yes, please. I'm looking for a cotton shirt, size medium.

Sales clerk: Any particular color?

You: Something light, if you have it.

Sales clerk: How about this one?

You: I like the style, but not the color. Do you have it in anything else?

Sales clerk: They come in white, pale yellow, aqua, red and green. Will that do?

You: A white one would be perfect. Could I try it on?

Sales clerk: Of course. The fitting rooms are just behind you.

You: Thank you. And if it doesn't fit, can I exchange it?

Sales clerk: Within thirty days, with the receipt.

3. Asking a stranger for help

You: Excuse me — would you mind taking a picture of us?

Man: Sure. Which button do I press?

You: Just this big one on the right. It focuses by itself.

Man: Do I need to hold it steady for long?

You: No, it's quick. Thanks so much.

Man: All right — say cheese!

You: Cheese!

Man: Here you go. I took two, just in case.

You: That's very kind of you. Thank you.

Vocabulary

  • to be out of something — to have none left (American English; British English often says "it's off")
  • the main course — the biggest, central dish of a meal
  • instead — in place of the other thing
  • to try something on — to put on clothing in a store to see if it fits
  • a fitting room — the small room where you try on clothes
  • to exchange — to return something and take another in its place
  • Would you mind…? — a very polite request, followed by -ing: Would you mind taking…?
  • Will that do? — Is that acceptable / good enough?
  • just in case — in order to be safe if something goes wrong

Speaking & Writing

Discussion

There are no right answers here — use these to practice speaking, or write a short answer to each.

  • Role-play situation 1: one of you is the waiter and must say that two things on the menu are unavailable.
  • What do you say in your own language when a shop does not have what you want? Is it as indirect as English?
  • Ask a partner three polite requests using "Would you mind…?"
  • Write a short dialogue (about 8 lines) in a clothes store where the first item is the wrong size.
