A1

Speaking

Four guided speaking tasks. Say your answers out loud — alone, with a partner, or with your instructor — then use the notes box to write down the key words you used, just to keep a record of your practice.

1. Introduce Yourself

Stand up (or sit up straight!) and say your answers out loud, one sentence at a time. Don't worry about mistakes — the goal is to speak without stopping.

  • What is your name?
  • How old are you?
  • Where are you from?
  • Where do you live now?
  • What is your job? (or: What do you study?)

2. Everyday Questions

Work with a partner if you can. Take turns asking and answering these questions out loud. If you are alone, ask yourself the question, then answer it.

  • Do you have any brothers or sisters?
  • What time do you wake up?
  • What is your favorite food?
  • Is there a park near your house?
  • What color is your bedroom?

3. Role-Play: Meeting Someone New

Practice this short conversation out loud. If you have a partner, take turns being A and B. Then try it again, changing the name, job and city.

SpeakerLine
AHi! My name is Marco. What's your name?
BHi, Marco. I'm Sofia. Nice to meet you.
ANice to meet you too. Where are you from, Sofia?
BI'm from Brazil. And you?
AI'm from Italy. What do you do, Sofia?
BI'm a nurse. What about you?
AI'm a student.

4. Describe Your Day

Talk for about one minute, describing a normal day. Use time words like first, then, after that, and in the evening. Try to speak without long pauses.

  • What time do you wake up?
  • What do you do in the morning?
  • What do you do in the afternoon?
  • What do you do in the evening?

Quick Reference: Key Structures

StructureFormExample
To Beam / is / areI am a teacher. She is tired.
Simple Presentbase verb / verb + -sI work from home. She works every day.
Present Continuousam/is/are + verb-ingI am reading right now.
There Is / There Arethere is + singular; there are + pluralThere is a cat. There are two dogs.
Possessive Pronounsmine, yours, his, hers, its, ours, theirsThis book is mine.
Articlesa / an / theI have a cat. The cat is black.
Wh- QuestionsWh-word + do/does + subject + verbWhere do you live?
Imperativesbase verb, no subjectClose the door, please.

Common Mistakes at A1

AvoidUse InsteadWhy
She work every day.She works every day.Third person singular (he/she/it) needs -s in the simple present.
I am liking coffee.I like coffee.“Like” describes a state, not an action, so it stays in the simple present.
There is two books.There are two books.A plural noun needs “there are,” not “there is.”
This is my of book.This book is mine.Possessive pronouns (mine, yours…) stand alone, with no extra “of.”
What is your name is?What is your name?Don't repeat the verb “is” at the end of a wh-question.
