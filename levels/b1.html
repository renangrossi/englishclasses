B1

Speaking

Five speaking tasks that build toward opinions, advice, experiences, light debate and problem-solving. Speak out loud first — alone, with a partner, or with your instructor — then use the notes box to keep a short written record of what you said.

1. Giving Opinions

Say what you think about each topic, and give at least one reason. Use phrases like “In my opinion…”, “I think that…”, “I'm not sure, but…”.

  • Is social media good or bad for teenagers?
  • Should students wear school uniforms?
  • Is it better to live in a big city or a small town?

2. Giving Advice

Read each situation out loud, then give advice using “should,” “shouldn't,” or “If I were you, I would…”.

  • A friend is always tired because they sleep very late. What should they do?
  • A coworker feels stressed about a big project. What advice would you give?
  • A friend wants to learn English faster. What would you suggest?

3. Talking About Experiences

Answer each question out loud using the present perfect (“I have…”/“I haven't…”), then add a detail in the simple past.

  • Have you ever traveled to another country? Where did you go?
  • Have you ever tried a food you didn't like? What was it?
  • Have you ever learned a new skill as an adult? What was it, and how did it go?

4. Simple Debate

Choose one side of the topic (even if it's not your real opinion) and argue for it out loud for about 30–60 seconds. If you have a partner, have them argue the other side.

  • Working from home vs. working in an office — which is better?
  • Should university education be free?
  • Is it better to have a strict daily routine or a flexible one?

5. Role-Play: Solving a Problem

Practice this dialogue out loud with a partner (A = customer, B = customer service). Then invent your own problem and solve it the same way.

SpeakerLine
AHi, I booked a hotel room online, but when I arrived, there was no reservation under my name.
BI'm really sorry to hear that. Can you show me your confirmation email?
ASure, here it is. I paid for two nights already.
BI see the payment. It looks like there was a system error. Let me give you a room right now, and I'll refund the booking fee.
AThank you, I appreciate that.
BAgain, I'm sorry for the trouble. Enjoy your stay.

Revision

A quick reference grid, the most common B1 mistakes, and two mixed-review exercises pulling together everything from this level.

Quick Reference: Key Structures

StructureFormExample
Present Perfecthave/has + past participleI have visited Paris twice.
Present Perfect Continuoushave/has been + verb-ingI have been studying for two hours.
Past Perfecthad + past participleShe had left before I arrived.
Future Perfectwill have + past participleBy 8pm, we will have finished.
Second ConditionalIf + past simple, would + base verbIf I had more time, I would travel.
Third ConditionalIf + past perfect, would have + past participleIf I had known, I would have come.
Passive Voicebe + past participleThe cake was made by her.
Must / Might / Shouldmodal + base verb (no “to”)You must finish this today.
Future Formswill + base verb; be going to + base verbI will call you. I am going to travel.

Common Mistakes at B1

AvoidUse InsteadWhy
I have seen him yesterday.I saw him yesterday.A finished, specific past time (“yesterday”) needs the simple past, not the present perfect.
If I have more time, I would travel.If I had more time, I would travel.The if-clause of a second conditional needs the past simple, not the present.
The cake was make by her.The cake was made by her.The passive voice needs the past participle (made), not the base verb.
You must to finish this today.You must finish this today.Modal verbs like “must” are followed directly by the base verb, with no “to.”
I am going travel next week.I am going to travel next week.“Going to” needs “to” before the base verb.
