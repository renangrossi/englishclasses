TOPIC: Advanced Passive Structures

1. BRIEF EXPLANATION

Beyond the basic "be + past participle" passive, C1 English uses three advanced passive patterns:

a) Passive reporting structures — used to report general beliefs, rumors or claims without naming the source.
   - It + is/was + reporting verb (said, believed, thought, reported, understood, rumored, alleged) + that-clause
     e.g. It is believed that the fire started in the kitchen.
   - Subject + is/was + reporting verb + to-infinitive (simple infinitive for the same time; perfect infinitive "to have + past participle" for an earlier time)
     e.g. He is believed to be abroad. / He is believed to have left the country.

b) Passive with modals — modal + be + past participle for present/future obligation or possibility; modal + have been + past participle (modal perfect passive) for something that should/might/must have happened (or not) in the past.
   e.g. The report must be submitted by Friday.
   e.g. The deadline should have been extended.

c) Get-passive — get + past participle, mainly in informal/spoken registers. It often adds a sense of something happening suddenly, unexpectedly, or with some difficulty/responsibility involved, and it can sound more dynamic than the neutral be-passive.
   e.g. Our flight got cancelled at the last minute.
   Compare: The flight was cancelled. (neutral, factual) vs. The flight got cancelled. (something happened to us, more personal/dramatic)

Use the reporting passive to distance yourself from a claim (useful in journalism and academic writing), the modal passive to talk about obligations/expectations, and the get-passive for informal, event-focused narration.

2. EXAMPLES

1. He is said to have worked as a spy during the war.
2. It is thought that the vase dates back to the Ming dynasty.
3. She is rumored to be leaving the company next month.
4. The missing files are believed to have been destroyed.
5. All applications must be reviewed before Friday.
6. The bridge should have been repaired years ago.
7. My laptop got stolen on the train yesterday.
8. He got promoted after just one year in the role.
9. It is widely reported that the negotiations collapsed.
10. The contract is understood to have been signed under pressure.
11. Mistakes like this shouldn't be tolerated in a hospital.
12. Their car got towed while they were at the concert.

3. COMMON MISTAKES

1. Incorrect: He is said writing the letter himself.
   Correct: He is said to have written the letter himself.
   Why: The reporting passive needs a to-infinitive; a completed past action needs the perfect infinitive "to have + past participle."

2. Incorrect: The task should finished by now.
   Correct: The task should have been finished by now.
   Why: The modal perfect passive requires "have been," not just "been" or the bare verb.

3. Incorrect: The window got broke during the storm.
   Correct: The window got broken during the storm.
   Why: Get-passive still needs the past participle (broken), not the past simple form (broke).

4. Incorrect: It is said he was rich, that he owned three houses.
   Correct: It is said that he was rich and owned three houses.
   Why: Don't mix the that-clause pattern with a stray comma splice; join ideas with "and" or restructure into one clean that-clause.

5. Incorrect: The report is said to written by an intern.
   Correct: The report is said to have been written by an intern.
   Why: Missing "have been" — needed for a passive action that happened before now.

C. Error Correction
1. The building is thought to be demolished next year. → ...to be demolished... (needs "be" for the passive infinitive)
2. He is said to have escaped through the roof. (needs "to have," not "having")
3. The proposal should be approved before the deadline. (needs "be approved")
4. My phone got stolen on the bus this morning. (past participle "stolen," not "steal")
5. It is believed that the treaty will be signed soon. (needs "believed," not "believe")
6. The files must have been deleted by mistake. (needs "have been," not "has been," after a modal)
