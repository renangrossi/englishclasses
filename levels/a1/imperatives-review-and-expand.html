Imperatives (Review and Expand)

A mixed review that puts your A1 grammar to work in one real-time scene.

ObjectivesExplanationRulesExamplesCommon MistakesPracticeSummaryTest YourselfRelated

Introduction

This review lesson combines the grammar you've learned so far — present simple, present continuous, and imperatives — inside one real-time scene, the way real English actually mixes them.

By the end of this lesson you can…

  • Choose the correct form based on context clues in a scene
  • Recognize imperatives inside natural dialogue
  • Review present simple and present continuous together, and know when to use each

Explanation

Explanation

Real English rarely uses just one form at a time. A real-time scene — describing what's happening right now — might use the present simple for routines and facts about the people in it, the present continuous for something happening at this exact moment, and imperatives inside quoted dialogue (“Hurry up, the taxi is waiting!” Emily calls to James).

This lesson is a chance to practice recognizing which form fits a given context, rather than learning a brand new rule.

Grammar Rules

The Rules

  • Present simple → routines, habits and facts about the people in the scene (wakes up, takes)
  • Present continuous → something happening at this exact moment (is packing, is waiting)
  • Present continuous → a plan already arranged for very soon (are traveling this afternoon)
  • Imperatives → direct instructions inside quoted speech (“Hurry up.”)

Examples

See It in Action

  • Every day, Emily wakes up early and goes for a run — but not today.
  • “Hurry up! The taxi is waiting outside,” she calls to James.
  • They are traveling to London this afternoon.
  • “I'm coming, I'm coming!” he shouts back.
  • “Don't forget your passport,” Emily reminds him.

Common Mistakes

Watch Out For

AvoidEvery day, Emily wake up early.

Use insteadEvery day, Emily wakes up early.

Third person singular (Emily/she) needs -s in the simple present: wakes.

AvoidHurry up (as a statement to yourself)

Use insteadHurry up!

Imperatives use the base verb with no subject — this is already correct as a command.

AvoidLook, the taxi waits outside!

Use insteadLook, the taxi is waiting outside!

“Look” signals something happening right now, not the simple present.

Tip

When you read a mixed scene, underline the time clues (“every day,” “right now,” “this afternoon”) — they usually tell you which form to use.

Summary

Review

  • Real scenes naturally mix present simple and present continuous based on context.
  • Time expressions are strong clues for which form fits: “every day/usually” → simple, “right now/at this moment” → continuous.
  • Imperatives appear inside dialogue as direct instructions.
