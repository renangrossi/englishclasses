How to Brew Good Coffee

A step-by-step guide — and a useful model for giving instructions in English.

Listen & Read

The Text

Play the audio and follow along, then read it again on your own. Hover over a highlighted word to see what it means. 2:06

Brewing coffee means getting the flavor and the smell out of ground coffee beans by adding hot water. It sounds simple, and it is — but a few small choices make the difference between a good cup and a disappointing one. Here are the steps.

First, grind the beans. Use fresh beans if you can, and grind them just before you brew. How fine you grind them depends on your method: a French press needs a coarse grind, while an espresso machine needs a very fine one.

Next, heat the water. The best temperature is between 195 and 205 degrees Fahrenheit, which is just below boiling. If the water is boiling hot, it burns the coffee and the taste turns bitter.

Then prepare the filter. If you are using a paper filter, put it in the basket and pour a little hot water through it. This washes out the taste of the paper and warms up the pot at the same time.

Now add the coffee. A good rule of thumb is one to two tablespoons of ground coffee for every six ounces of water. Use more if you like it strong, less if you like it mild.

Pour the water slowly over the grounds, moving in a circle so that all the coffee gets wet. Stop after a few seconds and wait about half a minute. The coffee will rise and bubble a little — this is called the bloom — and then you can add the rest of the water.

How long you wait depends on the method. A French press takes four to five minutes. Drip coffee takes two to four. An espresso machine needs only twenty to thirty seconds.

Finally, take out the filter, throw away the grounds, and pour the coffee into a mug. Drink it while it is hot.

The taste of your coffee depends on the beans, the grind, the water temperature and the time. Change one thing at a time, and you will soon find the cup you like best.

Vocabulary

  • to grind — to break something into very small pieces or powder
  • coarse / fine — in large pieces / in very small pieces
  • to brew — to make coffee or tea with hot water
  • bitter — a sharp, unpleasant taste, like very strong coffee
  • mild — not strong
  • the grounds — the wet coffee that is left after brewing
  • a rule of thumb — a simple, approximate rule that usually works
  • a tablespoon — a large spoon used for measuring in cooking
