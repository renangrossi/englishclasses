Yes/No Questions

Ask questions that can be answered with a simple yes or no.

ObjectivesExplanationRulesExamplesCommon MistakesPracticeSummaryRelated

Introduction

Yes/No questions are answered with a simple yes or no, and the way you form them depends on which verb is in the sentence: to be, do/does, or a modal like can.

By the end of this lesson you can…

  • Form yes/no questions with “to be”
  • Form yes/no questions with “do/does”
  • Give short answers correctly

Explanation

Explanation

With to be, simply move the verb before the subject: Is he ready? With can, have, has and other auxiliaries, do the same: Can you swim?

With ordinary verbs (like, work, go...), English needs the helper verb do or does at the start of the question: Do you like coffee? Does she work here?

Grammar Rules

The Rules

  • Be (am/is/are) → Am I...? / Is he...? / Are they...?
  • Do/Does → Do you...? / Does she...?
  • Can / Have / Has → Can you...? / Have you...? / Has she...?
  • Short answers: Yes, I am. / No, she doesn't. / Yes, they can.

Examples

See It in Action

  • Are you a student? Yes, I am.
  • Does he speak English? No, he doesn't.
  • Do you have a car? Yes, I do.
  • Can she swim? Yes, she can.
  • Is it raining? No, it isn't.

Common Mistakes

Watch Out For

AvoidDo she like coffee?

Use insteadDoes she like coffee?

Third person singular (she) needs “does,” not “do.”

AvoidDoes you have a pen?

Use insteadDo you have a pen?

“You” needs “do,” not “does.”

AvoidIs you ready?

Use insteadAre you ready?

“You” needs “are,” not “is.”

Tip

Once you identify the main verb type (be, ordinary verb, or modal), the question word almost writes itself.

Review
