B2

Future-in-the-Past

Talk about something that was going to happen, seen from a point in the past.

ObjectivesRulesExamplesCommon MistakesPracticeSummary

Introduction

You already know "will" and "going to" for talking about the future from now. But when you're telling a story or describing a past situation, you often need to talk about a future that existed back then — a plan, an expectation, or something about to happen at that past moment. This is called future-in-the-past.

By the end of this lesson you can…

  • Use was/were going to for a past plan (often one that changed or didn't happen)
  • Use would as a narrative future, for what happened later in a story
  • Use was/were about to for something on the very brink of happening
  • Tell the difference between the three, based on the nuance each one adds

Grammar Rules

The Rules

a) was/were going to + bare infinitive

A plan or intention that existed at a point in the past — very often, one that changed or never happened.

  • She was going to call me, but she forgot.
  • We were going to have a picnic, but it started raining.

b) would + bare infinitive

Used in narration for something that turned out to happen later — usually without any sense of the plan failing; more like fate or how the story unfolded.

  • He didn't know it yet, but this decision would change his life.
  • Little did she know that the stranger would become her best friend.

c) was/were about to + bare infinitive

Something on the very brink of happening — often interrupted by something else.

  • I was about to leave when the phone rang.
  • They were about to sign the contract when the deal fell through.

Examples

See It in Action

  • He was going to study abroad, but the scholarship fell through.
  • She had no idea that this decision would change everything.
  • I was about to knock when the door opened.
  • We were going to sell the house, but we changed our minds.
  • He promised he would call as soon as he landed.
  • Just as I was about to give up, I found the missing file.
  • They were going to cancel the trip because of the weather.
  • She didn't know that this stranger would become her closest friend.

Common Mistakes

Watch Out For

AvoidShe was going to called me later.

Use insteadShe was going to call me later.

"Was going to" is always followed by the bare infinitive, not the past form.

AvoidHe didn't know that this decision will change everything.

Use insteadHe didn't know that this decision would change everything.

Inside a past narrative, future-in-the-past needs "would," not "will."

AvoidI was about to leaving when she called.

Use insteadI was about to leave when she called.

"Was about to" takes the bare infinitive, not the gerund.

AvoidThey was going to travel next month.

Use insteadThey were going to travel next month.

Subject-verb agreement — "they" needs "were," not "was."

Summary

Review

  • Was/were going to = a past plan, often one that changed or didn't happen.
  • Would = a narrative future, for what turned out to happen later, without implying failure.
  • Was/were about to = something on the very brink of happening, often interrupted.
  • This is the foundation for C1's Future Forms with Nuance, which adds the more formal was/were to and complex narrative timelines.
