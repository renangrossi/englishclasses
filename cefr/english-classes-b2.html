Past Perfect Continuous

A continuous action in the past of the past.

60

Affirmative

Iyouhe, she, itweyouthey
+HAD+BEEN+-ING
  • The team had been searching for hours before they found him.
  • She was exhausted — she had been working nights.
  • Their eyes were red. They had been crying.

Negative

had not (hadn’t) + been + -ing
  • If I hadn’t been practicing, I wouldn’t have won.
  • He failed because he had not been revising.

Interrogative

had + subject + been + -ing?
WHO · WHY
WHERE · WHAT
HOW · WHEN
HOW MANY (…)
HOW MUCH (…)
WHAT TIME
›
HAD
Iyouhe, she, itweyouthey
BEEN+-ING ?
  • Had you been waiting long when the bus came?
  • How long had they been talking before she left?
Result or duration? She had finished the report (done) · She had been working on it for hours (the activity). The same logic as the present perfect — one step further into the past.

Future Continuous

What will be in progress at a specific moment in the future.

61

Affirmative

will be + -ing
Iyouhe, she, itweyouthey
+WILL+BE+-ING

In progress at a future time

  • This time tomorrow, I’ll be lying on a beach.
  • At 9pm we’ll be watching the final.
  • Don’t call at noon — she’ll be driving.
  • Next year they’ll be living in Berlin.

Interrogative

polite questions about plans
  • Will you be using the car tonight?
  • Will she be joining us for dinner?
  • Will you be staying long?

Softer than “Will you use…?” — it asks about plans, not favors.

Negative

won’t be + -ing
  • I won’t be working on Friday — it’s a holiday.
  • They won’t be coming to the wedding.

Future Perfect

An action that will already be finished at a point in the future.

62

Affirmative

Iyouhe, she, itweyouthey
+WILL/ … GOING TO+HAVE+PARTICIPLEregular · irregular
  • By Friday, I will have finished the project.
  • The guests will have left by midnight.
  • We’re going to have saved enough by June.
  • Chef Rosa will have prepared a wonderful dinner.

Negative

won’t have · not going to have
  • He won’t have bought anything — he’s broke.
  • They won’t have arrived by the end of the day.
  • If we don’t hurry, we won’t have done anything useful!

Interrogative

will + subject + have + participle?
WHO · WHY
WHERE · WHAT
HOW · WHEN
HOW MANY (…)
HOW MUCH (…)
WHAT TIME
›
WILL
Iyouhe, she, itweyouthey
HAVE+PARTICIPLE ?
  • When will the movie have ended?
  • How many km will you have run by Sunday?

Future continuous or future perfect?

At noon, I’ll be traveling.still happening at that moment
By noon, I’ll have arrived.already done at that moment

“By” / “by the time” are strong signals for the future perfect.

Third & Mixed Conditionals

The past that didn’t happen — and conditions and results from different times.

64

Third conditional — the past that didn’t happen

IF+past perfect,would have + participle
  • If I had studied, I would have passed. I didn’t study.
  • If she had left earlier, she wouldn’t have missed the train.
  • They would have helped if they had known.

Past condition → present result

IF+past perfect,would + verbnow
  • If I had taken that job, I would be living in Tokyo now.
  • If he hadn’t missed the flight, he would be here with us.
  • If we had saved more, we wouldn’t be worried now.

Present condition → past result

IF+past simple,would have + participle
  • If she weren’t so shy, she would have spoken at the meeting.
  • If I were more organized, I wouldn’t have forgotten your birthday.
  • If you spoke Italian, you would have understood the joke.

The “if” part is a permanent trait — true then and now.

Look for time clues: “now” signals a present result; a permanent trait in the if-clause signals a present condition.

Wish · If Only

Unreal wishes about the present — and regrets about the past.

65

Present wish

wish / if only + past simple
  • I wish I had more free time.
  • If only I spoke Japanese.
  • She wishes she could sing.

Reality is different now.

Past regret

wish / if only + past perfect
  • I wish I had studied harder.
  • If only I hadn’t said that.
  • He wishes he had taken the job.

It happened — and I regret it.

Complaint

wish + someone + would
  • I wish you would stop interrupting.
  • I wish it would stop raining!
  • She wishes he would call more.

Annoyance — not for yourself.

Wish or hope?

hope + present / willa real possibilityI hope you feel better soon.
wish + pastunreal · imaginaryI wish you felt better. but you don’t
I wish I were… is the standard form for every subject: I wish I were taller.

Advanced Passive

Passives with modals, the get-passive, and passive infinitives and gerunds.

66

Modal + be + participle

must · can · should · might+be+participle
  • The form must be signed by Friday.
  • This problem can be solved in minutes.
  • Seatbelts should be worn at all times.

Get-passive · informal

get+participle
  • My bag got stolen at the station.
  • She got promoted last month.
  • Our flight got cancelled.

Common for unplanned or personal events.

Passive infinitive & gerund

to be + participleThe walls need to be painted.
being + participleI hate being interrupted.
He expects to be paid today.
She enjoys being photographed.

Reported Questions & Commands

What someone asked — and what someone told you to do.

67

Yes / no questions → if / whether + normal word order

“Do you like jazz?”→She asked if I liked jazz.
“Are you coming?”→He asked whether I was coming.
“Can you drive?”→They asked if I could drive.

Wh- questions → keep the question word + normal word order

“Where do you work?”→She asked where I worked.
“What time is it?”→He asked what time it was.
“Why did you leave?”→They asked why I had left.

Commands & requests → tell / ask + object + (not) to + verb

“Close the door.”→She told me to close the door.
“Please don’t be late.”→He asked me not to be late.
“Call me later.”→She asked him to call her later.

Modal Perfect

Should have, must have, might have — regret, certainty and guesses about the past.

68
should · must · might · could+HAVE+participle

SHOULD HAVE

regret · criticism
  • You should have told me earlier.
  • I shouldn’t have eaten that last slice.
  • We should have booked a table.

MUST HAVE

confident deduction
  • He must have left — his car is gone.
  • You must have been exhausted.
  • She must have forgotten our meeting.

MIGHT / COULD HAVE

uncertain guess
  • She might have missed the bus.
  • They could have gotten lost.
  • I might have left it at work.

Now or then?

About nowAbout the past
He must be tired.He must have been tired.
She might be at home.She might have been at home.
You should call her.You should have called her.
Always modal + have + participle — never has, never the past form: should have gone, not should have went.

Cleft Sentences

Split a sentence to put the key information in the spotlight.

70

It is / was … that / who

Emphasize a person, thing, time or place

Mia broke the vase.→It was Mia who broke the vase. · who
I met him in Madrid.→It was in Madrid that I met him. · where
The price worries me.→It’s the price that worries me. · what
We left on Friday.→It was on Friday that we left. · when

What … is / was

Emphasize an action, need or idea

I need a holiday.→What I need is a holiday.
His tone annoyed me.→What annoyed me was his tone.
She wants to rest.→What she wants is to rest.

Clefts in conversation — correcting and answering

  • “Did Tom call?” — “No, it was Nina who called.”
  • “You forgot the keys!” — “It was you who had them!”
  • What really matters is that nobody got hurt.

Inversion for Emphasis

Put a negative adverb first — and flip the subject and helper verb.

71
Never · Rarely · Seldom · Only after+auxiliary+subject+verb

Normal → inverted

I have never seen such a crowd.→Never have I seen such a crowd.
She rarely complains.→Rarely does she complain.
We seldom get a chance like this.→Seldom do we get a chance like this.
He had never felt so proud.→Never had he felt so proud.
She explained only after the meeting.→Only after the meeting did she explain.
I believed him only when I saw the photo.→Only when I saw the photo did I believe him.
No helper verb in the original? Add do / does / did. This is formal, dramatic style — not everyday conversation.

Reduced Relative Clauses

Drop the pronoun — keep a participle.

72

Active → -ing

drop who / which / that + the verb

The man who is standing there is my uncle.→The man standing there is my uncle.
People who live in cities complain about noise.→People living in cities complain about noise.
The woman who is sitting next to me is a pilot.→The woman sitting next to me is a pilot.

Passive → past participle

drop who / which / that + was / were

The book which was written by Tolkien…→The book written by Tolkien…
The car that was damaged in the storm…→The car damaged in the storm…
Forms that are signed before May are valid.→Forms signed before May are valid.
You can’t reduce when the pronoun is the object: The book that I read can’t become The book reading.

Advanced Connectors

Formal, written-style links for contrast, addition and consequence.

73

Contrast

whereasjoins two clausesThe north is dry, whereas the south is humid.
despite · in spite of+ noun / -ingDespite the rain, the match went on.
In spite of losing, they celebrated.
neverthelessstarts a new sentenceIt was risky. Nevertheless, they tried.

Addition

furthermoreThe app is slow. Furthermore, it crashes.
moreoverIt’s cheap. Moreover, it’s eco-friendly.

Consequence

consequentlySales fell. Consequently, costs were cut.
as a resultIt snowed. As a result, schools closed.
despite / in spite of never take a full clause directly — add the fact that: Despite the fact that it was raining…

Paired Expressions

Two-part connectors for options, additions and opinions.

74

Singular · options

EITHER … OR …

  • You can have either the soup or the salad.
  • She’s deciding between either moving abroad or buying a house.

NEITHER … NOR … — negative

  • Neither this nor that option works for me.
  • I drink neither coffee nor tea.

Plural · addition

BOTH … AND …

  • Both you and I know the answer.
  • Both the cat and the dog want to go outside.

NOT ONLY … BUT ALSO …

  • Not only was she talented, but she was also kind.
  • I’d like not only the bread but also the cheese.

Opinion

RATHER … THAN …

  • I’d rather walk than wait for the bus.
  • She’d rather cook than order food.

Option · it doesn’t matter which

WHETHER … OR …

  • I’ll go, whether it rains or not.
  • Music is good, whether at home or in the car.
Verb agreement: both … and takes a plural verb (Both are…). With either … or / neither … nor, the verb agrees with the nearer noun: Neither the kids nor the teacher is ready.

Articles in General

When English uses no article at all.

75

Generic → no article · specific → the

In generalOne specific case
Coffee is popular in Brazil.The coffee on the table is cold.
Dogs are loyal animals.The dogs next door bark all night.
Water is essential for life.The water in this glass is warm.
Children learn fast.The children in my class are noisy.

Abstract nouns

  • Life is short.
  • Music helps me relax.
  • Honesty is the best policy.
  • Money can’t buy happiness.

Fixed expressions

mealshave breakfast / lunch / dinner
transportby car · by train · on foot
placesgo to school / work / bed · at home
Portuguese speakers: O café é popular uses an article, but English does not: Coffee is popular.

Future in the Past

Seen from a point in the past: plans, fate — and near misses.

76

WAS / WERE GOING TO

a past plan · often it didn’t happen
  • She was going to call, but she forgot.
  • We were going to sell the house.
  • I was going to say the same thing!

WOULD

what turned out to happen · narration
  • He didn’t know this trip would change his life.
  • She promised she would write.
  • Years later, they would meet again.

WAS / WERE ABOUT TO

on the very brink · often interrupted
  • I was about to leave when the phone rang.
  • They were about to sign when the deal collapsed.
  • Just as I was about to give up, I found it.

Seen from now → seen from the past

I’m going to call her.I was going to call her.
This will change everything.I knew it would change everything.
We’re about to leave.We were about to leave.

Base form after all three

was going towouldwas about to+base verb
