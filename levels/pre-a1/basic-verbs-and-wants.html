PRE-A1

I Am / I Have / I Like / I Want

The four sentence patterns that let you say the most, with the least grammar.

ObjectivesRulesExamplesCommon MistakesPracticeSummary

Introduction

You don't need to know all of English grammar to start communicating. These four simple patterns, used correctly, let you say a huge number of useful things right away.

By the end of this lesson you can…

  • Use "I am" to describe yourself
  • Use "I have" to talk about what you own or your family
  • Use "I like" to talk about preferences
  • Use "I want" and "I need" to ask for things

Grammar Rules

The Rules

I am + adjective/noun

  • I am happy. / I am tired. / I am a student.

I have + noun

  • I have a car. / I have two children. / I have a question.

I like + noun

  • I like coffee. / I like music. / I like this song.

I want / I need + noun

  • I want a coffee, please. / I need help. / I need more time.

Examples

See It in Action

  • I am from Brazil.
  • I have a big family.
  • I like pizza.
  • I want a glass of water, please.
  • I need your help.
  • I am a little tired today.

Common Mistakes

Watch Out For

AvoidI have happy.

Use insteadI am happy.

For feelings and descriptions, use "I am," not "I have."

AvoidI like coffee very much because I want it.

Use insteadI like coffee very much.

"Like" already expresses that you enjoy something — "want" is for asking for something specific, not needed here.

AvoidI am 20 years.

Use insteadI am 20 years old.

Age needs "years old," not just "years."
